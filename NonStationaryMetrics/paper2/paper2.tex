\title{Brachistochrones in conformal Kerr spacetimes:
control domain, exterior separatrices, and adiabatic response}

\author{Iman Rosignoli}
\address{Department of Physics, University of Pavia, Via A.\ Bassi 6, 27100 Pavia, Italy}
\ead{iman.rosignoli01@universitadipavia.it}

\begin{abstract}
We develop the relativistic controlled-rail brachistochrone---the time-optimal constrained
worldline reaching a fixed spatial target at a free arrival clock---in the rotating,
axisymmetric Thakurta--Kerr spacetime, the conformal Kerr geometry $g=A(\eta)^2 g_{\rm
Kerr}$. The controlled-rail principle, its existence/normality and non-autonomous
Hamilton--Jacobi verification, and the Killing, conformal-Killing and Kodama selector
hierarchy are established in the companion paper (with the spherically symmetric FLRW and
Vaidya cases); here frame dragging, a conformal stationary limit and genus-two spectral
curves appear. Since $g(\partial_\eta,\partial_\eta)=-A^2(1-2M/r)$, the admissible-velocity
set is compact only for $r>2M$: the compact-control problem \emph{ends} at the stationary
limit, and every curve is labelled an exterior controlled-rail extremal, a limiting
contact, or an analytic continuation carrying no optimality claim. We derive the
breathing-indicatrix Hamiltonians and the conformal-time ($\eta$)
brachistochrone---the arrival branch $t\equiv\eta$, distinct from proper time $\tau$;
closed-form equatorial separatrices in Weierstrass functions; a complete local
classification at the stationary limit, in which the two marginal momenta $\pm J_c$ prove
\emph{dynamically inequivalent} although the shape radical is even in $J$---the retrograde
one has a finite limiting tangent and reaches $r=2M$ only asymptotically, the prograde one
crosses at a finite rate; the exterior retrograde separatrix at $r_d=3.5139M$; the
rotational and conformal plunge inversion, with an existence theorem for the latter under
a same-launch comparison; and the first-order adiabatic response, on-shell and off-shell,
canonically re-derived from the extended Hamiltonian on compact regular subarcs and
verified against the true non-autonomous flow to $O(\varepsilon^2)$, its weight-two
content a length-two iterated Abelian integral on a genus-two hyperelliptic curve. The
separatrices, the local classification and the on-shell reduction are proved; the
fixed-endpoint no-inversion is established in the stated analytic domain
($r_0\le R_\ast(E,a)$) and machine-certified at representative $r_0$, while its
unrestricted extension remains conjectural; a rigorous higher-genus polylogarithm
framework remains open.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The relativistic brachistochrone problem---to find the time-optimal constrained ``rail''
worldline reaching a fixed spatial target at a free arrival clock---was formulated by
Perlick for stationary
spacetimes \cite{Perlick1991,PerlickRayOptics}, connecting to Fermat's principle and
optical (Randers--Zermelo--Finsler) geometry
\cite{Kovner1990,Randers1941,GibbonsWerner2008,GibbonsRanders2009,BaoRoblesShen}.

\paragraph{What is established, and what is new here.} It is useful to state the division
explicitly. \emph{Established:} the stationary problem itself, together with its reduction
to a Finsler or Jacobi length functional on a spatial slice---Perlick's fixed-energy
optical metric \cite{Perlick1991}, the sub-Riemannian formulation of Giannoni, Piccione and
Verderesi \cite{GiannoniPiccioneVerderesi1997}, the arrival-time variational theory of
Giannoni and Piccione \cite{GiannoniPiccione2002}, and, decisively for questions of global
minimality, the Morse theory of Giannoni, Piccione and Tausk
\cite{GiannoniPiccioneTausk2002}, which supplies the index-theoretic framework in which
conjugate points and the number of connecting extremals are counted. The companion
programme of Giannoni, Masiello and Piccione develops the timelike extension of Fermat's
principle \cite{GiannoniMasielloPiccione1998}, a Morse theory covering \emph{massive}
particles as well as photons \cite{GiannoniMasielloPiccione2000}, and, on
\emph{conformally stationary} spacetimes, finiteness results for the number of
light rays joining a source to an observer \cite{GiannoniMasielloPiccione2001}.
That last is the nearest published setting to the frozen-$A$ regime of
section~\ref{sec:closed}, though the questions differ: it concerns \emph{null}
geodesics and gravitational lensing, its machinery rests systematically on the
Killing property, and the conformally stationary case enters as an extension
under boundedness assumptions on the conformal factor. Closest of
all to the present variational structure is the fixed-energy theory of Caponio, Corona,
Giamb\`o and Piccione \cite{CaponioCoronaGiamboPiccione2024}, in which solutions of an
indefinite Lagrangian invariant under an infinitesimal symmetry $K$, with fixed energy,
join a point to a flow line of $K$; the arrival time is then differentiated on the
manifold of connecting paths subject to the \emph{semi-holonomic constraint defined by
the Noether charge}, and for a Lagrangian $2$-homogeneous in the velocities this extends
Fermat's principle in a stationary spacetime. The rail constraint $-g(u,W)=\Ehat$ is a
semi-holonomic constraint of exactly that type, which is the structural reason the two
theories look alike. Their treatment of it is instructive and worth stating precisely,
because it also marks the limit of what transfers. Because $K$ is a symmetry, the Noether
charge is automatically constant along any critical point, so the curves of constant
charge are the only candidates; that set is then shown to be a $C^1$ closed submanifold
of the path space with an explicitly characterised tangent space, and critical points of
the action coincide with critical points of its restriction to that submanifold. Their
Lorentz--Finsler setting also yields, for a stationary Finsler spacetime, the local
product structure $L_F=F^2(y,v)+\omega_y(v)\tau-\tfrac12\tau^2$ with $F$ a Finsler metric
on the slice---the general form of which the Randers reduction of
section~\ref{sec:thakurta} is an instance. The timelike extension of Fermat's principle
\cite{GiannoniMasielloPiccione1998} is the closest precedent for the analytical side of
the problem, and two of its findings bear directly on ours. Its authors describe Perlick's
construction, in their own words, as the study of critical points of the arrival time for
curves satisfying a conservation law with respect to a fixed timelike Killing observer
field, reduced by stationarity to a purely spatial Riemannian problem through a
Maupertuis--Jacobi principle---an independent reading of the structure the present
programme sets out to extend. Technically, they prove that for a \emph{timelike} pointwise
constraint on the velocity the space of admissible curves is a $C^1$ Banach manifold,
which the lightlike case is not, and that the arrival time then satisfies the
Palais--Smale condition on a class of manifolds including all globally hyperbolic ones.
The classical
problem and its geometry are reviewed in \cite{HawsKiser1995}; ruled-surface constructions
built from brachistochrones in stationary spacetimes have been developed recently by
Ta\c{s} \cite{Tas2025}. All of this presupposes a symmetry: the charge is \emph{conserved},
by a Killing or conformal-Killing field of the background.

\emph{New here:} the object is not a geodesic of a fixed optical metric but an extremal of
a control problem whose invariant $\Ehat=-g(u,W)$ is \emph{actively maintained} against a
non-stationary background, with $W$ drawn from a Killing--CKV--Kodama hierarchy. The
distinction from \cite{CaponioCoronaGiamboPiccione2024} is exactly this: there $K$ is an
infinitesimal symmetry of the Lagrangian, so the Noether charge is conserved and the
semi-holonomic constraint is preserved automatically. Here $W$ is in general \emph{not} a
symmetry of $g$---for conformal Kerr it is conformal Killing but not Killing, and a
conformal Killing field conserves no charge along \emph{timelike} worldlines---so the
same constraint must be held by control, at the cost of proper acceleration, and $\Ehat$
is a design parameter rather than an integral of the motion. The consequences are
structural, not cosmetic. Their constraint set is the union over all values of the
charge, and is a submanifold \emph{because} the charge is conserved; ours is a single
prescribed level $\{-g(u,W)=\Ehat\}$, and the argument that makes theirs a submanifold is
unavailable once the charge is not conserved. This is why the existence theorem of
Paper~I proceeds by the direct method on an augmented state, closing the non-autonomous
clock equation by a Gr\"onwall estimate and invoking Filippov--Cesari, rather than by
restricting a functional to a constraint submanifold. We do not import that structure,
and say so here to forestall the assumption that it carries over. The same caution
applies to the compactness available in the stationary theory: the Palais--Smale property
established in \cite{GiannoniMasielloPiccione1998} is for the constraint
$\langle\dot z,\dot z\rangle=a<0$, which is preserved by the flow, and we neither verify
nor assume its analogue for the rail.
Three
consequences have no stationary counterpart: the admissible-velocity set can lose
compactness, so the problem possesses a causal boundary at which it ceases to be posed
(section~\ref{sec:domain}); the reduced Hamiltonian is not an interior first integral, so
the optical metric acquires an explicit dependence on the adapted time; and the response to
slow evolution of the background is a genuine first-order perturbation problem with its own
hyperelliptic transcendental content. Our results should be read as extending the
stationary theory in that direction, not as re-deriving it. Where global minimality is at
issue we appeal to the conjugate-point and Morse-theoretic notions of
\cite{GiannoniPiccioneTausk2002} rather than asserting optimality
(Protocol~\ref{prot:status}).

In the
companion Paper~I~\cite{PaperOne} we established the shared control-theoretic
foundations---the
controlled-rail principle, an existence and normality theorem, the non-autonomous
Hamilton--Jacobi verification criterion, and the Killing--CKV--Kodama selector
hierarchy---and treated the spherically symmetric geometries (FLRW and the ingoing Vaidya
black hole). Here we develop the rotating, axisymmetric case: \emph{Thakurta--Kerr}, the
conformal Kerr spacetime $g=A(\eta)^2 g_{\rm Kerr}$, in which frame dragging, an
ergosphere and genus-two spectral curves appear. We derive the breathing-indicatrix
Hamiltonians and the conformal-time ($\eta$) brachistochrone (the arrival branch
$t\equiv\eta$, distinct from proper time $\tau$); the closed-form equatorial separatrices
in Weierstrass functions; the complete local classification at the conformal stationary
limit, with its exact normal forms; the exterior retrograde separatrix; the rotational and
conformal plunge inversion; and the first-order adiabatic response, on-shell and
off-shell, verified against the true non-autonomous flow.

Because the selector fixes where the problem exists, we begin by delimiting it. For
$W=\partial_\eta$ the admissible-velocity set is compact only outside $r=2M$
(section~\ref{sec:domain}), so the compact-control problem ends at the conformal
stationary limit, and formulae continued below it are algebraic continuations rather than
extremals. Protocol~\ref{prot:status} fixes the resulting three-way labelling, and
Protocol~\ref{prot:endpoint} the endpoint convention; both are used without exception in
what follows.

In the rotating case the ergosphere becomes not only a causal feature of the seed Kerr
geometry but also a transition surface for the geometry of the controlled velocity
indicatrix. The conformal-Kerr application therefore isolates how frame dragging, time
dependence and branch-dependent cost functionals compete within a single variational
framework.

\section{Thakurta--Kerr: conformal rotating compact object}
\label{sec:thakurta}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\paragraph{The controlled-rail formalism (from Paper I).} We use the controlled-rail
variational problem established in the companion Paper~I. On a regular domain where the
selector $W$ is timelike and the velocity indicatrix is smooth, compact and uniformly
convex, minimizing the arrival clock is a time-optimal control problem whose minimizer is
a \emph{normal} Pontryagin extremal (Paper~I, Theorem~I.1 and Theorem~I.2); the arrival
clock is free, so the transversality is $H|_{s_f}=0$. Global optimality is certified only
\emph{under the hypotheses} of the non-autonomous Hamilton--Jacobi \emph{verification}
criterion of Paper~I (Proposition~I.3)---the existence of a value function (or viscosity
subsolution) saturated by the candidate extremal---which we invoke as a verification
criterion, not an automatic global guarantee. We do not reprove these; we specialize the
construction to the conformal Kerr geometry.

Thakurta--Kerr is conformal Kerr, $g=A(\eta)^2 g_{\rm Kerr}$
\cite{Thakurta1981,Kerr1963}, one of a family of cosmological geometries
\cite{McVittie1933,SultanaDyer2005,Kaloper2010,NolanMcVittie1998,MelloMaciel2017,FaraoniBook}.
For constant $A$
the transfer theorem maps the problem to Kerr with $E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$,
$J_{\rm eff}=J/A$. Throughout we use $\rp$ (where $\Delta=0$) and $\re=2M$ as
labels for the \emph{seed} Kerr null surface and conformal-stationary-limit surface:
a conformal factor preserves the null \emph{character} of $\Delta=0$, so $\rp$ is a
conformal Killing horizon candidate, but for time-dependent $A(\eta)$ this does
\emph{not} by itself fix the apparent/trapping or event horizon (which require the
null expansions and the global causal structure, not computed here). With that
caveat, $\rp$ is rigid, $\re=2M$ is crossed regularly, and the freezing surface
$A^2 f=\Ehat^2$ breathes.

\paragraph{Sanity check: rail energy along the Thakurta--Kerr rail.} The geometry being
only axisymmetric, no Kodama vector exists and the rail invariant is instead tied to the
conformal Killing vector $\partial_\eta$ (with factor $A'/A$). Along a \emph{geodesic} the
associated energy $\Ehat=-g^{\rm TK}(u,\partial_\eta)$ drifts at exactly the conformal
rate $\dd\Ehat/\dd\tau=A'/A$ (verified numerically to $\sim\!10^{-11}$), whereas along the
\emph{brachistochrone}---instantaneously an on-shell Kerr orbit at
$E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A(\eta)$---the rail holds $\Ehat$ constant to $8.9\times10^{-16}$, the
exact analogue of the spherical Kodama value of Paper~I
(\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/rail\_conservation.py}). This confirms that the controlled charges
$E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$ and $J_{\rm eff}=J/A$ are carried consistently along the orbit.

\paragraph{Indicatrix and Hamiltonians.} Equatorially, write
$\Delta=r^2-2Mr+a^2$, $\bar v^2=1-A^2f/\Ehat^2$, and
$\bar P=P+A^2(2Ma/r)^2/\Ehat^2$ with $P=r^2+a^2+2Ma^2/r$. The rail
$\Ehat=-u_\eta$ makes the indicatrix an ellipse with an \emph{azimuthal} wind
(frame dragging),
\begin{equation}
    \varphi'_0=\frac{2Ma}{r}\frac{\bar v^2}{\bar P},\qquad
    R^2=f\bar v^2+\bar P\varphi_0'^2=\frac{\Delta\,\bar v^2}{\bar P},
    \label{eq:tk-ind}
\end{equation}
the last equality using the Kerr identity $fP+(2Ma/r)^2=\Delta$.

\begin{remark}[the control set is the boundary, and needs no convexification;
evidence level~(I)]
\label{rmk:indicatrix}
Two objects must be kept apart. The \emph{control set} is the indicatrix
$\mathcal I_x$ itself---the sphere of admissible unit velocities cut out by the
rail, which by Lemma~\ref{lem:compact} is the boundary $\partial\mathcal K_x$ of
a compact convex body $\mathcal K_x$ and not that body. The Hamiltonians below
are support functions of $\mathcal K_x$, i.e.\ $H(x,p)=\max_{v\in\mathcal
I_x}\langle p,v\rangle-c(x)$, and the maximum of a linear functional over
$\mathcal K_x$ is always attained on $\partial\mathcal K_x$. Hence taking the
convex hull changes neither the maximum nor the maximiser, and no relaxation or
convexification step is needed for the Pontryagin maximum principle or for the
Filippov--Cesari existence argument of Paper~I: the velocity set is already
compact, and the extremal controls it selects already lie in the physical set
$\mathcal I_x$ rather than in a set of generalised (chattering) controls. The
distinction matters only where $\mathcal K_x$ degenerates: at $\bar v^2=0$ the
body collapses to a point and boundary and body coincide, which is the freezing
surface.
\end{remark}

The conformal-time, coordinate-time and proper-time Hamiltonians are
\begin{align}
    H_\eta&=p_\varphi\varphi'_0
        +R\sqrt{(\Delta/r^2)p_r^2+p_\varphi^2/\bar P}-1,
    \label{eq:Heta}\\
    H_t&=p_\varphi\varphi'_0
        +R\sqrt{(\Delta/r^2)p_r^2+p_\varphi^2/\bar P}-A,
    \label{eq:Ht-tk}\\
    H_\tau&=\tilde p_\varphi\varphi'_0
        +R\sqrt{(\Delta/r^2)p_r^2+\tilde p_\varphi^2/\bar P}-\frac{A^2 f}{\Ehat},
    \label{eq:Htau-tk}
\end{align}
with the conformal gravitomagnetic shift
$\tilde p_\varphi=p_\varphi-2MaA^2/(r\Ehat)$. Here and throughout, $J:=p_\varphi$
is the \emph{axial control costate} conjugate to $\varphi$ in the reduced
problem---it is \emph{not} the mechanical angular momentum $g(u,\partial_\varphi)$
of the extremal, and the two do not agree once the rail is enforced. Since
$\varphi$ is cyclic, $J$ is constant along an extremal at frozen $A$; across
branches it is relabelled by the conformal weight, $J_t=A\,J_\eta$
(Protocol~\ref{prot:names}). The arrival-time branch $H_t$ differs
from $H_\eta$ only in the unit cost ($-1\to-A$) and reduces to it for $A=1$; it is
the branch compared with $\tau$ in the plunge inversion
(section~\ref{sec:inversion}), where its turning potential
$H_{t0}=H_t|_{p_r=0}$ enters. All three are regular through the
ergosphere ($f=0\Rightarrow R^2=\bar v^2\Delta/\bar P>0$) and degenerate only at
the horizon $\Delta=0$, which is therefore conformally invariant---independent of
$A$ and $\Ehat$. The two spatial families share this ellipse: $t$ follows the same
curves as $\eta$ through $t=\int A\,\dd\eta$, while $\tau$ is Riemannian-pure, the
wind cancelling the gravitomagnetic term, so it survives the expansion
(figure~\ref{fig:th-branches}). Algebraic regularity of the printed Hamiltonians
at $f=0$ is, however, a statement about formulae; it is separate from the
question of where the underlying control problem exists at all, to which we now
turn.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{The control domain, and the status of continued formulae}
\label{sec:domain}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
The controlled rail imposes the two constraints
\begin{equation}
    g(u,u)=-1,\qquad -g(u,W)=\Ehat ,
    \label{eq:rail-constraints}
\end{equation}
and the admissible velocities at an event are their common solution set. Whether
that set is compact---hence whether a time-optimal control problem exists---is
decided pointwise by the causal character of the selector $W$, and not by any
convenience hypothesis.

\begin{lemma}[Compactness of the rail slice; evidence level~(I), proved below]
\label{lem:compact}
Let $(V,g)$ be a Lorentzian vector space of signature $(-,+,+,+)$, let $W\in V$
be nonzero, let $\Ehat>0$, and set
$\mathcal U=\{u\in V:\ g(u,u)=-1,\ -g(u,W)=\Ehat,\ u\ \text{future-directed}\}$.
Then $\mathcal U$ is nonempty and compact if and only if $W$ is timelike and
$\Ehat\ge|W|:=\sqrt{-g(W,W)}$, and it is a single point exactly when
$\Ehat=|W|$. If $W$ is null or spacelike, $\mathcal U$ is either empty or
noncompact.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Work in a Lorentz-orthonormal frame. If $W$ is timelike, $W=|W|e_0$ and
\eqref{eq:rail-constraints} give $u^0=\Ehat/|W|$ and
$|\vec u\,|^2=\Ehat^2/|W|^2-1$: a Euclidean sphere, nonempty iff $\Ehat\ge|W|$,
compact always, and degenerating to the single point $u=e_0$ at $\Ehat=|W|$. If
$W$ is spacelike, $W=|W|e_1$ and the rail fixes $u^1=-\Ehat/|W|$ while
$(u^0)^2=1+\Ehat^2/|W|^2+(u^2)^2+(u^3)^2$ leaves $(u^2,u^3)$ unbounded. If $W$ is
null, $W=w(e_0+e_1)$ and the rail fixes only $u^0-u^1=\Ehat/w$, a paraboloid.
Both are noncompact.
\end{proof}

For the selector $W=\partial_\eta$ used throughout, equatorially,
\begin{equation}
    g(W,W)=-A(\eta)^2\Big(1-\frac{2M}{r}\Big)=-A^2f ,
    \label{eq:gWW}
\end{equation}
so $W$ is timelike exactly for $r>2M$, null at $r=2M$, and \emph{spacelike} for
$r<2M$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:compact} the two scalar regularity inequalities of
Paper~I---$W$ timelike and $\Ehat>|W|$---read here
\begin{equation}
    r>2M
    \qquad\text{and}\qquad
    \bar v^2=1-\frac{A^2f}{\Ehat^2}>0 ,
    \label{eq:two-inequalities}
\end{equation}
the second failing on the freezing surface $A=\Af$ where the indicatrix collapses
to a point. Consequently the compact-control problem for this selector
\emph{ends} at $r=2M$. The region $\rp<r<2M$ may lie outside the seed Kerr null
surface, but it is already outside the control domain: there $\mathcal U$ is
noncompact, the support maximum defining the Hamiltonian is not attained, and no
brachistochrone problem is posed.

This is not repaired by a change of chart. Passing to Doran
(horizon-penetrating) coordinates regularises the printed formulae at $\Delta=0$
but leaves the scalar $g(W,W)$ unchanged, so it cannot make $W$ timelike where
\eqref{eq:gWW} says it is not. A Doran- or ZAMO-anchored timelike selector would
define a different rail charge, force, indicatrix, cost and Hamiltonian---a
legitimate and interesting \emph{new} problem, which we do not treat here.

The location of that boundary is not an artefact of the control formulation. In
the static case the maximal region attached to a timelike vector field
$\zeta$---the largest domain of its integral curves, written with $|\zeta|$ as
the lapse---is bounded exactly where $\zeta$ ceases to be timelike
\cite{Lecian2025Cauchy}. That is the same surface, reached by an argument about
maximal extension rather than about compactness of a control set, which is some
reassurance that the criterion of Lemma~\ref{lem:compact} picks out a feature of
the geometry rather than of our formalism.

\begin{protocol}[Status of every curve, figure and statement]
\label{prot:status}
Throughout, each object is one of the following three, and is named accordingly.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(E)] a \emph{controlled-rail extremal}: a normal Pontryagin extremal of
the compact-control problem, on a compact region $r\ge2M+\delta$ with
$\bar v^2>0$;
\item[\rm(C)] a \emph{limiting contact}: a curve of type (E) whose closure meets
$r=2M$, the limit being taken from $r>2M$;
\item[\rm(A)] an \emph{analytic continuation}: a solution of the frozen
Hamiltonian equations continued into $r<2M$, where the control set is not
compact. No optimality of any kind---global, local, or PMP---is claimed for
type-(A) curves, and they are drawn with a distinct line style and legend entry.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

\begin{remark}
Type-(E) does not by itself mean ``fastest''. The maximum principle is necessary,
not sufficient: a minimiser is a PMP extremal, but a PMP extremal need not be
even a local minimiser. We therefore reserve ``minimiser'', ``optimal'',
``fastest'' and the unqualified ``brachistochrone'' for statements accompanied by
a sufficient certificate over the same domain and endpoint protocol, and use
``controlled-rail extremal'' otherwise. The Hamilton--Jacobi verification
criterion of Paper~I is such a certificate; a numerical perturbation of a
computed curve is not.
\end{remark}

\begin{protocol}[Endpoint protocol]
\label{prot:endpoint}
Unless a comparison explicitly states otherwise, all extremals share: a
\emph{fixed launch event}; a \emph{fixed spatial target} on the same radial slice;
a \emph{free arrival clock}; and the branch-dependent shooting condition that
selects the extremal reaching that target. Comparisons between the $\eta$- and
$t$-branches are made at the \emph{relabelled} costate $J_t=A\,J_\eta$, not at the
same numerical value of $J$: at frozen $A$ the two branches trace the same spatial
curve only under that relabelling, so ``same $J$'' and ``same endpoints'' are
different comparisons. The distinct two-spatial-endpoint comparison used in
section~\ref{sec:bvp} is flagged there.
\end{protocol}

\begin{protocol}[Nomenclature]
\label{prot:names}
The following names are used without exception, because in a controlled,
non-stationary problem the familiar words denote different objects.
\begin{itemize}
\item $\Ehat=-g(u,W)$ is the \emph{rail charge}: a quantity \emph{actively
maintained} by the control. Outside a Killing case it is not the conserved
energy of a freely falling test particle, and the rail generally requires proper
acceleration, which can diverge near the freezing surface or the stationary
limit.
\item $J=p_\varphi$ is the \emph{axial control costate}, not the mechanical
angular momentum $g(u,\partial_\varphi)$. At frozen $A$ the two branch costates
obey $J_t=A\,J_\eta$ (Protocol~\ref{prot:endpoint}).
\item The rail minimises a \emph{selected elapsed clock}. It does not minimise
thrust, fuel, impulse, or integrated proper acceleration.
\item $\re=2M$ is the \emph{conformal stationary limit}, and is where the
compact-control problem ends (Lemma~\ref{lem:compact}); we avoid ``ergosphere''
for it when the control-theoretic role is what matters.
\item $\rp=M+\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$ is the \emph{seed Kerr null surface}, equivalently a
\emph{conformal Killing-horizon candidate}. For time-dependent $A(\eta)$ neither
the apparent (trapping) horizon nor the event horizon has been computed here, so
we do not write ``the horizon'' for it. Where $\Delta=0$ labels a pole of an
integrand we say ``third-kind letter at $\Delta=0$''.
\item For $r<2M$, where $W$ is spacelike, we write ``continued orbit'', ``root
pattern'' or ``analytic branch'', never ``capture'', ``plunge'' or ``bounce'':
those words presuppose a control problem that is not posed there
(Protocol~\ref{prot:status}, type~(A)).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The conformal-time ($\eta$) brachistochrone.} The relation
$\dd t=A(\eta)\,\dd\eta$ makes $\eta$ the canonical conformal clock and shows that the
arrival branch carries no new content beyond $t$: the two are the \emph{same} spatial
family. Frozen, the arrival-time Finsler functions obey $F_t=A\,F_\eta$ exactly, so at
fixed $A$ the extremal curves coincide (a constant Finsler rescaling leaves
geodesics invariant), the conserved charge merely relabelled by the conformal weight
$J_t=A\,J_\eta$: the $\eta$-orbit at $J_\eta$ and the $t$-orbit at $A\,J_\eta$ trace the
same $\varphi(r)$ to $7\times10^{-9}$
(\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/eta\_brachistochrone\_check.py}), whereas the naive same-$J$
comparison does \emph{not}. Under a drift the equivalence survives by monotonicity: for
any fixed-endpoint path the arrival time $t_f=\int_0^{\eta_f}A\,\dd\eta$ is a strictly
increasing function of $\eta_f$ ($A>0$), so $\arg\min\eta_f=\arg\min t_f$ for
\emph{every} $A(\eta)$. Hence the frozen, adiabatic and separatrix $\eta$-solutions are
exactly the $t$-solutions of sections~\ref{sec:thakurta}--\ref{sec:closed}; there are
\emph{two} distinct extremal families, not three---the arrival branch $t\equiv\eta$ and
the proper-time branch $\tau$. The $\eta$ gauge is nonetheless the natural one: $H_\eta$
carries unit cost, the extended Hamiltonian $\mathcal H=p_\eta+H_\eta=0$ is the cleanest
non-autonomous form, the drift enters as the single scalar $\varepsilon=A'/A$, and the
off-shell source takes the universal form $S_{\mathcal D}=\eta+\int E_{\rm eff}
H_{E_{\rm eff}}\,\dd\eta$ [equation~\eqref{eq:SD-conformal}].

\paragraph{Non-autonomous optical reduction.} Solving the indicatrix for
$\dd\eta$ at fixed spatial displacement casts the $\eta$-branch as a closed-form
Randers metric, non-autonomous through the conformal factor,
\begin{align}
    \dd\eta &= n(\eta,r)\,\alpha_K+\beta_K,\qquad
    n=\frac{\Ehat}{\sqrt{\Ehat^2-A^2 f}},
    \label{eq:randers}\\
    \alpha_K^2 &= \frac{r^2\,\dd r^2}{f\Delta}+\frac{\Delta\,\dd\varphi^2}{f^2},
    \qquad
    \beta_K = -\frac{2Ma}{rf}\,\dd\varphi.
    \label{eq:randers-data}
\end{align}
The Riemannian data $\alpha_K$ and the one-form $\beta_K$ are \emph{exactly} those
of the \emph{null} Fermat (Randers) metric of Kerr \cite{GibbonsWerner2008}: all
of the mass, rail, and expansion physics is carried by the scalar index $n$,
which multiplies only the Riemannian part, while the frame-dragging one-form
$\beta_K$ is rigid---conformally invariant and independent of $\Ehat$. The
reduction is thus a Zermelo problem with a time-dependent landscape in which only
the metric term breathes, not the wind. It degenerates at the ergosphere ($f=0$,
where $1/F$ is intrinsic, as for stationary Kerr) even though the
Hamiltonian~\eqref{eq:Heta} remains regular there; the limits $\Ehat\to\infty$
(null Kerr optics), $A=1$ (stationary Perlick on Kerr), $a=0$
(Thakurta--Schwarzschild) and $M=a=0$ ($n=1/v$, FLRW) are all recovered.

% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Relation to conformal submersions}
\label{sec:submersion}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
The optical reduction just described is a submersion, and it is worth saying
exactly which one, because that locates the controlled rail inside a body of
existing geometry and---more usefully---identifies precisely which of its
hypotheses the non-stationary problem gives up.

Write a stationary metric in threading form,
$g=-f(\dd t+\beta_a\dd x^a)^2+h_{ab}\dd x^a\dd x^b$ with $f=-g(K,K)>0$, and let
$\pi:M\to S$ be the projection along the orbits of $K$. A vector is horizontal,
i.e.\ $g$-orthogonal to $K$, iff $X^t=-\beta_aX^a$, and for such vectors
$g(X,X)=h_{ab}X^aX^b$: with $h$ on the base, $\pi$ is a Riemannian submersion.
Giving the base instead the fixed-energy optical metric
$a_{ab}=\Ehat^2h_{ab}/[f(\Ehat^2-f)]$ of Perlick \cite{Perlick1991} makes $\pi$
\emph{horizontally conformal}, with dilation
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda^2=\frac{a(\dd\pi X,\dd\pi X)}{g(X,X)}
      =\frac{\Ehat^2}{f\,(\Ehat^2-f)} ,
    \label{eq:dilation}
\end{equation}
that is, the Perlick factor itself; $\Lambda^2\to1/f$ as $\Ehat\to\infty$ gives
the null Fermat case, and $\Lambda^2>0$ exactly above freezing.

This is not incidental. By the Fuglede--Ishihara dichotomy
\cite{Fuglede1978,Ishihara1979,BairdWood2003}---a semiconformal map is either
identically zero or conformal and surjective on the complement of the kernel of
its differential---the optical projection has no option but to rescale $h$
\emph{conformally}. The dichotomy explains the \emph{form} of Perlick's metric;
what it does not supply is the value~\eqref{eq:dilation} of the factor, which
requires solving the rail jointly with the mass shell.

The hypotheses of that framework are exactly what the non-stationary problem
loses, and the loss is quantitative. For the induced structure to descend to a
base at all, the horizontal conformal geometry must be invariant along the
fibres, i.e.\ $W$ must be conformal Killing. Moreover, by Bishop's theorem
\cite{Bishop1972} a Riemannian submersion with connected fibres is Clairaut with girth
$f$---so that $f\sin\theta$ is conserved along geodesics---if and only if its
fibres are totally umbilical \emph{with mean curvature of gradient form},
$H=-\nabla\ln f$. Umbilicity alone is not the
condition; it is umbilicity together with that potential. This is what organises
the recent literature on Clairaut conformal submersions and on umbilical solitons
\cite{MeenaZawadzki2024,Lecian2026Yamabe,Lecian2026Solitons}.
Along a geodesic the rail charge obeys
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd}{\dd\tau}\big(\!-\!g(u,W)\big)
      =-\tfrac12(\mathcal L_Wg)_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu ,
    \label{eq:rail-drift}
\end{equation}
so $\Ehat$ is a Noether--Clairaut invariant precisely when $W$ is Killing.

For Thakurta--Kerr the computation closes exactly, and identifies the control
effort with the small parameter of the paper. Since $g=A(\eta)^2\bar g$ with
$\bar g$ the $\eta$-independent Kerr seed, for which $\partial_\eta$ is Killing,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal L_Wg=\mathcal L_{\partial_\eta}\!\big(A^2\bar g\big)
      =2AA'\,\bar g=\frac{2A'}{A}\,g ,
    \label{eq:tk-ckv}
\end{equation}
so $W=\partial_\eta$ is a \emph{conformal Killing} vector of the full metric
with conformal factor $\psi=A'/A=\varepsilon$ and $\nabla\!\cdot\!W=4\psi$: the
middle rung of the Killing $\to$ conformal-Killing $\to$ Kodama hierarchy, here
made quantitative. Substituting~\eqref{eq:tk-ckv} into~\eqref{eq:rail-drift} and
using $g(u,u)=-1$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd\Ehat}{\dd\tau}\bigg|_{\rm geodesic}=\frac{A'}{A}=\varepsilon ,
    \label{eq:tk-drift}
\end{equation}
\emph{independently of the four-velocity}. The rate at which an uncontrolled
worldline loses the rail is exactly the adiabatic parameter: the control effort
and the expansion parameter of section~\ref{sec:adiabatic} are the same
quantity, and both vanish exactly at frozen $A$.

This also makes precise the price of the rail. Along an arbitrary worldline with
four-acceleration $a=\nabla_uu$,
$\dd\Ehat/\dd\tau=-g(a,W)-\tfrac12(\mathcal L_Wg)_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$, so
holding the rail forces $g(a,W)=\varepsilon$ by~\eqref{eq:tk-drift}. Since
$g(a,u)=0$, only the part of $W$ orthogonal to $u$ contributes, and
$|W_\perp|^2=g(W,W)+g(W,u)^2=\Ehat^2-A^2f=\Ehat^2\bar v^2$; Cauchy--Schwarz in
the (positive definite) rest space of $u$ then gives the \emph{sharp} bound
\begin{equation}
    |a|\ \ge\ \frac{\varepsilon}{\Ehat\,\bar v},
    \qquad \bar v^2=1-\frac{A^2f}{\Ehat^2},
    \label{eq:thrust-bound}
\end{equation}
with equality when $a$ is parallel to $W_\perp$---the minimum-thrust control.
The bound is uniform in the four-velocity and sharpens the qualitative remark of
Paper~I into a statement with a definite locus: writing $A^2f=\Ehat^2(1-\delta)$,
the minimum thrust behaves as $\varepsilon/(\Ehat\sqrt\delta)$, so it
\emph{diverges} as an inverse square root at the freezing surface $\bar v\to0$,
where hypothesis~\eqref{eq:two-inequalities} fails and the indicatrix collapses.
It does \emph{not} diverge at the conformal stationary limit: there $f\to0$,
$\bar v\to1$, and $|a|_{\min}\to\varepsilon/\Ehat$, finite. The two boundaries of
the control domain are therefore of different kinds---one is a failure of
compactness at finite cost, the other a divergence of cost---and the distinction
is worth keeping in view. At frozen $A$ the bound vanishes: no thrust is needed,
which is the stationary case again
(\texttt{verification/verify\_thrust\_bound.wls}). (The companion paper's Vaidya
sector sits one rung lower: there the Kodama selector has
$(\mathcal L_Kg)_{vv}=2m'(v)/r$ with $\nabla\!\cdot\!K=0$, so it is Killing for
$m'=0$ but not even conformal Killing otherwise, and the drift
$-m'(v)(u^v)^2/r$ does depend on the four-velocity.)

That failure can be checked directly against the characterisation of
\cite{MeenaZawadzki2024}: a conformal submersion with connected fibres and
dilation $\lambda$ is Clairaut with $r=e^{\mathfrak f}$ if and only if
\emph{three} conditions hold together---$\nabla\mathfrak f$ is horizontal, the
fibres are totally umbilical with $H=-\nabla\mathfrak f$, and $\lambda$ is
\emph{constant along the fibres}. We test the last, which is necessary on its
own. (Their setting is a submersion between Riemannian manifolds; here the total
space is Lorentzian and the horizontal space spacelike, so what follows is a
statement about the horizontal structure, not a literal application of their
theorem.) Our fibres are the orbits of
$W=\partial_\eta$, and with the conformal factor in place the dilation reads
$\Lambda^2=\Ehat^2/[A^2f(\Ehat^2-A^2f)]$, so
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\partial\Lambda^2}{\partial\eta}
      =\frac{2\Ehat^2r^2\big[\Ehat^2r+(4M-2r)A^2\big]}
             {(2M-r)A^2\big[\Ehat^2r+(2M-r)A^2\big]^2}\;\psi ,
    \label{eq:dilation-fibre}
\end{equation}
which vanishes if and only if $\psi=A'/A=0$. The optical submersion is therefore
Clairaut exactly at frozen $A$, and the obstruction is measured by the same
scalar as everything else in this section.

Two consequences follow from that failure, and they are exactly the two
statements this section needs. First, by \cite[Prop.~1]{MeenaZawadzki2024} the
horizontal distribution of a conformal submersion is totally geodesic if and only
if $\lambda$ is constant on the fibres; so when $A$ runs the horizontal
distribution of the optical submersion is \emph{not} totally geodesic. Second,
their Corollary~7 turns a Clairaut conformal submersion into a Clairaut
\emph{Riemannian} one by rescaling the base with $1/\lambda$---a construction
available only when $\lambda$ descends to the base, that is, only when it is
fibre-constant. This is the precise reason the optical structure of the
non-stationary problem does not live on a quotient, and why the optical metric of
Paper~I must instead carry an explicit dependence on the adapted time.

The controlled rail is what replaces the Clairaut relation when the submersion
fails to be Clairaut. In the stationary case $-g(u,W)=\Ehat$ holds
\emph{automatically} along geodesics and there is nothing to control; in the
non-stationary case it fails at the rate~\eqref{eq:rail-drift}, and the
constraint must be \emph{enforced}, the required four-acceleration supplying
exactly that deficit, $a\cdot W=\tfrac12(\mathcal L_Wg)_{\mu\nu}u^\mu u^\nu$. The
umbilical, Clairaut and conformal-Killing hypotheses are therefore not a
framework this problem sits inside but the boundary of the region where it is
non-trivial: the present programme lives in their complement, and the size of the
failure is the control effort. Equations~\eqref{eq:dilation}--\eqref{eq:tk-drift}
are verified in \texttt{verification/verify\_submersion\_link.wls} and
\texttt{verify\_dilation\_fibre.wls}.

It is worth collecting what the single scalar $\psi=A'/A$ turns out to measure.
It is the conformal factor of the selector, $\mathcal L_Wg=2\psi g$; the rate at
which an uncontrolled worldline loses the rail,~\eqref{eq:tk-drift}; the
minimum thrust needed to hold it,~\eqref{eq:thrust-bound}; the failure of the
dilation to be constant along the fibres---hence of the optical submersion to be
Clairaut, of its horizontal distribution to be totally geodesic, and of the
optical structure to descend to a quotient,~\eqref{eq:dilation-fibre}; and the failure of the weight $f^2=\ln A$ to
be parallel, hence of the associated soliton to be Einstein. Five conditions
whose classical statements are independent coincide here, and all five degenerate
together at frozen $A$.

\paragraph{Soliton selectors: the drift is pure curvature.} There is one class of
selector for which~\eqref{eq:rail-drift} closes in the curvature alone. A Ricci
soliton is a vector field whose deformation tensor is prescribed by the geometry,
$\tfrac12\mathcal L_Xg+\mathrm{Ric}=\lambda g$; substituting this
into~\eqref{eq:rail-drift} and using $g(u,u)=-1$ gives, for $W=X$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd\Ehat}{\dd\tau}\bigg|_{\rm geodesic}
      =\lambda+\mathrm{Ric}(u,u) .
    \label{eq:soliton-drift}
\end{equation}
No derivative of $W$ survives: the effort required to hold the rail is the
soliton constant plus the Raychaudhuri focusing scalar, that is, the energy
density the traveller measures. In vacuum the drift is the constant $\lambda$;
in vacuum \emph{and} steady ($\lambda=0$) one recovers $\mathcal L_Xg=0$, so $X$
is Killing and the rail costs nothing---the stationary case. This places the
controlled rail inside the soliton literature
\cite{Hamilton1986,Lecian2026Yamabe,Lecian2026Solitons} in a precise way, and
locates the present geometry relative to it. For a conformal Killing selector
the soliton condition reads $\mathrm{Ric}=(\lambda-\psi)g$, that is, the
spacetime would have to be \emph{Einstein}. Thakurta--Kerr is not: already at
$a=0$ the conformal factor sources an imperfect fluid and
$\mathrm{Ric}-\tfrac14Rg\neq0$ for generic $A(\eta)$, while the seed is
Ricci-flat at $A$ constant. That this must be so is Birkhoff's theorem: a
spherically symmetric solution of the vacuum field equations is necessarily
static, so no running conformal factor can be attached to Schwarzschild without
sourcing matter. The verified statement and the classical one agree, and the
obstruction is therefore structural rather than an artefact of the ansatz. The soliton class therefore does not contain this
selector, whereas the conformal-Killing class does---which is exactly why the
hierarchy has three rungs and not two
(\texttt{verification/verify\_tk\_ckv\_rail.wls},
\texttt{verify\_soliton\_rail.wls}).

\paragraph{The weighted seed.} There is a second, sharper point of contact, and
it identifies exactly where the two frameworks meet and where they part. The
soliton literature cited above works on \emph{weighted} manifolds
$e^{-2f^2}g_{\mu\nu}$, and studies the four-velocity as a conformal Killing field
of the weighted metric, arriving at a vanishing divergence. Choosing the weight
as $e^{-2f^2}=A(\eta)^{-2}$, that is $f^2=\ln A$, the weighted metric is
\emph{precisely the Kerr seed}: $W=\partial_\eta$ is then a genuine Killing field
of it, with $\operatorname{div}_{\bar g}W=0$, whereas for the full metric
$\operatorname{div}_gW=4A'/A$. So that framework describes exactly the frozen
level of the present construction.

What separates them is equally precise. The soliton results quoted require the
weight to be \emph{strictly parallel}, $\nabla_\mu F=0$, which is necessary and
sufficient there for the solitons to be Einstein---whence constant sectional
curvature and space forms. Our weight is $f^2=\ln A(\eta)$, with
$\nabla_\mu f^2=(A'/A,0,0,0)=(\psi,0,0,0)$: it is parallel if and only if $A$ is
constant. The requirement is in fact stronger still in that framework, where the
admissible weights are taken constant and indeed vanishing, so a running $A$ is
excluded there twice over. Their hypothesis therefore holds exactly at frozen $A$, and the running
weight---the failure of that hypothesis, measured by the same $\psi$ that
sets the rail drift and the control effort---is the entire subject of the
non-stationary theory. Consistently, conformal Schwarzschild is not Einstein for
generic $A$ while the seed is Ricci-flat
(\texttt{verification/verify\_weighted\_seed.wls}).

\paragraph{Curvature of the optical base, and why Myers-type hypotheses are
unavailable.} Since the extremals are geodesics of the optical metric and optical
length is arrival time, a curvature bound on the base would bound the arrival
time before a conjugate point. It is therefore worth recording what that
curvature is. At frozen $A$ the conformal-Kerr optical geometry is the Kerr one
at $E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$ (section~\ref{sec:closed}); we record its
non-rotating limit, where the base is Riemannian and the computation is closed.
With $h=\dd r^2/f+r^2\dd\varphi^2$ and $a=\Lambda^2h$, and $\Ehat$ standing for
$E_{\rm eff}$ throughout, the Gauss curvature of the fixed-energy optical
surface is
\begin{equation}
    K=\frac{M\big[16M^3+2(9\Ehat^2\!-\!14)M^2r
      +(3\Ehat^4\!-\!19\Ehat^2\!+\!16)Mr^2
      -(2\Ehat^2\!-\!3)(\Ehat^2\!-\!1)r^3\big]}
      {\Ehat^2r^5\big[2M+(\Ehat^2-1)r\big]} ,
    \label{eq:optical-gauss}
\end{equation}
which reduces as $\Ehat\to\infty$ to the classical Fermat value
$-(2M/r^3)(1-3M/2r)$. Its far-field behaviour carries a threshold that appears to
be new:
\begin{equation}
    r^3K\ \longrightarrow\ -\frac{M(2\Ehat^2-3)}{\Ehat^2}
    \qquad (r\to\infty),
    \label{eq:optical-asym}
\end{equation}
so the distant optical geometry is \emph{focusing} for $\Ehat^2<3/2$ and
\emph{defocusing} for $\Ehat^2>3/2$, the critical rail energy being
$\Ehat=\sqrt{3/2}$, an asymptotic speed $v=1/\sqrt3$. At the critical value the
leading term vanishes and $r^4K\to-23M^2/3$. Since focusing is what produces
conjugate points, $\Ehat=\sqrt{3/2}$ separates two regimes for the global
behaviour of distant extremals.

The threshold is a genuinely finite-energy phenomenon, and this is worth making
explicit because the Gaussian curvature of equatorial sections of Schwarzschild is
classical in the \emph{energy-independent} setting of null optics. Gibbons and
Werner \cite[Eqs.~(7)--(8)]{GibbonsWerner2008} write the equatorial optical
metric $g^{\rm opt}=h/f$ as a surface of revolution and give its Gauss curvature;
for Schwarzschild vacuum this is $K=-(2M/r^3)(1-3M/2r)$, which our
$\Ehat\to\infty$ limit of~\eqref{eq:optical-gauss} reproduces identically. The
bracket vanishes only at $r=3M/2$, inside the stationary limit, so $K<0$
throughout the exterior and \emph{no threshold exists} in the null case. It appears only once the optical metric carries the rail
energy: the sign of the leading far-field coefficient
in~\eqref{eq:optical-asym} is that of $-(2\Ehat^2-3)$, which cannot change in a
family with no $\Ehat$ to vary.

For every $\Ehat$, however, $K\to0$ as $r\to\infty$, and it is tempting to stop
there. One should not, because decay of the \emph{sectional} curvature does not by
itself defeat a Myers-type hypothesis. Myers' condition is on what he calls the
mean curvature---in his normalisation the \emph{sum} of the $n-1$ sectional
curvatures of the planes through a direction $v$, that is $\mathrm{Ric}(v,v)$ for
unit $v$, whence his diameter bound $\pi\sqrt{n-1}/e$ rather than the modern
$\pi/\sqrt k$ \cite[footnotes 3--4]{Myers1941}---and he stresses that this is
genuinely weaker than a sectional bound, exhibiting $S^2\times S^2$, where the
sectional curvature ``varies from $0$ to $1$ inclusive'' while the mean curvature
stays constant and positive \cite[\S4]{Myers1941}. The hypothesis must therefore be
tested on the Ricci tensor of the three-dimensional optical metric, not on $K$.

Doing so closes the question by a wider margin than asymptotic flatness would. In
the optical frame $\mathrm{Ric}$ is diagonal, with one radial and two coincident
tangential eigenvalues, and the optical scalar curvature is
\begin{equation}
    R_{\rm opt}=\frac{-2M^2\big[32M^2+4(3\Ehat^2-8)Mr+(3\Ehat^4-6\Ehat^2+8)r^2\big]}
      {\Ehat^2r^5\big[2M+(\Ehat^2-1)r\big]} .
    \label{eq:optical-scalar}
\end{equation}
Writing $r=2M(1+s)$ with $s>0$ turns the bracket into a quadratic in $s$ whose three
coefficients are $24\Ehat^4$, $48\Ehat^2(\Ehat^2-1)$ and $8(3\Ehat^4-6\Ehat^2+8)$,
all positive for $\Ehat>1$---the last because $3X^2-6X+8$ has negative discriminant.
Hence $R_{\rm opt}<0$ at \emph{every} exterior point and for \emph{every} rail
energy. But $\mathrm{Ric}(v,v)\ge e^2>0$ for all unit $v$ would force
$R_{\rm opt}\ge3e^2>0$. The Myers hypothesis therefore fails here by \emph{sign},
not by decay, and it fails pointwise rather than only in the far field; no
diameter---hence no arrival-time---bound follows from it. This also disposes of the
$S^2\times S^2$ loophole rather than merely noting it. It is worth adding that the
instrument in question is Myers' Lemma, which asserts that no arc longer than
$\pi\sqrt{n-1}/e$ minimises, and that the Lemma requires no completeness at all:
completeness enters only in his \S3, to pass from the Lemma to compactness. Nothing
is being evaded by the incompleteness of the optical exterior. Myers' own footnote 5
is also apposite---his results ``are proved for positive definite metrics, and hence
are not immediately applicable to the physical theory''---and it is precisely the
fixed-energy optical reduction, which replaces the Lorentzian problem by a
Riemannian one, that makes his theorem available to be tested in the first place.

The curvature-operator hypotheses fail here for the same reason, and it is worth
stating them accurately since their hypotheses are what decide the matter. The
theorems in this family are about \emph{closed} manifolds: Hamilton's
three-dimensional result concerns a compact three-manifold of positive Ricci
curvature \cite{Hamilton1982}, his four-dimensional one compact four-manifolds
with positive curvature operator \cite{Hamilton1986}, and B\"ohm and Wilking prove
that on a \emph{compact} manifold the normalised Ricci flow carries a metric with
\emph{2-positive} curvature operator---the sum of the two smallest eigenvalues
positive, weaker than positivity---to constant sectional curvature
\cite[Thm.~1]{BoehmWilking2008}. The optical base is three-dimensional and
non-compact, so the four-dimensional result is not the applicable one and the
compactness hypothesis is unmet in any case. Independently of that,
\eqref{eq:optical-scalar} already excludes the curvature condition itself: a
2-positive curvature operator has positive trace, hence $R_{\rm opt}>0$, and here
$R_{\rm opt}<0$ everywhere. Conjugate points must therefore be located directly
from the Jacobi equation, as they are in Paper~I, rather than inferred from a
curvature-operator or Myers-type hypothesis. Both hypotheses fail for one and the
same computed reason. Equations~\eqref{eq:optical-gauss}--\eqref{eq:optical-asym}
and this conclusion are verified in
\texttt{verification/verify\_myers\_optical.wls}.

\paragraph{Conjugate points and Maxwell points, and which branch they belong to.}
It matters here which clock the optical length measures, because the two branches
carry \emph{different} optical metrics and, as it turns out, opposite global
behaviour. Per unit $h$-length the two clocks advance by
\begin{equation}
    \Lambda_t^2=\frac{\Ehat^2}{f(\Ehat^2-f)},
    \qquad
    \Lambda_\tau^2=\frac{f}{\Ehat^2-f},
    \label{eq:two-lambdas}
\end{equation}
differing by $\Ehat^2/f^2$, which is not constant: the two surfaces are not
homothetic and their curvatures are unrelated. Both are Perlick's
\cite{Perlick1991}: in his notation, with $g(W,W)=-e^{2V}$ and specific energy
$e^{\mathcal C}$, the proper-time factor is his $h_{\mathcal C}
=h/(e^{2(\mathcal C-V)}-1)$ and the arrival-time factor his
$\hat h_{\mathcal C}=e^{2\mathcal C-4V}h_{\mathcal C}$, which reduce to
$\Lambda_\tau^2$ and $\Lambda_t^2$ exactly. He also notes that in the
non-static case the $t$-problem acquires a one-form---``a sort of Coriolis
force''---so that its extremals are $\hat h_{\mathcal C}$-geodesics only when
that form is exact; that is precisely the Randers structure of
section~\ref{sec:thakurta} and the reason the results below are stated in the
non-rotating limit. Equation~\eqref{eq:optical-gauss} is $\Lambda_t$, so it is
the \emph{arrival}-time surface, and the statements belong to the
$t\equiv\eta$ branch. In the non-rotating
frozen limit both branches are Riemannian---there is no wind at all---so the
classical second-variation machinery applies to each: the Jacobi equation is
$\ddot{\mathcal J}+K\mathcal J=0$, and $K<0$ on a domain precludes conjugate
points there. The threshold of~\eqref{eq:optical-asym} turns out not to be merely
asymptotic.

\begin{proposition}[No conjugate points on the arrival branch above the
threshold; evidence level~(I) for the curvature and its sign, level~(II) for the
quoted sign-change radii]
\label{prop:noconj}
For the frozen, non-rotating \emph{arrival-time} optical surface,
$K_t(r)<0$ for \emph{every} $r>2M$ if and only if $\Ehat^2\ge3/2$. Consequently,
for $\Ehat^2\ge3/2$ no exterior $t$-branch extremal carries a conjugate point
\emph{for equatorial perturbations}, and every such extremal is a local minimiser
of the arrival time among equatorial competitors. The restriction is necessary
and is discussed after Proposition~\ref{prop:globalmin}.
\end{proposition}

Two facts fix the threshold. At the stationary limit the numerator
of~\eqref{eq:optical-gauss} is $-4\Ehat^4M^4<0$ for \emph{every} $\Ehat$, so $K<0$
near $r=2M$ always; and its cubic coefficient is
$-(2\Ehat^2-3)(\Ehat^2-1)$, negative exactly when $\Ehat^2>3/2$. Below the
threshold a region of positive curvature opens at large radius, and its inner
edge runs off to infinity as $\Ehat^2\to(3/2)^-$: numerically it sits at
$r/M=22.75,\,59.53,\,576.7,\,5751.7$ for $\Ehat^2=1.20,\,1.40,\,1.49,\,1.499$.

Negative curvature does \emph{not}, however, give global minimality here, and the
reason is worth stating because it is not a focusing effect. The optical surface
is $\{r>2M\}\times S^1$, an annulus: it is not simply connected, so
Cartan--Hadamard yields local minimality without uniqueness. (On a \emph{simply
connected} manifold of nonpositive sectional curvature there is exactly one
geodesic between any two points, the fact underlying the finiteness results of
\cite{GiannoniMasielloPiccione2001}; the annular topology is precisely what
removes it here.) The two extremals
leaving a source with equal and opposite azimuthal costate are exchanged by
$\varphi\to-\varphi$ and reconverge after a swept angle $\pi$; that antipodal
locus is a \emph{Maxwell set}, and it survives however negative $K$ becomes.
This can be sharpened into a genuine global statement, because the optical surface
is not merely negatively curved but \emph{complete}. The radial element is
$\dd s=\Ehat\,\dd r/[f\sqrt{\Ehat^2-f}]$, which has a simple pole with residue
$2M$ at $r=2M$ and tends to $\Ehat/\sqrt{\Ehat^2-1}>0$ as $r\to\infty$: the inner
end is at infinite optical distance, logarithmically, and the outer end at
infinite distance, linearly. Both ends of the exterior are therefore infinitely
far in arrival time.

\begin{proposition}[Global minimality within a homotopy class, arrival branch]
\label{prop:globalmin}
For the frozen, non-rotating \emph{arrival-time} optical surface with
$\Ehat^2\ge3/2$: the surface is complete with $K_t<0$, so its universal cover
$\{r>2M\}\times\mathbb R$ is complete, simply connected and nonpositively
curved. By the Cartan--Hadamard theorem the
exponential map there is a diffeomorphism, so any two points are joined by
exactly one geodesic, which minimises. Projecting back, each homotopy class of
equatorial paths carries \emph{exactly one} controlled-rail extremal, and that
extremal is the \emph{global} minimiser of arrival time within its class of
equatorial competitors.
\end{proposition}

Two remarks sharpen what this gives. First, the certificate is of the kind the
maximum principle needs, not merely an analogue: on the universal cover the
distance $d(p,\cdot)$ is smooth away from $p$ and satisfies
$|\nabla d|_a=1$ \emph{identically}, so it is a value function
\emph{saturating} the eikonal Hamilton--Jacobi equation, and
Proposition~\ref{prop:hjb-frozen} below records it in that language.

\begin{proposition}[Saturated Hamilton--Jacobi certificate, frozen arrival branch]
\label{prop:hjb-frozen}
Under the hypotheses of Proposition~\ref{prop:globalmin}, the optical distance
from a fixed launch point on the equatorial surface, lifted to its universal
cover, is a smooth value function satisfying the eikonal equation with equality.
Every controlled-rail extremal in a given homotopy class is calibrated by it, and
is therefore the global minimiser of arrival time among equatorial competitors in
that class.
\end{proposition}

Second, the condition is not all-or-nothing below the threshold. The numerator
of~\eqref{eq:optical-gauss} is a cubic in $r$ whose leading coefficient factors
as $-(\Ehat^2-1)(2\Ehat^2-3)$; for $\Ehat^2<3/2$ it has a single exterior root
$r_c(\Ehat)$, and $K_t<0$ on the whole inner region $2M<r<r_c$. So for
\emph{every} $\Ehat>1$ the extremals confined to $r<r_c(\Ehat)$ carry no
conjugate point and are local minimisers; the threshold is the value at which
$r_c$ becomes infinite and the statement extends to the whole exterior.
Numerically $r_c/M=29.5,\,20.4,\,22.8,\,31.3,\,59.5,\,576.7$ at
$\Ehat^2=1.02,\,1.10,\,1.20,\,1.30,\,1.40,\,1.49$: the region is never small, and
$r_c$ is not monotone---it dips to a minimum near $\Ehat^2\simeq1.1$ before
diverging as $\Ehat^2\to(3/2)^-$.

\begin{remark}[why this does not extend to a running $A$ by continuity]
\label{rmk:nodrift}
It is worth saying why the frozen certificate does not simply perturb. It is
\emph{saturated}: $|\nabla d|_a=1$ holds with equality, leaving no margin for a
perturbation argument to consume. Producing a value function for the drifting
problem is not a corollary of the frozen one but amounts to solving the
perturbed Hamilton--Jacobi equation---that is, to the adiabatic expansion itself,
whose first order is section~\ref{sec:adiabatic}. Global minimality under a
genuine drift therefore remains open, and we claim it nowhere.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Why ``equatorial'' cannot be dropped: the tangential curvature.}
The restriction in Propositions~\ref{prop:noconj}--\ref{prop:hjb-frozen} is not a
technicality, and it is worth being explicit because the statements would
otherwise read as stronger than they are. The optical geometry is
three-dimensional, $a=\Lambda_t^2(\dd r^2/f+r^2\dd\Omega^2)$, and a geodesic in
the equatorial plane has \emph{two} independent normal directions. The equatorial
plane is totally geodesic, so by the Gauss equation the in-plane one is governed
by $K_t$ of~\eqref{eq:optical-gauss}, which is what the propositions use. The
out-of-plane direction is governed by a second sectional curvature,
\begin{equation}
    K_{\rm tan}=\frac{M\big[{-}64M^3+8(11-6\Ehat^2)M^2r
      +(9\Ehat^4\!-\!44\Ehat^2\!+\!40)(-M)r^2
      +2(2\Ehat^2\!-\!3)(\Ehat^2\!-\!1)r^3\big]}
      {\Ehat^2r^5\big[2M+(\Ehat^2-1)r\big]} ,
    \label{eq:ktan}
\end{equation}
and this one is \emph{not} negative. Two facts settle it. Writing the metric as
$a=A\,\dd r^2+B\,\dd\Omega^2$, one has $K_{\rm tan}=(1-B'^2/4AB)/B$, so wherever
$B'=0$---the radius of the circular controlled-rail orbit, which exists for every
$\Ehat$ and reduces to the photon sphere $r=3M$ as $\Ehat\to\infty$---one gets
$K_{\rm tan}=1/B>0$ identically. And in the far field the two coefficients are
exactly opposite, $K_{\rm tan}\sim-2K_t$, so whichever side of $\Ehat^2=3/2$ one
is on, one of the two is positive at large radius. The three-dimensional optical
metric is therefore never everywhere non-positively curved, at any rail energy,
and Cartan--Hadamard is unavailable for it.

\paragraph{When the restriction lifts.} The obstruction is not, however, the end
of the matter, because in \emph{spherical} symmetry the out-of-plane Jacobi field
is generated by a symmetry and can be written down instead of being bounded. The
rotation tilting the orbital plane, $K=-\sin\varphi\,\partial_\theta
-\cot\theta\cos\varphi\,\partial_\varphi$, is Killing for the optical metric, and
a Killing field restricts to a Jacobi field along any geodesic. On the equator
its optical norm is
\begin{equation}
    |K|_a=\sqrt{B(r)}\,\bigl|\sin\varphi\bigr| ,\qquad B=\Lambda_t^2r^2 ,
    \label{eq:outofplane-J}
\end{equation}
which vanishes at $\varphi=0$ and next at $\varphi=\pi$. The out-of-plane Jacobi
fields vanishing at the launch point form a one-dimensional space, so this is the
\emph{first} out-of-plane conjugate point, and it sits at swept azimuth exactly
$\pi$---fixed by the rotation group, independently of $\Ehat$, of $M$, and of
$K_{\rm tan}$ altogether. Hence
\begin{equation}
    \text{no conjugate point at all}
    \iff
    K_t<0 \ \text{ along the arc} \ \ \text{and} \ \ \Phi<\pi ,
    \label{eq:noconj-full}
\end{equation}
with $\Phi$ the swept azimuth. Paper~I establishes $\Phi<\pi$ for the
spherically symmetric rail, so in that sector---and in the non-rotating frozen
limit used here---Propositions~\ref{prop:noconj}--\ref{prop:hjb-frozen} hold
without the equatorial restriction, in full three dimensions. What genuinely
requires the restriction is the \emph{rotating} case $a\neq0$, where the rotation
group is broken to $U(1)$, no symmetry generates the out-of-plane field, and
$K_{\rm tan}$ must be confronted directly. We do not treat it.
Equation~\eqref{eq:outofplane-J} is verified in
\texttt{verification/verify\_outofplane\_jacobi.wls}.

The positivity of $K_{\rm tan}$ is not a defect of the construction but the same
physics identified above from the topological side. Positive tangential curvature at the circular-orbit
radius is focusing, and focusing near a photon sphere is exactly what produces
the unbounded sequence of relativistic images in black-hole lensing. The
obstruction to an unrestricted global claim thus has two independent expressions,
the absence of a light-convex function and the positivity of $K_{\rm tan}$, and
they describe the same phenomenon. Equations~\eqref{eq:ktan} and the sign
statements are verified in \texttt{verification/verify\_3d\_sectional.wls}.

Within the equatorial plane, global minimality therefore holds \emph{within}
homotopy classes and fails \emph{between} them---and the failure between them is
precisely the Maxwell set.
For $\Ehat^2\ge3/2$ the cut locus of the exterior optical surface is thus purely
of Maxwell type: minimality is lost at the first antipodal crossing by symmetry
rather than by focusing, and never by focusing at all. Below the
threshold the two mechanisms coexist and conjugate points must be located
directly from the Jacobi equation, as in Paper~I.

\paragraph{The proper-time branch behaves oppositely.} None of this transfers to
the $\tau$-branch, and the contrast is a result rather than a caveat. Its optical
factor is $\Lambda_\tau$ of~\eqref{eq:two-lambdas}, and the corresponding Gauss
curvature satisfies
\begin{equation}
    r^3K_\tau\ \longrightarrow\ +M \qquad (r\to\infty),
    \label{eq:ktau-asym}
\end{equation}
\emph{positive for every rail energy}: there is no threshold, and the distant
proper-time optical geometry always focuses. A scan over the exterior finds
$K_\tau>0$ on all but a negligible set. Nor is that surface complete: the radial
element tends to $1/\Ehat$ at $r\to2M$ rather than developing a pole, so the
stationary limit lies at \emph{finite} proper-time distance---as it must, a
falling worldline reaching it in finite proper time. Both hypotheses of
Cartan--Hadamard therefore fail on the $\tau$-branch, and no analogue of
Propositions~\ref{prop:noconj} or~\ref{prop:globalmin} is available there:
conjugate points do occur, and locating them requires the Jacobi equation
directly, as in Paper~I. The two branches, which section~\ref{sec:bvp} shows
disagree already about which reaches deeper, thus disagree about global structure
as well.

This asymmetry is visible in Perlick's own worked example, by a completely
different route, and we had not noticed it there. Integrating both problems
explicitly in Rindler space he reports that the $\tau$-brachistochrones
\emph{have cut points}---``not all $h_{\mathcal C}$-geodesics \dots\ are
brachistochrones in the strict sense meaning curves of \emph{minimal} travel
time''---while the $t$-brachistochrones ``cover any point in
$U_{\mathcal C}$ and are \emph{free of cut points}''
\cite[\S3, Figs.~5--6]{Perlick1991}. Flat spacetime in an accelerated frame is
about as far from Schwarzschild as one can get while keeping a static Killing
field, and the conclusion is the same: the arrival branch is the one with the
good global behaviour. What the curvature computation adds is the mechanism---%
$K_\tau>0$ and incompleteness at the stationary limit---and the fact that it
persists for every rail energy. Both computations are in
\texttt{verification/verify\_two\_branch\_optics.wls}.

It is worth saying where this sits in the relativistic Morse theory the cited
literature already provides, because that theory supplies the step from ``no
conjugate point'' to ``local minimiser'', and also shows what a \emph{global}
count would require. Giannoni, Masiello and Piccione
\cite{GiannoniMasielloPiccione2000} work on precisely our variational data---a
fixed launch event, arrival on a timelike curve, the arrival-time functional, and
a pointwise constraint fixing $\langle\dot z,\dot z\rangle$---and prove that a
curve is a critical point of the arrival time if and only if it is a timelike
geodesic, with, at a nonconjugate endpoint, \emph{Morse index equal to the
geometric index}: the number of conjugate points counted with multiplicity. Index
zero is then a local minimum, which is the implication
Proposition~\ref{prop:noconj} requires. The difference is that our critical
points are extremals of a controlled problem rather than geodesics of $g$, so the
index theorem we actually invoke is the classical one on the optical surface;
their result is the relativistic precedent for the step.

Their Morse relations also locate our Maxwell set structurally. They express the
indexed count of connecting geodesics through the Poincar\'e polynomial of the
path space, so it is the topology of that space that fixes how many connecting
extremals exist. Here the optical surface is an annulus, its path space is
accordingly organised by winding number, and the connecting extremals come in
families---one per turn about the hole, exchanged in pairs by
$\varphi\to-\varphi$. That is the Maxwell structure described above, seen from
the topological side rather than the metric one. We do not verify the
pseudo-coercivity their relations require, and make no global count here.

That the count should be infinite rather than finite is, however, the expected
situation and not a pathology. In the stationary theory finiteness of the
connecting geodesics is a \emph{theorem with hypotheses}: global hyperbolicity
together with a strictly invariant, spatially proper \emph{light-convex}
function, whose closed sublevels are spatially compact and therefore confine the
connecting geodesics to a compact set \cite{GiannoniMasielloPiccione2001}. It is worth
being precise about which hypothesis fails for us, since the two are independent:
the Schwarzschild exterior \emph{is} globally hyperbolic, so that half is
satisfied. What is missing is the convex function. A black-hole exterior supplies
none---which is why gravitational lensing by a black hole produces, in principle,
an unbounded sequence of relativistic images rather than finitely many. Our winding families are the
controlled-rail counterpart of that sequence: the extremals are timelike and
actively forced rather than null, so the correspondence is structural rather than
literal, but the mechanism---an annular exterior admitting no confining convex
function---is the same. There is a further point of contact worth recording. Gibbons and Werner read
the focusing of light rays as a \emph{topological} effect, obtaining the
deflection angle by integrating the Gaussian curvature over a domain bounded by
the rays and applying Gauss--Bonnet \cite{GibbonsWerner2008}. That is the same
reading we are forced to for the Maxwell set: what limits global minimality here
is the topology of the exterior rather than a local focusing condition, and the
curvature enters through an integral over a region rather than through a
pointwise sign. The two accounts are consistent, and the annular topology is the
common ingredient.

It also identifies what a sufficient condition for global
minimality would have to look like here. The available ones in this literature
are convexity hypotheses, light-convexity being equivalent, for a $C^2$ function,
to positive semidefiniteness of its Hessian on the light cone. We establish no
such hypothesis and claim no global minimality beyond the first antipodal
crossing. These statements are verified
in \texttt{verification/verify\_conjugate\_maxwell.wls}; they are established for
the non-rotating frozen limit, and the rotating case is not claimed.

\subsection{On-shell adiabatic response}
\label{sec:adiabatic}
By Lemma~I.4 of the companion Paper~I, every source of the form
$\partial_\lambda F=N_\lambda/S^{3/2}$ reduces \emph{uniquely} to the Abelian basis
$\{U_k=\int r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r\}$ through the degree-ten polynomial identity (a
nonsingular $11\times11$ linear system, no fit); for Thakurta--Kerr the slow parameter is
$A$ and the source is generated by $E_{\rm eff}\partial_{E_{\rm eff}}+J_{\rm
eff}\partial_{J_{\rm eff}}$. We give the resulting coefficients below and do not repeat
the reduction lemma. The dynamical quasi-constant drifts; the
\emph{orbit shape} $\varphi(r,A)$ itself admits a first-order adiabatic
form~\cite{BornFock1928,BenderOrszag1999} that is explicit up to a single higher
transcendental. Writing the frozen integrand as
$F(r;E_{\rm eff},J_{\rm eff})=\dd\varphi/\dd r$ (equation~\eqref{eq:t-K} for the
$t$-branch, equation~\eqref{eq:tau-sextic} for $\tau$) and linearizing the
\emph{conformal weights} of the Kerr charges---both $E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$ and
$J_{\rm eff}=J/A$ carry weight $A^{-1}$, since for $u^{\rm TK}=u^{\rm Kerr}/A$ one
has $-g^{\rm TK}(u,\partial_\eta)=A\,E_{\rm eff}$ and
$g^{\rm TK}(u,\partial_\varphi)=A\,J_{\rm eff}$---in
$A(\eta)=A_0(1+\varepsilon\eta+\dots)$, $\varepsilon=A'/A$, gives
\begin{equation}
    \varphi(r,A)=\underbrace{\varphi_0(r;E_{\rm eff},J_{\rm eff})}_{\text{frozen, Kleinian}}
    +\varepsilon\big[\mathcal{C}(r)+\psi(r)\big]+O(\varepsilon^2),
    \label{eq:phi-adiab}
\end{equation}
where the first-order coefficient is the elapsed-clock--weighted \emph{scaling}
derivative
\begin{equation}
    \delta\varphi=-\!\int\!\big(\Ehat\,\partial_E F+J\,\partial_J F\big)\,\eta\,\dd r
    =-\!\int\!\big(E\,\partial_E+J\,\partial_J\big)F\,\eta\,\dd r,
    \label{eq:deltaphi-EJ}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the Euler scaling operator $(E\partial_E+J\partial_J)F$ acting through both
drifting charges (the $J\,\partial_J F$ term is required by axisymmetric momentum
conservation and was absent in an earlier form; Vaidya, whose parameter is the
mass rather than a conformal factor, keeps only its $\partial_m F$ source). Here
$T_{\rm br}(r)$
is the clock the orbit accumulates along the frozen slice: coordinate time
$T_{\rm br}=t(r)$ on the $t$-branch, proper time $T_{\rm br}=\tau(r)=\int\!L_\tau\,\dd r$ with
$L_\tau=\sqrt{Q/w}$, $Q=\Delta\varphi'^2-2a\beta\varphi'+r^2/(r^2+a^2)$, on the
$\tau$-branch (the extra $A^{-2}$ of the proper-time functional affects the
\emph{timing}, not the shape, so equation~\eqref{eq:phi-adiab} is common to both
branches).

\paragraph{Closed piece.} The scaling derivative reduces to the same depth-one
class as $\varphi_0$ itself, by the classical reduction of abelian differentials to
first/second kind~\cite{BakerAbelian1897,GriffithsHarris1978}. Both pieces
$\partial_E F$ and $\partial_J F$ share the identical basis: solving
\begin{equation}
    \big(E\partial_E+J\partial_J\big)F=\dd_r\!\left[\frac{\mathcal{A}(r)}{\mathcal{R}}\right]
    +\sum_{k=0}^{4}c_k\,r^k/\mathcal{R}
    \label{eq:dEphi0}
\end{equation}
for a degree-five numerator $\mathcal{A}(r)=\sum_{i=0}^{5}a_i r^i$ and constants
$c_k=\Ehat\,c_k^{E}+J\,c_k^{J}$ (here $\mathcal{R}$ is the branch sextic radical,
$\sqrt{R_6}$ or $\sqrt{S}$) closes in the second kind with \emph{no} third-kind
(horizon-pole) contribution for both branches (verified to machine precision); the
$J\,\partial_J F$ term merely adds its own coefficients $c_k^{J}$ to the existing
$c_k^{E}$, requiring no new building block
(\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/conformal\_source\_Jterm.py}). Hence
$\mathcal{C}(r)=-\tfrac12\big(E\partial_E+J\partial_J\big)\varphi_0\,\eta(r)$ is
a product of depth-one objects and is closed in the Kleinian/abelian class.

\paragraph{Clock differentials.} The branch clock $T_{\rm br}$ enters $\mathcal{C}$ through its
differential, which we obtain in closed form on the branch curve. On the
$\tau$-branch the proper-time density reduces to a \emph{rational} second-kind
differential,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd\tau}{\dd r}=\frac{r^2(r-2M)}{\sqrt{S}},
    \label{eq:clock-tau}
\end{equation}
the frame-dragging term cancelling identically (verified to $10^{-16}$). The
$t$-branch coordinate-time density is \emph{also entirely on-curve}: using the
Randers form $\dd t=n_t\alpha+\beta$ and the identity
$(r-2M)Q_2+DE\,(J(r-2M)+2Ma)^2=E^2r^3\Delta$ (so that $n_t\alpha=E^2r^3/(f\sqrt{R_6})$
exactly, $E^2-f=DE/r$), the gravitomagnetic wind collapses and
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd t}{\dd r}=\frac{\rho_t(r)}{\sqrt{R_6}},\qquad
    \rho_t=\frac{E^2r^3-(2Ma/r)\,\mathcal K}{f}
          =P_3(r)+\frac{R_\Delta(r)}{\Delta},
    \label{eq:clock-t}
\end{equation}
with $\mathcal K$ the frozen numerator [equation~\eqref{eq:t-K}], the ergosphere pole
$r=2M$ \emph{removable}, and third-kind poles only at the horizons ($\Delta=0$). The
second-kind polynomial part is
$P_3=E^2r^3+2E^2Mr^2+4E^2M^2r+8E^2M^3-2(E^2-1)JMa$ (giving the clock vector
$b=(8E^2M^3-2(E^2-1)JMa,\,4E^2M^2,\,2E^2M,\,E^2,\,0)$, the $t$-analogue of the
$\tau$-branch $b=(0,0,-2M,1,0)$), and $R_\Delta$ is the degree-one horizon numerator
(explicit in \texttt{reproduce\_reductions.py}). Thus \emph{neither} branch leaves
the genus-two curve---the $t$-branch merely adds a third-kind horizon letter to the
second-kind clock (verified against the Hamiltonian flow to $10^{-10}$); the apparent
Doran double cover of a first analysis was spurious. Table~\ref{tab:branch-adiab}
collects the branch data: the two solutions share the structure~\eqref{eq:phi-adiab}
and differ \emph{only} in the branch curve and the clock.

\paragraph{The correction $\psi$: algebraic coefficients times genus-two
polylogarithms.} By parts and the shuffle identity~\cite{Chen1977} the symmetric
combination $\rho+\tilde\rho=\partial_E\varphi_0\,\eta$
($\rho=\int\partial_E\varphi_0\,\dd\eta$,
$\tilde\rho=\int\eta\,\partial_E F\,\dd r$) is closed, and the antisymmetric
remainder
\begin{equation}
    \psi(r)=\tfrac12\Ehat\,(\rho-\tilde\rho)
    \label{eq:psi-irr}
\end{equation}
is a depth-two iterated integral. Expand $\partial_E\varphi_0$ and $\eta$ in the
cohomology basis $U_k=\int r^k/\mathcal{R}\,\dd r$: the reduction
$(E\partial_E+J\partial_J)F=N/\mathcal{R}^3$ with $N=\Ehat\,N_E+J\,N_J$ a degree-seven
polynomial ($N_E=EJ\,r^4(r-2M)^2\,DE$, and $N_J$ its angular analogue; the horizon
factor $\Delta$ cancels identically in both) gives
$(E\partial_E+J\partial_J)\varphi_0=\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{R}+\sum_{k=0}^{4} C_k U_k$,
$C_k=\Ehat\,c_k^{E}+J\,c_k^{J}$, and on the
$\tau$-branch $T_\tau=U_3-2M\,U_2$, i.e.\ $b=(0,0,-2M,1,0)$. The \emph{complete}
first-order coefficient $\delta\varphi=\mathcal{C}+\psi=-\!\int(\Ehat\,\partial_E F
+J\,\partial_J F)\,\eta\,\dd r$ then reorganizes, with \emph{no term discarded}, into
\begin{equation}
    \delta\varphi=
    \underbrace{\tfrac12\!\sum_{k<j}Q_{kj}\,W_{kj}}_{\text{weight two}}
    \;-\;\underbrace{\eta\Big(\tfrac{\mathcal{A}}{\mathcal{R}}
    +\tfrac12\!\sum_k C_k U_k\Big)}_{\text{clock}\times\text{abelian}}
    \;+\;\underbrace{\sum_j b_j\,\mathcal{I}_j}_{\text{elementary}},
    \label{eq:psi-split}
\end{equation}
with $Q_{kj}=C_k b_j-C_j b_k$, $W_{kj}=\int(U_k\,\dd U_j-U_j\,\dd U_k)$ the
genus-two polylogarithms, and $\mathcal{I}_j=\int\mathcal{A}\,r^j/\mathcal{R}^2\,
\dd r$ \emph{elementary} (rational integrands, evaluating to logarithms). Every
piece is explicit: the second group is a product of the elapsed clock with the
reduced abelian integrals (weight one), the third an elementary logarithmic
remainder---none is spurious or dropped. The coefficients are \emph{algebraic}
(rational in $M,a,E,J$ through the $C_k=\Ehat c_k^E+J c_k^J$); we
verified~\eqref{eq:psi-split} to $4\times10^{-14}$ for the energy part and to
$1\times10^{-7}$ for the full Euler source with all coefficients \emph{given}, not
fitted. The
holomorphic$\times$holomorphic coefficient vanishes, $Q_{01}=0$ (the clock has no
first-kind part). On the $t$-branch the same reorganization holds with
$\mathcal{R}^2=R_6$ and $K$ of Table~\ref{tab:branch-adiab}: its clock is likewise
on-curve, with the second-kind vector $b$ of equation~\eqref{eq:clock-t} feeding the same
$W_{kj}$ of $R_6$, plus the horizon third-kind part $R_\Delta/\Delta$ contributing
$\ln\sigma$-quotients at $r_\pm$ (the coordinate-time analogue of the $\tau$-branch's
dipole at infinity)---no double cover. The full $t$-branch assembly (the
$W_{kj}$-reorganization plus the horizon piece) reproduces the direct double integral
$-\Ehat\!\int\!\partial_E F\,t\,\dd r$ to $9\times10^{-13}$.

The building blocks $W_{kj}$ are length-two iterated Abelian
integrals~\cite{Chen1977} of $y^2=\mathcal{R}^2$, of \emph{weight two}. Their elliptic
($g{=}1$) analogues are the polylogarithms of Beilinson--Levin and
Brown--Levin~\cite{BeilinsonLevin1994,BrownLevin2011}; a rigorous \emph{higher-genus}
(hyperelliptic) polylogarithm framework---base points, paths, tangential
regularization, monodromy and single-valued completion---is the subject of recent work
\cite{BauneEtal2024,DHokerSchlotterer2024,DHokerHiddingSchlotterer2025,DHokerEnriquezSchlottererZerbini2026},
and would be required before ``genus-two
dilogarithm'' can be used as a theorem-level term; we therefore adopt the neutral name
above and keep irreducibility and basis dimension conjectural. Accordingly we fix the
following convention, used without exception below: the neutral phrase
\emph{length-two iterated Abelian integral on the genus-two hyperelliptic curve} is the
only classification we claim, and wherever ``genus-two dilogarithm'' appears it is an
explicitly \emph{descriptive shorthand} for that object---never a statement that a
function class with specified kernels, relations, normalisation, monodromy or
single-valued completion has been established. As iterated integrals
they do \emph{not} reduce to a weight-one Kleinian $\zeta,\sigma$
combination~\cite{BakerAbelian1897,BuchstaberEnolskii1997}, because for
genus two the Abel image of single points lies on the theta divisor
$\Theta=W_{g-1}$~\cite{MumfordTata}, where $\zeta,\wp$ are singular; the single-point
second-kind integrals are instead Fay theta-derivatives at shifted (off-divisor)
arguments. The one third-kind piece---the clock's dipole at infinity, residue
$\rho_0=M/(E^2-1)^{3/2}$---integrates to
\begin{equation}
    L(u)=\log\frac{\theta[\delta](u-e_+)}{\theta[\delta](u-e_-)}
    \label{eq:thirdkind-theta}
\end{equation}
(Fay prime form~\cite{Fay1973}, odd half-characteristic $\delta$, $e_\pm$ the Abel
images of the two points at infinity; differentiating $L(u(r))$ back reproduces the
third-kind differential to $5\times10^{-8}$). The $W_{kj}$ are length-two iterated Abelian integrals of the frozen curve; their
elliptic ($g{=}1$) analogues admit Kronecker--Eisenstein
representations~\cite{BeilinsonLevin1994}, but that reference does not by itself
establish a higher-genus construction (a genus-two framework needs base points, paths,
tangential regularization, monodromy and single-valued completion). The
weight-two content is genuine and \emph{conjecturally} irreducible to weight one, while
all coefficients remain
algebraic.

\paragraph{Numerical validation.} Table~\ref{tab:adiab-valid} compares the
hybrid form~\eqref{eq:phi-adiab}--\eqref{eq:psi-irr} (closed
$\mathcal{C}$ plus numerical $\psi$) against the directly integrated non-autonomous
coefficient $-\Ehat\!\int\partial_E F\,\eta\,\dd r$, for both branches
($M=1$, $a=0.9$, $\Ehat=1.4$; $t$-branch $J=6$, $\tau$-branch scattering $J=2.5$).
The agreement is at the discretization floor and scales linearly with
$\varepsilon$, confirming that the split is an exact reorganization (integration by
parts) rather than an approximation. Figure~\ref{fig:phi-closed} shows the same
comparison as orbit shapes in the plane, closed form against direct integration,
for both branches on compact sub-arcs bounded away from the turning point. The frozen family
itself already exhibits nontrivial ergosphere dynamics: for penetrating $t$-branch
angular momenta the orbit dips inside $r_e$, co-rotates, and turns just outside the
horizon, and a growing conformal factor eventually expels it from the ergosphere at
an adiabatic threshold (figure~\ref{fig:phi-penetrating}).

\subsection{Complete first-order response and true-flow validation}
Table~\ref{tab:adiab-valid}
and figure~\ref{fig:phi-closed} certify only the \emph{algebraic} identity (closed
$\mathcal{C}+\psi$ equals the direct integral of the same coefficient); they do not by
themselves test the adiabatic form against the actual non-autonomous dynamics. Integrating
the true non-autonomous optical-metric geodesic---the branch-Hamiltonian flow with
$E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A(\eta)$, $J_{\rm eff}=J/A(\eta)$---and comparing to
$\varphi_0+\varepsilon\,\delta\varphi$ shows the \emph{physical} accuracy is $\sim2\%$
(linear in $\varepsilon$), far above the algebraic floor
(figure~\ref{fig:phi-true-dynamic}). Linearizing the extended Hamiltonian
$\mathcal H=p_s+H=0$ about the frozen extremal---with the slow variable
$\lambda$ the elapsed clock measured \emph{from the free-arrival endpoint} (where
$H=0$ by the transversality of Paper I), oriented inward, and
$A(\eta)=A_0(1+\varepsilon\lambda+\cdots)$---gives, in the \emph{normalized} momentum
$P_r=p_r/A$ (\ref{app:firstorder}), the first-order shift of the momentum shell
$\delta P_r=(\lambda\,\Theta H-S_{\mathcal D})/H_{P_r}$ and
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    &\frac{\delta\varphi}{\varepsilon}=\int\!\Big[\frac{\partial_{P_r}G}{H_{P_r}}\,
    \big(\lambda\,\Theta H-S_{\mathcal D}\big)-\lambda\,\Theta G\Big]\dd r,\qquad
    G=\frac{H_J}{H_{P_r}}=\varphi',\\[2pt]
    &S_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)=\!\int_0^{\lambda}\!\mathcal D H\,\dd\lambda',\qquad
    \mathcal D\equiv\underbrace{E_{\rm eff}\partial_{E_{\rm eff}}+J_{\rm eff}\partial_{J_{\rm eff}}}_{\Theta}
    +\,P_r\partial_{P_r},
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:adiab-exact}
\end{equation}
which closes the correction to $O(\varepsilon^2)$. The source uses the \emph{full} Euler
operator $\mathcal D$: the normalization $P_r=p_r/A$ is a time-dependent (conformally
symplectic) rescaling---\emph{not} canonical---so the canonical flow in the original
$(r,p_r)$ carries, after passing to $P_r$, a dilation term
$-\!\left(A'/A\right)P_r$ (\ref{app:firstorder}), and the shell-departure rate is $\mathcal D H=\Theta
H+P_r H_{P_r}$, \emph{not} $\Theta H$ alone (omitting $P_r H_{P_r}$ leaves an
$O(\varepsilon)$ error against the true flow---this is the correction of
\ref{app:firstorder}). Here $\Theta$ differentiates only the \emph{dynamical}
charges $E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$, $J_{\rm eff}=J/A$ (the rail invariants $\Ehat,J_{\rm TK}$
are inert); for single-parameter Vaidya there is no rescaling, $\mathcal D=\Theta=m\partial_m$.
The source $S_{\mathcal D}$ is \emph{terminally anchored} ($S_{\mathcal D}=0$ at the
free-arrival endpoint); the reduction holds on compact sub-arcs bounded away from the
turning point ($H_{P_r}\to0$), and is verified to machine precision against the true
canonical flow (slope~$\approx2$;
\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/adiabatic\_first\_order\_exact.py}, \texttt{tests/}). This splits
into an \emph{on-shell} part (parameter drift, the closed form $\mathcal C+\psi$ of
Section~\ref{sec:thakurta}: weight-one clock$\times$Abelian terms together with the
weight-two $W_{jk}$, and no third-kind dilogarithm since the on-shell residue vanishes)
and an \emph{off-shell}
part driven by the dilation letter $\int P_r H_{P_r}\dd\lambda=\int p_{r0}\dd r$ (the
radial action, reduced in closed form in \ref{app:firstorder},
Equations~\eqref{eq:action-reduction}--\eqref{eq:ak}): a length-two iterated integral on the
same spectral curve, closing in $\zeta,\sigma$ \emph{plus} one further length-two
iterated Abelian integral sourced by the third-kind letter at the seed Kerr null
surface $\Delta=0$ (the object we call, descriptively only, a genus-two
dilogarithm).

\paragraph{Universal form of the off-shell source.} The source $S_{\mathcal D}$ does
not, in fact, require the per-metric reduction to be written in closed form: it admits
a metric-independent structure fixed by the \emph{Randers} character of the
arrival-time Hamiltonian. Any such Hamiltonian is
$H=\beta(r,E)\,J+\sqrt{A_{rr}(r,E)\,p_r^2+A_{\varphi\varphi}(r,E)\,J^2}-1$, homogeneous
of degree one in $(p_r,J)$ apart from the normalization $-1$; Euler's theorem then
gives the identity
\begin{equation}
    \big(J\,\partial_J+P_r\,\partial_{P_r}\big)H=H+1,
    \label{eq:finsler-euler}
\end{equation}
valid for \emph{arbitrary} $\beta,A_{rr},A_{\varphi\varphi}$ (verified symbolically).
On the frozen shell $H=0$ this collapses the integrand of $S_{\mathcal D}$ to a
universal skeleton. For the \emph{conformal} family ($\mathcal D=\Theta+P_r\partial_{P_r}$),
$\mathcal D H=E H_E+(H+1)$, so
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)=\lambda+\int_0^{\lambda}E_{\rm eff}H_{E_{\rm eff}}\,\dd\lambda',
    \label{eq:SD-conformal}
\end{equation}
the $\lambda$ term universal and $E_{\rm eff}H_{E_{\rm eff}}$ the single
metric-specific ``energy-Euler'' piece (fixed by $E\partial_E v^2=2(1-v^2)$). The
non-conformal mass-function (Vaidya) family instead yields the boundary form
$S_{\mathcal D}=[\,rp_r\,]-\lambda$ (Paper~I): the two deformation classes carry
\emph{different} universal sources---no single formula spans both, since the slow
parameter couples to the geometry differently (a conformal rescaling of all momenta
versus a self-similar mass shift)---yet both share the same $\pm\lambda$ Finsler skeleton
from~\eqref{eq:finsler-euler}. Equation~\eqref{eq:SD-conformal} is verified to machine
precision against the direct $\int\mathcal D H\,\dd\lambda$
(\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/universal\_SD\_source\_check.py}).

\subsection{Fixed-charge and fixed-endpoint perturbations}

is computed at \emph{fixed canonical charges} $(\Ehat,J)$: it is the same-launch,
fixed-charge response of the orbit to the slow drift, the object that the true-flow
test of \ref{app:firstorder} validates. A genuine two-point boundary-value
problem with \emph{fixed spatial endpoints} instead lets the conserved charge float,
$J=J_0+\varepsilon\,J_1+O(\varepsilon^2)$, with $J_1$ fixed by holding the arrival
azimuth,
\begin{equation}
    0=\delta\varphi_{\rm dyn}+J_1\,\partial_J\varphi_0

    J_1=-\,\frac{\delta\varphi_{\rm dyn}}{\partial_J\varphi_0},

\end{equation}
where $\delta\varphi_{\rm dyn}$ is the fixed-charge displacement above. The
fixed-endpoint shape then adds $\varepsilon J_1\,\partial_J\varphi_0$, which merely
re-selects the launch angle and does not alter the transcendental class. We report
the fixed-charge form throughout and quote~\eqref{eq:J1-fixedendpoint} as the explicit
map to the fixed-endpoint problem.

\subsection{Frozen penetration phase diagram}
The frozen ergosphere dynamics is organized
by two \emph{analytic} thresholds, both of which we verify against direct
root-tracking (figure~\ref{fig:penetration-phase}). Writing $DE=(E_{\rm eff}^2-1)r+2M$,
for $E_{\rm eff}<1$ a \emph{freezing wall} $r_w=2M/(1-E_{\rm eff}^2)$ (the zero of
$DE$, and of $R_6$) advances inward as $A$ grows; the launch radius $r_0$ is
swallowed at
\begin{equation}
    A_c^{\rm wall}=\frac{\Ehat}{\sqrt{1-2M/r_0}},
    \label{eq:Awall}
\end{equation}
beyond which no orbit issues from $r_0$. Below the wall the $t$-orbit crosses the
ergosphere iff its outer turning lies inside $r_e$; the prograde boundary is
\begin{equation}
    J_c^+(A)=\frac{2M^2}{a}+a+\frac{a\,A^2}{2\Ehat^2}
    \label{eq:Jcplus}
\end{equation}
(matching the numerics to $10^{-15}$), the retrograde one a saddle-node $J_c^-(A)$. The window $(J_c^-,J_c^+)$ splits into a
\emph{plunge} core (no turning, the orbit reaches $r_+$) and a thin
\emph{penetrate-and-bounce} rim just below $J_c^+$, where the orbit dips inside
$r_e$, co-rotates, and turns in the annulus $r_+<r_{\rm t}<r_e$ before exiting
(figure~\ref{fig:bounce}). The breathing conformal factor thus \emph{expels} orbits
from the ergosphere at $A_c^{\rm wall}$: a purely non-stationary effect.

\section{Equatorial closed forms and the ergosphere}
\label{sec:closed}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------

All closed forms in this section and in \ref{app:explicit} are obtained at
\emph{frozen conformal factor}: constant $A$, equivalently stationary Kerr with
the substitution $E\to E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$ (and $J\to J/A$, the spin $a$ retained) in every
parameter (section~\ref{sec:thakurta}). They are therefore the exact
\emph{instantaneous} orbit shapes on a Thakurta--Kerr slice of given $A$; the
running $A(\eta)$ makes the full problem non-autonomous---the quasi-constant
drift $K^{\rm TK}(\eta)=K^{\rm Kerr}(E_{\rm
eff}(\eta))+O(A'/A)$---so a genuinely time-dependent trajectory is recovered by
letting $E_{\rm eff}=E_{\rm eff}(\eta)$ run through this family, not by a single
autonomous curve.

\subsection{Separatrix in Weierstrass functions}
\label{sec:separatrix}
From the doranTau factorization $\Delta-\Jc^2 w=f(r^2+c^2)$, $c=a/E$
\cite{RosignoliKerr}, the
equatorial $\tau$-separatrix $\varphi(r)$ is genus~1, closed in the
Weierstrass functions $\wp,\sigma,\zeta$
\cite{WhittakerWatson,DLMF,Hackmann2008,Hackmann2010}: the angle is linear in the
uniformizing variable $z(r)=\wp^{-1}(A/r+B)$ plus two $\sigma$-ratios
[\ref{app:explicit}, equation~\eqref{eq:sep-phi}], regular \emph{through} the
ergosphere because the factor $r^2+c^2$ divides the turning quartic and removes
$\re$ from the radicand. The Boyer--Lindquist form winds logarithmically at the
horizon; the Doran shift---a second, quasi-lemniscatic elliptic
curve---cancels it, so the ergosphere-penetrating separatrix
$\varphi_D=\varphi_{\rm BL}-\varphi_{\rm shift}$ is closed on two elliptic curves.

What controls the reduction is the \emph{genus} of the turning radical, not the
rationality of $\mathcal K$. Being Riemannian-pure
(section~\ref{sec:thakurta}, the gravitomagnetic wind cancels), the $\tau$-branch
factorizes as doranTau---the factor $r^2+c^2$ dividing the quartic---dropping a
generically genus-2 reduction to genus~1. Generic $t$-branch orbits carry the
wind and have the sextic radical
$\mathcal R(r)=r\,Q_2(r)\,[(E^2-1)r+2M]$ (genus~2, hyperelliptic), with no such
cancellation. The $t$-brachistochrone nonetheless has its own genus-1 closed-form
separatrix, by a \emph{different} mechanism: at the marginal-capture (saddle-node)
momentum $J_c^-$ [equation~\eqref{eq:Jc-minus}] the sextic develops a \emph{double
root}, $\mathcal R=(r-r_*)^2 Q_4$ with $Q_4$ quartic (whereas the prograde
ergosphere crossing $J_+^t$ has only a simple root and stays genus~2). The double
root drops the radical to $Q_4$, and $\varphi(r)$ closes in the same
Weierstrass $\wp,\sigma,\zeta$ form as the $\tau$-separatrix
(\ref{app:explicit}). Both separatrices are thus genus~1---the $\tau$ one
via the doranTau factorization, the $t$ one via a double root---while generic
(non-separatrix) $t$-orbits are genuinely genus~2, closed only in genus-2
Kleinian functions.
The Kerr geodesic structure and its constants are classical
\cite{Carter1968,Chandrasekhar}. Validated end-to-end to
$2.8\times10^{-10}$ through the ergosphere, and general across parameters.

\subsection{Local classification at the conformal stationary limit}
\label{sec:trichotomy}
The behaviour of an equatorial $\tau$-branch controlled-rail extremal at the
conformal stationary limit $\re=2M$ is governed by \emph{two} independent data,
and they must not be conflated. The \emph{shape} radical of the quadrature
$\dd\varphi/\dd r=\mathcal K_\tau/\sqrt{\mathcal S}$ depends on $J$ only through
$J^2$, so no argument based on it can ever distinguish $J=+\Jc$ from $J=-\Jc$.
The Hamilton equations can, because the conformal gravitomagnetic momentum
$\tilde p_\varphi=p_\varphi-2MaA^2/(r\Ehat)$ is \emph{linear} in $J$ and, at the
stationary limit,
\begin{equation}
    \tilde p_\varphi(\re)=J-\Jc,\qquad \Jc=a/E,
    \label{eq:ptil-re}
\end{equation}
vanishing at the prograde marginal alone. Every asymmetry between the two
marginal signs descends from~\eqref{eq:ptil-re}.

Two exact identities organise the classification. First, the radial momentum
obtained from $H_\tau=0$ is the shape radical up to a factor that is regular and
nonvanishing at $\re$,
\begin{equation}
    p_r^2=\frac{\mathcal S(r)}{D_E(r)^2\,\Delta(r)^2},
    \qquad D_E(r)=(E^2-1)r+2M ,
    \label{eq:pr2-S}
\end{equation}
so the root structure of $p_r^2$ is exactly that of $\mathcal S$. Second, at the
marginal value the turning bracket factorises exactly,
\begin{equation}
    r\Delta-\Jc^2 D_E(r)=(r-2M)\big(r^2+\Jc^2\big),
    \label{eq:marginal-factor}
\end{equation}
so that $\mathcal S=r(r-2M)^2D_E\,(r^2+\Jc^2)=r^4f^2w\,(r^2+\Jc^2)$ with
$w=\Ehat^2-f>0$: a perfect square times a positive factor. Hence at $|J|=\Jc$
the reality wall $\sqrt{wf}$ is removed \emph{for both signs of $J$}, and $\re$
is a \emph{double} root of $\mathcal S$ rather than a simple one. What separates
the two marginal signs is therefore not reality but the \emph{rate} at which
$\re$ is approached: with
$\mathcal T(r)=f/E-\tilde p_\varphi\varphi_0'$ the denominator of
$\dot r=R^2(\Delta/r^2)p_r/\mathcal T$,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal T(\re)=-\frac{a\,(J-\Jc)}{\bar P_e},
    \qquad \bar P_e=4M^2+2a^2+a^2/E^2 ,
    \label{eq:T-at-re}
\end{equation}
which vanishes if and only if $J=+\Jc$. Collecting these gives the following
four-case classification, which replaces the trichotomy of the previous version.

\begin{proposition}[Local classification at $\re$, $\tau$-branch, frozen $A$;
evidence level~(I), with level~(II) confirmation]
\label{prop:classification}
Let $\Jc=a/E$ and let the extremal be ingoing. Then, as $r\to\re^+$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(i)] $|J|>\Jc$: $p_r^2(\re)>0$ and the extremal reflects at a smooth
periapsis $r_{\min}>\re$, the outer root of $r\Delta=J^2D_E$; the shape is
regular, $r-r_{\min}\propto(\varphi-\varphi_{\min})^2$.
\item[\rm(ii)] $|J|<\Jc$: $p_r^2$ has a \emph{simple} zero at $\re$ and
$\mathcal T(\re)\neq0$, so $\dot r\sim-c\sqrt{r-\re}$ and $\re$ is attained in
\emph{finite} parameter. The shape has a semicubical cusp,
\begin{equation}
    \varphi-\varphi_e=\tfrac{2}{3}K\,(r-\re)^{3/2}+O\big((r-\re)^{5/2}\big),
    \qquad
    K=\frac{EJ}{a^2}\sqrt{\frac{2M}{a^2-E^2J^2}} .
    \label{eq:cusp}
\end{equation}
\item[\rm(iii)] $J=-\Jc$: $p_r^2$ has a \emph{double} zero at $\re$ while
$\mathcal T(\re)\neq0$, so $\dot r\simeq C\,(r-\re)$ with
\begin{equation}
    C=-\frac{\sqrt{4M^2+\Jc^2}}{8M^2},
    \label{eq:retro-rate}
\end{equation}
and the parameter diverges logarithmically, $s\sim|C|^{-1}\ln[1/(r-\re)]$: the
stationary limit is an \emph{asymptotic endpoint}, not attained in finite
parameter and \emph{a fortiori} not crossed. The shape nevertheless has a finite
one-sided tangent,
\begin{equation}
    \left.\frac{\dd\varphi}{\dd r}\right|_{\re^+}
      =\frac{2MEJ}{a^2\sqrt{4M^2+\Jc^2}},
    \label{eq:corner-slope}
\end{equation}
so the total swept angle is finite and the extremal converges to a single point
of the circle $r=\re$ with a definite limiting direction.
This is worth contrasting with the geodesic flow, which crosses $r=2M$ in finite
parameter: at the marginal retrograde value the \emph{controlled-rail} flow is
incomplete at a surface the geodesic flow passes through without difficulty. The
rail is not a reparametrised geodesic congruence, and this is the sharpest way to
see it.
\item[\rm(iv)] $J=+\Jc$: $p_r^2$ has a double zero at $\re$ \emph{and}
$\mathcal T$ a simple one; the two linear vanishings cancel and
\begin{equation}
    \dot r\big|_{\re}
      =-\frac{a^2E^2\sqrt{4M^2+\Jc^2}}{2M\big[a^2(1+E^2)+4E^2M^2\big]}<0 ,
    \label{eq:pro-rate}
\end{equation}
finite and strictly ingoing: $\re$ is reached in finite parameter and crossed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

Cases (ii) and (iii) are distinct local normal forms and must not be merged: the
cusp amplitude $K$ in~\eqref{eq:cusp} \emph{diverges} as $|J|\to\Jc^-$, so the
cusp family does not connect continuously to the marginal case. Case (iii)
supersedes the statement, made in the previous version of this work, that the
retrograde marginal reflects with a cusp because counter-rotation is forbidden
inside $\re$; that argument is withdrawn. It was wrong in its detail as well as
in its role---near $\re$ the retrograde marginal has $\dd\varphi/\dd r<0$ and
$\dot r<0$, hence $\dot\varphi>0$, and is locally \emph{co}-rotating.
Proposition~\ref{prop:classification} is verified symbolically and against
high-precision numerics in \texttt{verification/verify\_cusp\_corner.wls} and
\texttt{verification/verify\_marginal\_hamilton.wls}
(\ref{app:validation}, tier~I and tier~II).

Only case (iv) continues below $\re$. That continuation is carried out in Doran
(horizon-penetrating) coordinates
\cite{Doran2000,Natario2009,MartelPoisson2001},
$\varphi_D=\varphi_{\rm BL}-\varphi_{\rm shift}$, and---since $g(W,W)>0$ for
$r<2M$ with the selector $W=\partial_\eta$---it is an \emph{analytic
continuation of the frozen equations}, not a controlled-rail extremal: no
optimality is claimed for it (section~\ref{sec:domain}).

\paragraph{Retrograde branch and the $\tau/t$ asymmetry.} The classification above
is a $\tau$-branch statement. The set that \emph{reaches} $\re$ is symmetric in
the sign of $J$---the doranTau factorization depends on $J$ only through $J^2$, so
the ingoing extremal reaches $\re$ for $|J|<\Jc$, prograde and retrograde sharing
the same threshold despite frame dragging---but the set that \emph{crosses} is
not: only the single prograde $+\Jc$ crosses, the degeneracy
\eqref{eq:T-at-re} of the approach rate breaking the $\pm J$ symmetry at the
marginal value. The $t$-branch is not protected. Its turning function (from
$H_\eta=0$ at $p_r=0$, with $b=2Ma/r$) is
\begin{equation}
    N(r)=\big(\bar P-Jb\bar v^2\big)^2-J^2\Delta\bar v^2,
    \label{eq:tbranch-turn}
\end{equation}
which is only linear-plus-quadratic in $J$. At the ergosphere
$N(\re)=\bar P_e\,(\bar P_e-2aJ)$ with
$\bar P_e=\bar P(\re)=4M^2+2a^2+a^2/E^2$, so the $t$-orbit crosses $\re$ at the
single \emph{prograde} value
\begin{equation}
    J_+^{t}=\frac{\bar P_e}{2a}=\frac{4M^2+2a^2+a^2/E^2}{2a};
    \label{eq:Jplus-t}
\end{equation}
there is no retrograde $r_e$-crossing. Instead the retrograde $t$-capture is
bounded by the \emph{marginal} (double-root) condition $N=N'=0$ at a radius
$r_*>\re$---the massive analogue of the retrograde photon sphere---which closes
to
\begin{equation}
    J_c^{-}=-\,\frac{\bar P}{\bar v\,(\sqrt\Delta-b\bar v)}\bigg|_{r_*}<0 .
    \label{eq:Jc-minus}
\end{equation}
This retrograde saddle-node is the sharpest exact statement in the paper that
lies wholly inside the control domain, and we record it as such.

\begin{proposition}[Exterior retrograde separatrix; evidence level~(I) for the
factorisation and the domain inequalities, level~(II) for the quoted roots]
\label{prop:exterior-sep}
At $M=1$, $a=0.9$, $E=1.2$, $A=1$ the system $N=N'=0$ has the solution
\begin{equation}
    r_d=3.513905124011657\,M,\qquad J_c^{-}=-8.053516003877019 ,
    \label{eq:exterior-sep}
\end{equation}
a genuine double root of $N$, reproduced by the printed closed
form~\eqref{eq:Jc-minus} to $10^{-12}$. It is an \emph{exterior} degeneration:
$r_d>2M$, and at $r_d$ both control-domain inequalities
of~\eqref{eq:two-inequalities} hold strictly,
\begin{equation}
    g(W,W)\big|_{r_d}=-0.430833<0,
    \qquad \bar v^2\big|_{r_d}=0.700811>0 ,
\end{equation}
as they do on the whole approach arc $r\in[r_d,6M]$. It is therefore a
type-(E) object in the sense of Protocol~\ref{prot:status}: a controlled-rail
extremal, not an analytic continuation. Moreover $r_d>2M$ persists across the
spin range: $r_d/M=2.857675,\,3.204756,\,3.513905,\,3.579494$ at
$a=0.1,\,0.5,\,0.9,\,0.99$, so the exterior character is structural rather than
an accident of the representative parameters.
\end{proposition}

\begin{remark}
The same classification also produces a \emph{prograde} degeneration at
$r_d=1.512292\,M$, $J=2.936351$. For this spin that radius lies outside the seed
Kerr null surface $\rp=1.435890\,M$ but \emph{inside} $2M$, where
$W=\partial_\eta$ is spacelike. It is an algebraic degeneration of the
analytically continued frozen equations---type~(A)---and we do not call it a
physical control separatrix for this selector. Both values, and the
control-domain certificates above, are verified in
\texttt{verification/verify\_exterior\_separatrix.wls}.
\end{remark}

For $a=0.9$, $E=1.2$ (and $A=1$) equation~\eqref{eq:Jplus-t} gives
$J_+^{t}=3.43$, so with~\eqref{eq:exterior-sep} the $t$-branch crosses over the
wide, asymmetric band $J\in(J_c^{-},J_+^{t})$, far more crossing-prone on the

\subsection{Plunge inversion: rotational and conformal}
\label{sec:inversion}
Throughout this section the comparison is protocol (P1) of
section~\ref{sec:bvp}---same launch event, same initial data, free arrival---and
the ordering it produces is a statement about that protocol, not about the
extremals alone. Two mechanisms invert the $t/\tau$ ordering. Rotationally, in
the $(a,J)$ plane, $\rho(r_*)^2=H_{t0}/H_{\tau0}$, extending the stationary Kerr
analysis \cite{RosignoliKerr}. Conformally, the inversion locus is
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{cond}(r,A)=A^2 b\,Q-\bar P(\Ehat-A^2 f)=0,\quad
    Q=b\bar v^2+\sqrt{\bar v^2\Delta},
    \label{eq:condinv}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{cond}(1)<0$ and
$\mathrm{cond}(\Af)=\Ehat r\Delta(\Ehat-1)/(r-2M)$. Under the explicit hypotheses
$\Ehat>1$ (scattering energy), $r>2M$ and $\Delta>0$ (exterior, off-horizon), the
latter is $>0$, so by the intermediate value theorem a root
$A_{\rm inv}\in(1,\Af)$ exists; it is reachable when $A_{\rm inv}<\Af$, with far
field $A_{\rm inv}\to\sqrt{\Ehat}$. For $\Ehat\le1$ the sign is non-positive and no
such root is guaranteed: the conformal inversion is thus a \emph{conditional}
statement, holding in the stated energy/domain window rather than universally.

\subsection{Fixed-endpoint comparison and protocol dependence}
\label{sec:bvp}
The statement ``branch $X$ reaches deeper'' is protocol dependent, and this
section is the one place where the comparison is \emph{not} the default of
Protocol~\ref{prot:endpoint}. Two distinct protocols are in play and must not be
confused.

\emph{(P1) Same launch.} Fixed launch event and fixed initial data
$(r_0,J,p_r)$, free arrival: the $t$-branch reaches deeper,
$r_{\min}^t<r_{\min}^\tau$. This is the protocol of
section~\ref{sec:inversion}. Note that ``same $J$'' here means the same
\emph{numerical} costate; comparing the $\eta$- and $t$-branches on the same
spatial curve instead requires the relabelling $J_t=A\,J_\eta$
(Protocol~\ref{prot:endpoint}), so (P1) at a common numerical $J$ compares
different orbits by construction---which is precisely why the two protocols can
disagree.

\emph{(P2) Two fixed spatial endpoints.} Both branches join the same $A$ and $B$
and are distinct paths; the ordering reverses, $r_{\min}^\tau<r_{\min}^t$. The
optical-index ratio $n_t/n_\tau=E/f>1$ penalizes deep radii more heavily for the
$t$-branch, so the $t$-extremal bows outward while the $\tau$-branch dips.

Neither ordering is a property of the extremals alone; each is a property of the
comparison. Every depth statement below names its protocol, and
Table~\ref{tab:protocol} tabulates the resulting dependence. What is compared in
either case is a pair of \emph{selected controlled-rail extremals}: an ordering
between two selected extremals does not by itself show that either is the global
minimiser in its branch, and no such claim is made.

Crucially, neither driver of same-launch inversion survives the
fixed-endpoint constraint. Scanning the spin $a$ over the entire
Kerr flow with fixed symmetric endpoints $(r_0,\pm\Phi)$ leaves
$\Delta r=r_{\min}^t-r_{\min}^\tau$ strictly positive
($\Delta r\in[+0.45,+2.06]$ on $a\in[0.05,0.95]$); the map is nearly flat
in $a$, its gradient set almost entirely by the endpoint angle
$\Phi$ (encoded by $J$). The conformal scan (via the transfer
$E_{\rm eff}=\hat E/A$) is likewise everywhere positive
($\Delta r\in[+0.002,+3.711]$). The pointwise index bound $n_t/n_\tau=E/f>1$ does
not by itself order the minima of two distinct functionals---indeed at \emph{fixed}
$J$ the ordering is the opposite (the $t$-orbit turns deeper), and the fixed-endpoint
result is a reversal driven by the accumulated-angle map $J\mapsto\Phi$. It admits,
however, a clean reduction. Parametrize each branch by its turning radius $r_{\min}$
(which fixes $J$ through the turning condition $r\Delta=J^2 DE$ for $\tau$, $Q_2=0$
for $t$), and let
\begin{equation}
    \Phi_{\rm br}(r_{\min})=\int_{r_{\min}}^{r_0}\!F_{\rm br}\big(r;J_{\rm br}(r_{\min})\big)\,\dd r
\end{equation}
be the half-angle swept by the orbit turning at $r_{\min}$. Two lemmas---verified
across $a\in[0.3,0.9]$, $E\in[1.05,1.5]$---close the result. \emph{(A)} At matched
turning radius the $t$-integrand dominates \emph{pointwise},
$F_t(r;J_t)>F_\tau(r;J_\tau)$ for $r\in(r_{\min},r_0)$; since $J_{t,\tau}(r_{\min})$
are \emph{algebraic}, this is a polynomial (not transcendental) inequality, whence
$\Phi_t(r_{\min})>\Phi_\tau(r_{\min})$. \emph{(B)} $\Phi_{\rm br}$ is strictly
decreasing. Together: at equal angle
$\Phi_\tau(r_{\min}^\tau)=\Phi_t(r_{\min}^t)>\Phi_\tau(r_{\min}^t)$, so
$r_{\min}^\tau<r_{\min}^t$ by (B)---the fixed-endpoint no-inversion. Lemma~(A) is
\emph{proven in full}. Writing $F_t^2-F_\tau^2=(r\,DE/\Delta^2)\,N_G/(Q_2 P_\tau)$
with $N_G=(J_t(r-2M)+2Ma)^2 P_\tau-J_\tau^2(r-2M)Q_2$ and reducing modulo the turning
conditions ($J_\tau^2=r_{\min}\Delta_{\min}/DE_{\min}$, $Q_2(r_{\min},J_t)=0$), one
finds the key factorization
\begin{equation}
    N_G=(r-r_{\min})\,\frac{4r\,\Delta(r)}{r_{\min}-2M}\,W(r),\qquad W\ \text{\emph{linear} in }r .
\end{equation}
The prefactor is positive for $r>r_{\min}>2M$, so (A) reduces to $W(r)>0$; and a
linear $W$ with $W(r_{\min})>0$ and positive slope stays positive for all
$r>r_{\min}$. Both $W(r_{\min})=(r_{\min}-2M)\,a\,DE_{\min}(J^\ast-J_t)$ and the slope
$w_1=a\,DE_{\min}(J^{\ast\ast}-J_t)$ are positive because the prograde root $J_t$ of
$Q_2(r_{\min},\cdot)$ lies below both thresholds
$J^\ast=[E^2r_{\min}(a^2+r_{\min}^2)+2a^2]/(a\,DE_{\min})$,
$J^{\ast\ast}=[E^2r_{\min}^2(r_{\min}-M)+E^2a^2r_{\min}+a^2]/(a\,DE_{\min})$: the
quadratic opens downward ($A=-(r_{\min}-2M)DE_{\min}<0$) with negative vertex
$-2a/(r_{\min}-2M)$, while $J^\ast,J^{\ast\ast}>0$ and
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_2(r_{\min},J^\ast)&=\frac{-E^2r_{\min}^3(E^2r_{\min}^2+a^2)\Delta_{\min}}{a^2DE_{\min}},\\[2pt]
Q_2(r_{\min},J^{\ast\ast})&=\frac{-(r_{\min}-2M)(E^2r_{\min}^2+a^2)\big(E^2r_{\min}^2[(r_{\min}-M)^2+a^2]+a^2\big)}{a^2DE_{\min}}
\end{aligned}
\]
are both manifestly negative, placing the thresholds beyond the larger root
(\texttt{no\_inversion\_reduction.py}). Hence (A) is a theorem for all $r_{\min}>2M$,
$E>1$, $a>0$. Lemma~(B), the monotonicity $\Phi_\tau'(r_{\min})<0$, is
\emph{conditional}: $\Phi_\tau$ has a single maximum at a deflection radius
$r_{\rm pk}\approx3M$ (the strong-deflection/winding threshold, where the orbit
approaches the unstable circular $\tau$-orbit) and is strictly decreasing for
$r_{\min}>r_{\rm pk}$. In the effective-potential form
$\Phi_\tau=\sqrt{V_{\min}}\int_{r_{\min}}^{r_0}K/\sqrt{V-V_{\min}}\,\dd r$ with
$V=r\Delta/DE$, $K=\sqrt{r(r-2M)}/\Delta$, $V_{\min}=V(r_{\min})$, the substitution
$V=V_{\min}+\sigma^2$ and one integration by parts give, for $W\equiv K/V'$
($W>0$, $W'<0$),
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd\Phi_\tau}{\dd V_{\min}}=
    \frac{(V_0-2V_{\min})\,W(V_{\min})}{\Sigma\sqrt{V_{\min}}}
    +\frac{2}{\sqrt{V_{\min}}}\!\int_0^{\Sigma}\!W'(V_{\min}+\sigma^2)\,g(\sigma)\,\dd\sigma,
\end{equation}
$\Sigma=\sqrt{V_0-V_{\min}}$, where $g(\sigma)=(V_0-2V_{\min})\sigma/\Sigma+V_{\min}-\sigma^2$
satisfies $g(0)=V_{\min}>0$, $g(\Sigma)=0$ with its other root negative, hence
$g>0$ on $[0,\Sigma)$; the second term is therefore \emph{manifestly negative}.
When $V(r_0)\le2V(r_{\min})$ (the grazing regime, $r_{\min}$ near $r_0$) the first
term is $\le0$ as well, and $\Phi_\tau'<0$ is \emph{proven in closed form}. The same
closed form---using the exact identity (integration by parts on the Abel integral)
\begin{equation}
    \sqrt{V_{\min}}\,\Phi_\tau'=\frac{W(V_0)(V_0-2V_{\min})}{\Sigma}
    +\int_{V_{\min}}^{V_0}\!\frac{W'(t)\,(2V_{\min}-t)}{\sqrt{t-V_{\min}}}\,\dd t ,
    \label{eq:phitau-closed}
\end{equation}
together with the convexity of $W$ ($W''>0$)---extends the proof to the quarter
regime $V(r_0)\le4V(r_{\min})$. We accordingly \emph{define}
\begin{equation}
    R_\ast(E,a)=\sup\big\{\,r_0:\ V(r_0)\le 4\,V(r_{\min}(r_0))\ \text{along the marginal
    turning family}\,\big\},
    \label{eq:Rstar}
\end{equation}
with $V=r\Delta/DE$ the turning potential---an explicit $(E,a)$-dependent radius (it is
\emph{not} a function of $E$ alone, since $V$ carries the spin through $\Delta$)---so the
closed-form proof covers all endpoints $r_0\le R_\ast(E,a)$. In the
static limit $a\to0$ (Vaidya $\dot m=0$ / frozen Thakurta) we can go further:
$W(V)=\tfrac1{2V}-\tfrac1{2b^{3/2}V^{3/2}}+O(V^{-2})$ with $b=E^2-1$, so the
large-$r_0$ angle is
$\Phi_\tau=\arctan\!\sqrt{V_0/V_{\min}-1}-b^{-3/2}\sqrt{1/V_{\min}-1/V_0}+\cdots$,
exhibiting a \emph{single, non-degenerate} maximum at $V_{\rm pk}\sim b^{-3/2}\sqrt{V_0}$
with $\Phi_\tau''(V_{\rm pk})=-\tfrac12 b^{9/4}V_0^{-5/4}<0$; hence (B) holds for all
sufficiently large $r_0$. For intermediate $r_0$ the single-crossing property
(at every $r_{\min}$, \emph{either} $\Phi_\tau'<0$ \emph{or} $\Phi_\tau''<0$) is
established by a validated interval-arithmetic certificate, completed for $r_0=10M$
over the entire continuum $r_{\min}\in(2.6,6.05)M$ (the elementary grazing bound
covering $r_{\min}\gtrsim6.05M$; \texttt{no\_inversion\_schwarzschild\_CAP\_r0\_10.py}).
A uniform symbolic proof for all $(a,E,r_0)$ remains open---the deciding inequality is
tight at the \emph{transcendental} deflection threshold $r_{\rm pk}$. We therefore state
(B), and hence the fixed-endpoint no-inversion, as a \emph{theorem for $r_0\le R_\ast(E,a)$
and asymptotically as $r_0\to\infty$ in the static case}, machine-certified at
representative $r_0$, and a numerically supported conjecture in full generality
(verified over $a\in[0.2,0.9]$, $E\in[1.05,1.5]$); the fixed-endpoint turning points of
Figure~\ref{fig:bvp} ($r_{\min}^{\tau,t}\gtrsim4.7M$) lie in the certified scattering
regime.

\subsection{Adiabatic breathing of the conformal-Kerr indicatrix}
\label{sec:unified}
Combining Lemma~I.4 with the on-shell part of Theorem~I.5 of Paper~I yields the
indicatrix-breathing response: for a one-parameter family
of rail indicatrices with frozen integrand $F(r;\lambda)$ on the genus-two curve
$y^2=S(r;\lambda)$, a slow drift $\lambda=\lambda(\text{clock})$ gives, on-shell,
\begin{equation}
    \varphi(r)=\varphi_0(r;\lambda)+\dot\lambda\Big[\sum_{k<j} q_{kj}\,W_{kj}
    +(\text{elementary})\Big]+O(\dot\lambda^2),
    \label{eq:unified}
\end{equation}
with $q_{kj}$ \emph{algebraic} (Lemma~I.4, via the reduction $\partial_\lambda
F=N_\lambda/S^{3/2}$) and $W_{kj}=\int(U_j\dd U_k-U_k\dd U_j)$ length-two iterated Abelian
integrals of the frozen curve; the \emph{complete} first-order term adds the off-shell
(costate) piece of Theorem~I.5, itself of the same class (a $\sim2\%$ correction here). We
now specialize to $\lambda=A$, equivalently $E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$: every
$E_{\rm eff}$-dependent datum of the Thakurta--Kerr indicatrix breathes through
$\partial_A E_{\rm eff}=-\Ehat/A^2$---both the \emph{azimuthal} frame-dragging centre-wind
and the semi-axes. Decisively for the weight-two letter, the $t,\tau$ clocks grow
logarithmically at large $r$, giving Thakurta--Kerr a third-kind dipole \emph{at
infinity}; the ladder ``circle $\to$ radial-wind ellipse $\to$ azimuthal-wind ellipse'' of
Paper~I places conformal Kerr as the last (azimuthal) member on the same hyperelliptic
backbone.

\paragraph{Domain of validity.} The statement is a \emph{first-order} adiabatic
theorem, and it rests on two \emph{exact} lemmas---the algebraic reduction
$\partial_\lambda F=N_\lambda/S^{3/2}$ with the unique rational $c_k$ from the
polynomial identity~\eqref{eq:reduction-identity} (nonsingular whenever $S$ has
distinct roots, i.e.\ away from the separatrix), and the integration-by-parts
assembly~\eqref{eq:psi-split} using the shuffle relation---so the closed form is
rigorous, not fitted, wherever those hypotheses hold. Two caveats bound it honestly.
First, it is $O(\dot\lambda)$: the second-order term needs the next reduction.
Second, and more importantly, the expansion \emph{fails at the turning points}
$S(r)=0$: there $\partial_\lambda F\sim N_\lambda/S^{3/2}$ diverges and the na\"ive
$\dot\lambda\,\delta\varphi$ is not uniformly valid, so the closed forms hold on the
smooth arcs between turning points and must be joined across them by the standard WKB
connection formulae (matched asymptotics). On the separatrix the \emph{mathematics}
is a controlled limit---the genus drops and the reduction degenerates to the elliptic
(Bloch--Wigner) case---but the \emph{physics} there is delicate for a separate reason:
the radial period diverges (the orbit asymptotes an unstable circular orbit), so the
adiabatic hypothesis itself---``$\lambda$ varies slowly compared with the orbital
period''---degrades, and the separatrix correction is properly read as an
\emph{instantaneous, local} first-order statement about the neighbourhood of the
separatrix rather than a long-time orbit pinned to the moving locus. Moreover the degeneration
locus is not fixed under the evolution it responds to: under the conformal drift the
dilation ray $(\Ehat/A,J/A)$ leaves the marginal locus $J=\Jc(E_{\rm eff})$, so a
particle of conserved $J$ \emph{crosses} the moving separatrix as $A(\eta)$ runs---a
genuinely non-perturbative regime (a Neishtadt separatrix-crossing problem, appendix~C),
beyond first order. Within these bounds, which
are the usual bounds of first-order WKB, this closed form is the \emph{on-shell
component} of the first-order response---the parameter drift along the frozen
family---and is exact as such; the \emph{complete} first-order term is the
extended-Hamiltonian expression equation~\eqref{eq:adiab-exact}, which adds the
off-shell (costate $p_\eta$) displacement of the momentum shell (a $\sim2\%$
correction here, figure~\ref{fig:phi-true-dynamic}). The separatrix results in particular
quantify the response \emph{at} the instantaneous marginal configuration
$J=\Jc(E_{\rm eff})$, not a trajectory pinned to it. The complementary
adiabatic derivative along the degeneration family
(with the spherical Vaidya $J_{\rm deg}=m\,j(E)$ as the worked instance) is established
in Paper~I; here the same reduction applies verbatim with the conformal source
$N=\Ehat N_E+J N_J$ and $\dd\Jc/\dd E_{\rm eff}$ (\ref{app:tracking}).

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl-II}
\paragraph{Where the problem lives.} The first result is negative and delimiting, and it
governs everything else: for the selector $W=\partial_\eta$ one has
$g(W,W)=-A^2(1-2M/r)$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:compact} the admissible-velocity set is
compact only for $r>2M$ and the controlled-rail problem \emph{ends} at the conformal
stationary limit. Every curve in this paper is accordingly an exterior extremal, a
limiting contact, or an explicitly labelled analytic continuation carrying no optimality
claim (Protocol~\ref{prot:status}).

\paragraph{Proved, in the exterior domain.} The flagship exact result is the
\emph{exterior} retrograde separatrix of Proposition~\ref{prop:exterior-sep}: a genuine
double root of the $t$-branch turning function at
$r_d=3.513905\,M$, $J_c^{-}=-8.053516$ for the representative parameters, lying strictly
inside the control domain (both regularity inequalities hold on the whole approach arc)
and remaining exterior across the spin range $a\in[0.1,0.99]$. Alongside it: closed-form
equatorial separatrices in Weierstrass functions; and the complete local classification at
the stationary limit (Proposition~\ref{prop:classification}), whose content is that the two
marginal costates $\pm\Jc$ are \emph{dynamically inequivalent} even though the shape
radical is even in $J$---the symmetry being broken by $\tilde p_\varphi(\re)=J-\Jc$, with
the retrograde value approaching $\re$ only asymptotically and the prograde value crossing
at a finite rate. The on-shell first-order response reduces to length-two iterated Abelian
integrals on the genus-two curve, a proved closed form of \emph{weight two} built on the
weight-one Abelian primitives (their reduction to weight one conjectural). The arrival
branch is $t\equiv\eta$ (a monotone reparametrization), leaving two genuinely distinct
extremal families, arrival and proper time $\tau$.

\paragraph{Conditional and numerical, kept tiered.} Rotation and the conformal factor
invert the $t/\tau$ ordering under a same-launch comparison. The fixed-endpoint
no-inversion result is deliberately \emph{not} stated as a single claim: it is
(i) an analytic integrand inequality; (ii) a \emph{theorem} for
$r_0\le R_\ast(E,a)$ [equation~\eqref{eq:Rstar}]; (iii) an asymptotic result as
$r_0\to\infty$ in the static case; (iv) a computer-assisted proposition certified at
representative $r_0$ over a stated grid; and (v) a numerically supported \emph{conjecture}
in full generality. These five have different evidential status
(Protocol~\ref{prot:evidence}) and are not merged. The complete first-order (on-shell
$+$ off-shell) correction is verified against the true non-autonomous flow to
$O(\varepsilon^2)$ on compact regular subarcs; the weight-two letters are evaluated by a
convergent theta-nome series, their irreducibility and single-valued completion
conjectural.

\paragraph{Outlook.} Open threads: a general theory of moving turning points and of
adiabatic separatrices (a genuine crossing of a moving separatrix is a Neishtadt inner
problem, beyond first order); a rigorous higher-genus polylogarithm framework with a
canonical basis; and---a genuinely different problem rather than an unfinished part of
this one---a control formulation valid inside $r=2M$, which would require a selector that
is timelike there and would carry its own rail charge, indicatrix, cost and Hamiltonian
(section~\ref{sec:domain}). The off-equatorial
(three-dimensional) quasi-constant dynamics is deferred to a dedicated study.
Taken with the companion paper, these results show how a single controlled-rail
variational principle organizes time-optimal transport across non-stationary spacetimes,
from spherical mass flow to rotating frame dragging, and how the resulting extremals
encode that geometry in explicit hyperelliptic closed forms.

\section{Validation and consistency checks}
\label{app:validation}
Results in this paper rest on evidence of four different kinds, which are not
interchangeable. We separate them explicitly, because conflating them is the
commonest way for a computer-assisted result to overclaim.

\begin{protocol}[Levels of evidence]
\label{prot:evidence}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\rm(I)] \emph{Exact symbolic identity.} A polynomial or rational identity
returning an exact zero, in two independent computer-algebra systems, with no
tolerance involved. This is a proof. Examples: $p_r^2=\mathcal S/(D_E^2\Delta^2)$;
the factorisation~\eqref{eq:marginal-factor}; the reduction determinant as a
resultant.
\item[\rm(II)] \emph{Independent numerical agreement.} A quantity computed by
two genuinely different routes---Hamiltonian ODE flow against an evaluated
closed form, say---agreeing to a stated precision. This excludes transcription
and bookkeeping error. It is \emph{not} a proof, and we never cite it as one.
\item[\rm(III)] \emph{Recovery of established limits.} The formalism must
reproduce results validated elsewhere: $a\to0$ gives Schwarzschild, $A\to1$ the
stationary Kerr constants of Carter and Chandrasekhar
\cite{Carter1968,Chandrasekhar}, $\Ehat\to\infty$ the Fermat--Randers metric,
and the stationary rail reproduces Perlick's fixed-energy optical metric
\cite{Perlick1991} exactly (Paper~I). These are falsifiable external constraints
and are the closest analogue available, in a problem of this kind, to an
independent experimental check.
\item[\rm(IV)] \emph{Computer-assisted proposition.} A statement established by
interval arithmetic or an equivalent certificate over an explicitly stated grid
and window. It is a proof conditional on the arithmetic; its domain is always
quoted with it.
\end{enumerate}
Each numbered claim below is tagged with the level it rests on.
\end{protocol}

\paragraph{One command, one dataset.} Every number in the adiabatic validation---the
figure, the fitted slopes, their uncertainties, the raw convergence data and the file
checksums---is emitted by the single archived command
\begin{center}
\texttt{python3 paper2/provenance/make\_provenance.py}
\end{center}
which runs each validation script as a subprocess, records the $\varepsilon$-window
actually used, refits on sub-windows, hashes every script involved, and writes
\texttt{adiabatic\_slopes.json} (machine-readable), \texttt{adiabatic\_slopes.tex} (the
macros this manuscript uses for those numbers) and \texttt{MANIFEST.tsv}, which maps each
equation, figure and table to one command and one checksum. The manuscript
\texttt{\textbackslash input}s the generated fragment, so the printed values and hashes
cannot drift from the archive. This replaces the hand-transcribed values of the previous
version, which had done exactly that.

The $\varepsilon$-window is \provEpsWindow. The fit is taken on \provFitInterval, which
remains \provLocusSep\ from the nearest excluded locus, so the fitted interval is
uniformly away from turning points, the freezing surface and the conformal stationary
limit, as the local statement of the first-order theorem requires.

\begin{remark}
No level of \emph{experimental} validation is available, and we claim none.
Thakurta--Kerr is a conformal deformation of Kerr whose central object is a
compact object rather than a black hole, and the controlled rail is an actively
forced worldline, not a geodesic: it is not the trajectory of any freely falling
body that is observed. Level~(III) is what carries the external-consistency
burden here.
\end{remark}

Perturbing a computed extremal and observing that the arrival clock increases is
a level-(II) consistency check on the numerics. It does \emph{not} establish
minimality, which would require a second-variation or conjugate-point argument,
or a saturated Hamilton--Jacobi certificate over the same admissible class; a
finite family of perturbations cannot certify a minimum in an
infinite-dimensional class. We accordingly report such perturbations as
consistency checks only (the spherical branches are treated in Paper~I).
Stationary-limit residuals confirm
that the non-stationary Hamiltonian flow reproduces the closed-form Kerr expressions
as $A\to1$; the conformal-Killing rail charge $\Ehat$ is conserved to $\sim10^{-16}$ along the rail. The equatorial closed
forms are validated end-to-end by the three-method superposition
(ODE flow, quadrature, evaluated Weierstrass) through the ergosphere
(figure~\ref{fig:sep3}), and the fixed-endpoint no-inversion theorem is
checked over the asymmetric-endpoint grids (figures~\ref{fig:asym-spin}--%
\ref{fig:asym-conf}).

\subsection{Explicit closed forms}
\label{app:explicit}

All expressions below are at \emph{constant (frozen) conformal factor} $A$:
stationary Kerr with $E\to E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$, $J\to J/A$ (spin $a$ retained). They give
the instantaneous orbit shape on a Thakurta--Kerr slice; the time dependence
enters only through $E_{\rm eff}(\eta)$. The off-equatorial extension is left to future work.

\paragraph{Curve conventions (fixed once for all letters).} Every letter
below---the second-kind $U_k=\int r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r$, the weight-two
$W_{jk}=\int(r^jU_k-r^kU_j)/\sqrt S\,\dd r$, and the third-kind letters $L,\mathcal
D_k$---is an integral on the hyperelliptic curve $Y^2=\mathcal S(r)$ read with the
following fixed data. \emph{(i) Sheet:} $\sqrt S>0$ on the physical outer arc
$r>r_{\min}$, the sign flipping across each branch point, which fixes $Y$ globally by
analytic continuation. \emph{(ii) Cuts:} along the real segments between consecutive
real roots of $\mathcal S$, so the path stays on the physical sheet between turning
points. \emph{(iii) Base point and orientation:} all integrals share the lower limit
$r_0$ (the launch radius) and run with increasing $r$ toward arrival; reversing the
orientation flips the overall sign. \emph{(iv) Additive constants:} each
antiderivative is pinned at a regular reference---$U_k$ at $r_0$, the third-kind
letters at the regular point $z_\infty$ (where $\mathcal S$ vanishes as $1/r$), giving
the constants $C_{U_0},C_0$ used below---so that no spurious constant survives; these
constants multiply the common holomorphic differential and \emph{cancel} in every
assembled $\delta\varphi$. The same conventions apply verbatim to the separatrix
degeneration, with $\mathcal S\to Q_4$ and the Kleinian $\sigma,\zeta$ replaced by the
Weierstrass $\sigma,\zeta,\wp$.

\paragraph{Equatorial $\tau$-branch separatrix in Weierstrass functions.} On the
$\tau$-separatrix $J=\Jc$ the doranTau factorization $\Delta-\Jc^2 w=f(r^2+c^2)$,
$c=a/E$, makes the
turning quartic $Q_4=r\big((E^2-1)r+2M\big)(r^2+c^2)$, and the factor $r^2+c^2$
divides $Q_4$ so that only the horizon poles survive. Writing
\begin{gather}
    A=\frac{Ma^2}{2E^2},\qquad B=\frac{a^2(E^2-1)}{12E^2},\notag\\
    g_2=\frac{a^4(E^2-1)^2}{12E^4}-\frac{a^2M^2}{E^2},\notag\\
    g_3=\frac{a^4\big[36E^2M^2(1-E^2)-a^2(E^2-1)^3\big]}{216E^6},
    \label{eq:wp-invariants}
\end{gather}
the uniformizing variable is $z(r)=\wp^{-1}\!\big(A/r+B;g_2,g_3\big)
=\int_0^r\dd r'/\sqrt{Q_4}$, the Abel map of the turning quartic $Q_4$: it is the
elliptic argument in which the radial motion linearizes, so that $r$ is recovered
by $A/r+B=\wp(z)$ and the physical range $r\in[r_{\min},\infty)$ maps to a
segment of $z$. With $r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$ the horizons,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \alpha_\pm&=\frac{r_\pm\big((E^2-1)r_\pm+2M\big)}{r_\pm-r_\mp},\\
    \lambda_\pm&=\frac{\alpha_\pm}{\sqrt{Q_4(r_\pm)}},\qquad v_\pm=z(r_\pm),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
$\wp(v_\pm)=A/r_\pm+B$, and
$\Lambda_0=(E^2-1)-\alpha_+/r_+-\alpha_-/r_-+2\lambda_+\zeta(v_+)+2\lambda_-\zeta(v_-)$,
the angle is linear in the uniformizing variable $z\equiv z(r)$ plus two
Weierstrass-$\sigma$ ratios,
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \varphi(r)=\frac{a}{E}\Big[&\Lambda_0\,z(r)
      +\lambda_+\ln\frac{\sigma(z-v_+)}{\sigma(z+v_+)}\\
      &+\lambda_-\ln\frac{\sigma(z-v_-)}{\sigma(z+v_-)}\Big]+\text{const}.
\end{split}
    \label{eq:sep-phi}
\end{equation}
Because $Q_4(2M)=4M^2(4E^2M^2+a^2)>0$, equation~\eqref{eq:sep-phi} is regular
\emph{through} the ergosphere $\re=2M$; the only singularities are the
third-kind logarithms at the horizons. The conformal case is
$E\to E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$ in every parameter.

\begin{remark}[the Finsler condition marks the same boundary]
\label{rmk:finsler-domain}
There is a fourth, and entirely independent, derivation of the control-domain
boundary, and it comes from the Randers side. Gibbons, Herdeiro, Warnick and Werner
write a conformally stationary metric in threading form and read off Randers data
$a_{ij}=V^{-2}\gamma_{ij}$, $b_i=-\omega_i$, noting that
$F=\sqrt{a_{ij}v^iv^j}+b_iv^i$ is a genuine Finsler norm---positive, with positive
definite fundamental tensor---precisely under the \emph{Finsler condition}
$|b|^2_a=a^{ij}b_ib_j<1$, which they observe ``breaks down at an ergo-surface''
\cite[\S2.1, \S3.4]{GibbonsRanders2009}. For the Kerr seed this is explicit: in the
equatorial plane
\begin{equation}
    |b|^2_a=\frac{4a^2M^2}{r^2\,(r^2-2Mr+a^2)} ,
    \label{eq:finsler-condition}
\end{equation}
whose numerator and denominator make $1-|b|^2_a$ vanish at $r=2M$ exactly, and at
every latitude $1-|b|^2_a$ shares its zero set with $g_{tt}$. Two features then
combine. First, the Randers data are \emph{conformal invariants}: under
$g\mapsto A^2g$ both $V^2$ and $\gamma_{ij}$ carry the same factor, so $a_{ij}$ and
$b_i$ are unchanged. Second, therefore, Thakurta--Kerr carries exactly the Randers
structure of its seed, and the Finsler condition fails on the \emph{seed}
ergosurface---equatorially $r_e=2M$---independently of $A(\eta)$. That is the
boundary of section~\ref{sec:domain}, obtained without reference to the selector at
all, and it explains structurally why the conformal factor does not move it.
Equation~\eqref{eq:finsler-condition} and the conformal invariance are verified in
\texttt{verification/verify\_randers\_finsler.wls}.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Doran continuation through the horizon.} The shift used here is not an
ansatz: it is Doran's own azimuthal transformation composed with the
Boyer--Lindquist-to-Eddington step. Doran's \cite[Eq.~(21)]{Doran2000} carries
advanced Eddington--Finkelstein to his chart by
$\dd\varphi=\dd\bar\varphi-a\,\dd r/[r^2+a^2+\sqrt{2Mr(r^2+a^2)}]$, while
$\dd\bar\varphi=\dd\varphi_{\rm BL}+a\,\dd r/\Delta$. Using
$r^2+a^2-\Delta=2Mr$ the two combine to
\begin{equation}
    \frac{a}{\Delta}-\frac{a}{r^2+a^2+\sqrt{2Mr(r^2+a^2)}}
      =\frac{a}{\Delta}\sqrt{\frac{2Mr}{r^2+a^2}} ,
    \label{eq:doran-shift}
\end{equation}
which is the $\varphi_{\rm shift}$ used below---an exact identity, not an
approximation.

\begin{remark}
It is worth noting \emph{why} this chart cannot rescue the control problem below
$r=2M$, since the temptation is natural. Doran's construction is built on
\emph{free-falling} observers: his time coordinate records the proper time of
observers falling from rest at infinity along trajectories of constant
$\theta,\varphi$ \cite{Doran2000}. The vector field it privileges is therefore
the free-fall four-velocity, not our selector $\partial_\eta$, and changing chart
leaves the scalar $g(\partial_\eta,\partial_\eta)$ untouched. The chart is
horizon-penetrating and the formulae are regular across $\Delta=0$; the control
domain is unaffected (section~\ref{sec:domain}). The same point has a published
form on the Randers side: Gibbons \emph{et al.} exhibit two apparently different
Zermelo structures for Schwarzschild---a radial wind $\mathbf W=-\sqrt{2M/r}\,
\partial_r$ on flat space in Painlev\'e--Gullstrand coordinates, and no wind at all
in Schwarzschild coordinates---and observe that they differ by a \emph{gauge
transformation} of $b_i$, obvious in the Randers picture and not in the Zermelo one
\cite[Eqs.~(32)--(34)]{GibbonsRanders2009}. Doran's chart is the Kerr analogue of
that move, and it changes $b_i$ by a gauge, not the trajectories.
\end{remark}

The Boyer--Lindquist form
$\varphi(r)$ winds logarithmically at $\rp$ (the standard BL artifact); adding the
Doran shift $\dd\varphi_{\rm shift}=2Mar\,\dd r/(\Delta\sqrt{C_3})$ with the cubic
$C_3=2Mr(r^2+a^2)$ regularizes it. This is a \emph{second} elliptic curve, in
clean Weierstrass form $r(z)=(2/M)\wp(z;g_2,g_3)$ with $g_2=-M^2a^2$, $g_3=0$
(quasi-lemniscatic), giving
$\varphi_{\rm shift}=\tfrac{a}{r_+-r_-}\big(B_+-B_-\big)$,
$B_\pm=2\zeta(v_\pm)z+\ln[\sigma(z-v_\pm)/\sigma(z+v_\pm)]$. With
$\dd\varphi_{\rm shift}$ defined as above (a positive residue at $\rp$), the
log residues of $\varphi_{\rm BL}$ and $\varphi_{\rm shift}$ are \emph{equal}
(numerically $1.0324=1.0324$ at $a=0.9,E=1.2,J=3$), so they cancel under
\emph{subtraction}: the horizon-regular Doran azimuth is
\begin{equation}
    \varphi_D=\varphi_{\rm BL}-\varphi_{\rm shift},
    \label{eq:doran-phi}
\end{equation}
closed on two elliptic curves. (The single consistent sign is fixed by this
residue cancellation; the same convention is used throughout,
Equation~\eqref{eq:doran-phi}.)

\paragraph{General $\tau$-branch $\varphi(r)$: genus-2 Kleinian form.} Off the
separatrix, for \emph{arbitrary} $J$, the $\tau$ integrand
$\dd\varphi/\dd r=\mathcal K_\tau(r)/\sqrt{\mathcal S(r)}$ is genus~2, with the
sextic
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal S(r)=r(r-2M)\big[(E^2-1)r+2M\big]
      \big[r\Delta-J^2\big((E^2-1)r+2M\big)\big]
    \label{eq:tau-sextic}
\end{equation}
(six generically simple branch points, hyperelliptic) and the rational numerator
$\mathcal K_\tau=Jr(r-2M)\big[(E^2-1)r+2M\big]/\Delta$. Partial fractions split it
into a first-kind (holomorphic) part and third-kind poles at the horizons
$r_\pm=M\pm\sqrt{M^2-a^2}$,
\begin{equation}
    \varphi(r)=c_1\!\int\!\frac{r\,\dd r}{\sqrt{\mathcal S}}
      +c_0\!\int\!\frac{\dd r}{\sqrt{\mathcal S}}
      +\sum_{\pm}\alpha_\pm\!\int\!\frac{\dd r}{(r-r_\pm)\sqrt{\mathcal S}},
    \label{eq:tau-genus2}
\end{equation}
with closed-form coefficients (in $M,a,E,J$)
\begin{equation}
    c_1=J(E^2-1),\quad c_0=2MJ,\quad
    \alpha_\pm=\mp\frac{Ja^2\big[M(E^2+1)\pm(E^2-1)\sqrt{M^2-a^2}\big]}
      {2\sqrt{M^2-a^2}}.
    \label{eq:tau-coeffs}
\end{equation}
The two holomorphic integrals are the Abel-map coordinates (arguments of the
Kleinian hyperelliptic $\sigma$), the horizon terms the third-kind
$\ln\sigma$-quotients---the exact genus-2 analogue of the $t$-branch $J_+^t$
[equation~\eqref{eq:t-genus2}]. We built $Y^2=\mathcal S$ in SageMath at $J=-0.9\Jc$
and computed its Riemann period matrix $\tau$; both Riemann relations hold
($\tau=\tau^\top$ to $7\times10^{-12}$, $\operatorname{Im}\tau\succ0$), and
Equation~\eqref{eq:tau-genus2} reproduces the direct integral to $10^{-15}$. At
$|J|=\Jc$ the factor $f^2$ (from $\Delta-\Jc^2w=f(r^2+c^2)$) makes $r_e$ a
\emph{double} root of $\mathcal S$; the $(r-2M)$ in $\mathcal K_\tau$ cancels one
factor, dropping the radical to the quartic $Q_4$---genus~1, the Weierstrass
separatrix~\eqref{eq:sep-phi}. This double root is also why the retrograde
marginal $J=-\Jc$ has a \emph{finite} one-sided tangent~\eqref{eq:corner-slope}
rather than the divergent slope of a smooth grazing or the vanishing slope of a
cusp. It does not, however, reflect: by
Proposition~\ref{prop:classification}(iii) the double root also makes $\re$ an
asymptotic endpoint of the radial flow, approached at the linear
rate~\eqref{eq:retro-rate} and therefore never attained in finite parameter. Only
the prograde $+\Jc$ threads through, and it does so because
$\tilde p_\varphi(\re)=J-\Jc$ vanishes there, degenerating the approach rate
$\mathcal T$ simultaneously with $p_r$ and resolving the resulting $0/0$ to the
finite ingoing value~\eqref{eq:pro-rate}.

\paragraph{$t$-branch closed form: generic integrand.} The coordinate-time
brachistochrone has $\dd\varphi/\dd r=\mathcal K(r)/\sqrt{\mathcal R(r)}$ with the
\emph{sextic} radical $\mathcal R=r\,Q_2(r)\,[(E^2-1)r+2M]$, genus~2, the quartic
being (no cubic term)
\begin{equation}
    Q_2(r)=E^2r^4+\big[E^2(a^2-J^2)+J^2\big]r^2
      +2M(J-a)\big[E^2(J-a)-2J\big]r+4M^2(J-a)^2,
    \label{eq:t-Q2}
\end{equation}
and a \emph{closed-form} numerator in the problem quantities,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal K(r)=\frac{r\,[(E^2-1)r+2M]\,\big(J(r-2M)+2Ma\big)}{\Delta(r)},
    \qquad \Delta=r^2-2Mr+a^2,
    \label{eq:t-K}
\end{equation}
holding for \emph{any} $(M,a,E,J)$: the third-kind poles sit at the two horizons
$r_\pm$ ($\Delta=0$) and the polynomial part carries the first-/second-kind
pieces (there is \emph{no} pole at $r=0$, cancelled by the leading factor $r$).
By the conformal transfer $n^2_{\rm TK}=n^2_{\rm Kerr}(E_{\rm eff})$
(section~\ref{sec:thakurta}), equation~\eqref{eq:t-K} is also the Thakurta--Kerr
$t$-integrand under $E\to E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$, $J\to J/A$, so the conformal
factor is \emph{not} a separate variable of $\varphi(r)$.

\paragraph{$t$-branch separatrix in Weierstrass functions.} At the saddle-node
$J_c^-$ [equation~\eqref{eq:Jc-minus}] the radical acquires a
\emph{double root} at the marginal-capture radius $r_*$,
$\mathcal R=(r-r_*)^2 Q_4(r)$, and
$\dd\varphi/\dd r=\mathcal G(r)/\sqrt{Q_4}$ becomes a third-kind elliptic
differential on the quartic $Q_4$ (which retains the root $r=0$). With the same
uniformization $z(r)=\wp^{-1}(A/r+B;g_2,g_3)$ (invariants of $Q_4$) the closed
form is
\begin{equation}
    \varphi(r)=\Lambda_0\,z(r)+\sum_{k}\lambda_k\,
      \ln\frac{\sigma(z-v_k)}{\sigma(z+v_k)},
    \label{eq:t-sep-phi}
\end{equation}
now with third-kind poles only at $c_k\in\{\rp,\rm,r_*\}$---three real ones, with
\emph{no} pole at $r=0$ and no complex pair, unlike the $\tau$-separatrix. Here
$v_k=\wp^{-1}(A/c_k+B)$, $\lambda_k=-A\alpha_k/(c_k^2\wp'(v_k))$, and
$\Lambda_0=\Lambda_{\rm poly}-\sum_k\alpha_k/c_k+\sum_k 2\zeta(v_k)\lambda_k$,
with $\alpha_k$ the residues of $\mathcal G$. For $M=1$, $a=0.9$, $E=1.2$
($r_*=3.514$, $J_c^-=-8.05$) equation~\eqref{eq:t-sep-phi} reproduces the Hamiltonian
flow to $2\times10^{-14}$ (figure~\ref{fig:t-sep}). So both separatrices are
genus~1 by different mechanisms; only non-separatrix $t$-orbits need genus-2
Kleinian functions.

\paragraph{Genus-2 Kleinian form and ergosphere penetration.} Away from the
separatrices the sextic $\mathcal R$ keeps six \emph{simple} branch points
(here $\{-4.545,\,-1.722\pm1.793i,\,0,\,1.444,\,2.000\}$), so the curve
$y^2=\mathcal R(r)$ is a genuine genus-2 hyperelliptic Riemann surface and
$\varphi(r)$ closes only in \emph{Kleinian} (hyperelliptic $\sigma$) functions.
With the numerator~\eqref{eq:t-K} the partial-fraction split
$\mathcal K=c_1 r+c_0+\alpha_+/(r-\rp)+\alpha_-/(r-\rm)$ gives
\begin{equation}
    \varphi(r)=\underbrace{c_1\!\!\int\!\frac{r\,\dd r}{\sqrt{\mathcal R}}
      +c_0\!\!\int\!\frac{\dd r}{\sqrt{\mathcal R}}}_{\text{first kind}}
      +\underbrace{\sum_{\pm}\alpha_\pm\!\!\int\!
      \frac{\dd r}{(r-r_\pm)\sqrt{\mathcal R}}}_{\text{third kind}},
    \label{eq:t-genus2}
\end{equation}
the holomorphic pair being the Abel-map coordinates (arguments of the Kleinian
$\sigma$) and the horizon terms the third-kind $\ln\sigma$-quotients---the exact
genus-2 analogue of equation~\eqref{eq:t-sep-phi}. For $M=1$, $a=0.9$, $E=1.2$ we
built $y^2=\mathcal R$ in SageMath and computed its Riemann period matrix
$\tau$; both Riemann relations hold ($\tau=\tau^\top$ to $4\times10^{-12}$,
$\operatorname{Im}\tau\succ0$ with eigenvalues $\{0.61,1.08\}$), certifying the
homology basis. The single Abelian integral $\varphi(r)$ does not invert to a
one-$\theta$ quotient $r(\varphi)$ (that needs the 2D Jacobi inversion,
$r=\wp_{22}$ along the trajectory line in the Jacobian, à la
Hackmann--L\"ammerzahl); but equation~\eqref{eq:t-genus2} reproduces the Hamiltonian
flow to $2\times10^{-13}$. Physically these genus-2 orbits are exactly the ones
that \emph{penetrate the ergosphere}: the prograde threshold $J_+^t$
[equation~\eqref{eq:Jplus-t}] has its turning point \emph{on} $r_e=2M$ (simple root at
$r=2$), while for $J$ just inside the capture window $[J_c^-,J_+^t]$ the turning
point moves \emph{inside} the ergoregion and the closed form stays accurate
across and below $r_e$ (figure~\ref{fig:jpt-g2}). This penetration is generic in a
sharp sense that distinguishes the two branches (Table~\ref{tab:penetration}). The
$t$-branch obeys a \emph{dichotomy}: it penetrates over the whole finite interval
$J\in(J_c^-,J_+^t)=(-8.05,3.43)$---every $J$ inside crosses, every $J$ outside
reflects at a smooth periapsis $r_{\min}>r_e$, with \emph{no} cusp---because the
frame-dragging term auto-tunes the effective momentum
$\mathcal K(r_e)=fJ_t/E+2Ma/(rE)\to a/E$ to the critical value for \emph{all}
$J_t$, so a whole family (retrograde included) is carried across. The
$\tau$-branch obeys the four-case law of
Proposition~\ref{prop:classification}: its proper-time cost carries the reality
wall $\sqrt{wf}$ ($w=\Ehat^2-f$), imaginary for $f<0$ inside $r_e$, which
confines $\tau$-extremals to $r\ge r_e$ \emph{except} at $|J|=a/E$, where the
exact factorisation~\eqref{eq:marginal-factor} makes $\mathcal S=r^4f^2w(r^2+\Jc^2)$
a perfect square times a positive factor and removes the wall---for \emph{both}
signs. Reality is therefore not what selects the prograde value. The selection is
dynamical: the approach rate $\mathcal T(\re)$ of~\eqref{eq:T-at-re} degenerates
simultaneously with $p_r$ only at $J=+a/E$, so that value alone reaches $\re$ in
finite parameter and crosses [equation~\eqref{eq:pro-rate}], while the retrograde
marginal $J=-a/E$ approaches $\re$ at the linear rate~\eqref{eq:retro-rate} and
takes infinite parameter to arrive. Off the marginal values the $\tau$-extremal
reflects: smoothly for $|J|>a/E$ (periapsis outside $r_e$), and with a semicubical
\emph{cusp} at $r_e$ for $|J|<a/E$. The $\tau$ crossing set is thus a single
point, the $t$ one a finite interval: at coordinate time crossing the stationary
limit is the rule, at proper time the fine-tuned exception. Both retrograde
marginals are asymptotic endpoints, but of different kinds: at $J_c^-$ the
$t$-branch has a double root at $r_*=3.514>r_e$ where $\mathcal K$ does \emph{not}
vanish, so $\dd\varphi/\dd r$ diverges and the winding is infinite, whereas at
$-a/E$ the $\tau$-branch has $\mathcal K_\tau$ vanishing linearly too, so the
tangent stays finite~\eqref{eq:corner-slope} and the total swept angle converges.

\paragraph{Stationary-limit conserved quantities.} At $A=1$ the branch flows admit
the closed first integrals used for validation,
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal K_\tau=J,\qquad
    \mathcal K_t=\frac{fJ+2Ma/r}{E},\qquad
    \mathcal K_\eta=\frac{fJ}{E},
    \label{eq:kerr-conserved}
\end{equation}
the equatorial Kerr doranTau/doranT constants.

\paragraph{Rotational inversion function.} The same-launch $t/\tau$ ordering
inverts where $\rho(r_*)^2=H_{t0}/H_{\tau0}$ with the explicit ratio of
centrifugal potentials
\begin{equation}
    \rho=\frac{A^2}{\Ehat}\Big(f+\frac{2Ma}{rJ}\Big),
    \label{eq:rho-explicit}
\end{equation}
$\rho<1$ giving $t$ deeper, $\rho>1$ giving $\tau$ deeper.

\paragraph{Conformal form of the classification.} With constant $A$ the separatrix momentum is
$\Jc=sA^2/\Ehat$, and above it the smooth periapsis is the outer root of
\begin{equation}
    \Delta(r)-\Big(\tfrac{J}{A}\Big)^2\Big[\big(\tfrac{\Ehat}{A}\big)^2-f(r)\Big]=0,
    \label{eq:periapsis-root}
\end{equation}
while $|J|<\Jc$ reflects at a cusp $r_{\min}=\re$; the retrograde marginal
$-\Jc$ approaches $\re$ asymptotically with the finite
tangent~\eqref{eq:corner-slope} and never attains it, and only the prograde
$+\Jc$ reaches $\re$ in finite parameter and crosses towards $\rp$, the
continuation below $2M$ being of type~(A) in Protocol~\ref{prot:status}.

\subsection{Reproducing the reductions}
\label{app:reproduce}
By Lemma~I.4 of Paper~I, every coefficient in the adiabatic corrections is fixed by the
single degree-ten polynomial identity
\begin{equation}
    2\,\mathcal{A}'(r)\,S(r)-\mathcal{A}(r)\,S'(r)
    +2\,S(r)\sum_{k=0}^4 c_k\,r^k \;=\; 2\,N_\lambda(r),
    \qquad \mathcal{A}=\sum_{i=0}^5 a_i r^i,
    \label{eq:reduction-identity}
\end{equation}
whose $11\times11$ linear system returns the $c_k$ \emph{rationally} in the metric
parameters (nothing is fitted); we do not repeat that general reduction here. For the
Thakurta--Kerr radicals $S$ is the branch radical ($S$ on the $\tau$-branch,
$R_6=r\,Q_2\,DE$ on the $t$-branch). The
numerator is $N_\lambda=EJ\,r^4(r-2M)^2 DE$ for the $\tau$-branch energy reduction
(the horizon factor $\Delta$ cancels), $N_\lambda=R_6\,\partial_E\mathcal K
-\tfrac12\mathcal K\,\partial_E R_6$ for the $t$-branch (with $Q_2$, $\mathcal K$ of
Equations~\eqref{eq:t-Q2},~\eqref{eq:t-K}, the $\Delta$ in $\mathcal K$ again cancelling to
leave a degree-seven polynomial). The $t$-branch clock is entirely on-curve
[equation~\eqref{eq:clock-t}]: its second-kind vector $b$ feeds the $W_{kj}$ of $R_6$ and
its horizon part $R_\Delta/\Delta$ the third-kind $\ln\sigma$-quotients, with no
double cover. Three reference solutions to check against (polynomial residual $0$
exactly):

\section{Explicit adiabatic forms on the genus-degeneration loci}
\label{app:separatrix}
This appendix collects in one place the fully explicit first-order adiabatic
brachistochrone on the loci where the branch sextic acquires a double root and drops to
genus one. We treat the rotating Thakurta--Kerr $\tau$ and $t$ branches, whose degeneration
values $\Jc^\pm$ include the \emph{physical} external separatrix (accessible double root
$r_d>2M$) alongside further algebraic $J_{\rm deg}$ roots at $r_d<0$. The shared
genus-two$\,\to\,$genus-one machinery is recalled below on a representative locus (with
coefficients closed in the Weierstrass $\sigma,\zeta,\wp$); the spherical Vaidya
$J_{\rm deg}$ case ($r_d<0$, a purely algebraic degeneration) is in Paper~I. All objects
live on the elliptic curve $E:\,w^2=Q_4(r)$ into which the branch sextic collapses; the
adiabatic expansion is $\varphi(r)=\varphi_0(r)+\dot\lambda\,\delta\varphi(r)+
O(\dot\lambda^2)$, $\lambda=A$ via $E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$.

\paragraph{Curve and special functions.}
$\Jc$ denotes the branch degeneration value (the explicit rotating $\tau$- and
$t$-branch values are collected below); here $E\equiv E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$ and
$S=(r-r_d)^2Q_4(r)$ with double root $r_d$ and quartic $Q_4$ (roots
$e_1<e_2<e_3=2M<e_4$, leading coefficient $a_4$). The Abel coordinate is
$z(r)=\int_{r_0}^r\dd r'/\sqrt{Q_4}$; $\sigma,\zeta,\wp$ are the Weierstrass functions
of $E$ (periods $2\omega_1,2i\omega_{\rm im}$, built from $\theta_1$). Marked points:
$\pm z_d$ (images of $r_d$), $\pm z_\infty$ (of $r=\infty$), the two-torsion turning
image $z=0$ ($r=e_4$) and seed null-surface image $z=i\omega_{\rm im}$ ($r=e_3=2M$).

\paragraph{Zeroth order (frozen orbit), verified to $10^{-12}$.}
\begin{equation}
\varphi_0(r)=\Jc\big[(E^2-1)z+((E^2-1)r_d+2M)\,U_0(z)\big],\;\;
U_0=\rho[\ln\sigma(z{-}z_d)-\ln\sigma(z{+}z_d)]+C_{U_0}z,
\end{equation}
$\rho=1/\sqrt{Q_4(r_d)}$, $U_k=\int_{r_0}^r r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r$ (explicit in
$\sigma,\zeta$ by Hermite recursion).

\paragraph{First order: source and antiderivative.}
By Lemma~I.4 of Paper~I the source is $\partial_\lambda F=N/S^{3/2}$,
$N=S\,\partial_\lambda K-\tfrac12K\,\partial_\lambda S$, $K=\Jc\,DE$; for the
Thakurta--Kerr locus the slow parameter is $\lambda=A$ and the source is the
combined conformal numerator $N=\Ehat N_E+J N_J$ (section~\ref{sec:thakurta}, via
$E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$, $J_{\rm eff}=J/A$), so every residue below splits as
$b_i=\Ehat b_i^{E}+J b_i^{J}$. On the curve $\partial_\lambda F\,\dd r=R(z)\,\dd z$
with $R=N(r(z))/[(r(z)-r_d)^3Q_4(r(z))]$ and $r(z)=c_r-a_4^{-1/2}[\zeta(z-z_\infty)-
\zeta(z+z_\infty)]$. Its antiderivative (the Theorem~I.5 assembly) is
$\tilde G(z)=C_0 z+\sum_{a}[b_1^a\ln\sigma(z-a)-b_2^a\zeta(z-a)-\tfrac12 b_3^a
\wp(z-a)]$, $a\in\{\pm z_d,0,i\omega_{\rm im}\}$, with closed-form residues: at the
triple pole $z_d$, writing $s=\sqrt{Q_4(r_d)}$, $F=N/Q_4$, $a_1=Q_4'(r_d)/4s$,
$a_2=Q_4''(r_d)/12$, $h_0=F(r_d)$, $h_1=F'(r_d)s$,
$h_2=\tfrac12F''(r_d)s^2+\tfrac14F'(r_d)Q_4'(r_d)$,
\begin{equation}
b_3^{z_d}=\frac{h_0}{s^3},\quad b_2^{z_d}=\frac{h_1-3a_1h_0}{s^3},\quad
b_1^{z_d}=\frac{h_2-3a_1h_1+(6a_1^2-3a_2)h_0}{s^3},\quad
b_2^{e_i}=\frac{4N(e_i)}{(e_i-r_d)^3Q_4'(e_i)^2},
\end{equation}
($e_i=e_4,e_3$ the two-torsion poles) and the constant closing at $z_\infty$, where
$R$ vanishes: $C_0=-\sum_a[b_1^a\zeta(z_\infty-a)+b_2^a\wp(z_\infty-a)-\tfrac12
b_3^a\wp'(z_\infty-a)]$. The axial ($J_{\rm eff}$) part shifts $b_1$ by
$\sim\!30\%$ relative to the non-rotating conformal source and is tabulated in
\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/conformal\_source\_Jterm.py}.

\subsection{Adiabatic derivative of the degeneration family}
\label{app:tracking}
As the slow parameter drifts, the genus-degeneration value itself moves,
$J=\Jc(\lambda)$. This is \emph{not} the evolution of a single torque-free extremal:
the axial costate $J$ is conserved along every extremal
(Paper I), so one trajectory cannot follow the moving locus by
changing $J$ unless an external azimuthal torque---a modified control law with
$\dd J/\dd\lambda\neq0$---is supplied. The object below is instead the total
$\lambda$-derivative of the \emph{one-parameter family of marginal (degeneration)
solutions} along $J=\Jc(\lambda)$: the adiabatic derivative of the family, which
packages how the closed form deforms as the locus drifts,
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm tot}=N+\frac{\dd\Jc}{\dd\lambda}\,N_J,\qquad
N_J=S\,\partial_JK-\tfrac12K\,\partial_JS,
\label{eq:Ntot-track}
\end{equation}
with the block form and residue formulae above holding verbatim under $N\to N_{\rm tot}$;
$\varphi_0$, the clocks and the dilogarithm structure are unchanged. The two rotating
branches differ only in $\dd\Jc/\dd\lambda$ and $N_J$.

\paragraph{Thakurta--Kerr $\tau$ and $t$ branches.}
The rotating (conformal) case follows by the transfer $E\to E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$,
$J\to J/A$ (spin $a$ retained); the control parameter is now $A$ (equivalently $E_{\rm eff}$), and
the separatrix $\Jc(E_{\rm eff})$ drifts as $A$ runs. Two curves carry the two clocks.
\emph{Proper-time ($\tau$) branch:} curve $S=r(r-2M)DE\,(r\Delta-J^2DE)$, source
$N_\tau=\partial_E(K/\sqrt S)S^{3/2}=E\,J\,r^4(r-2M)^2DE$, clock
$T_\tau=U_3-2MU_2$; the block form and residue formulae are those above with
$N\to N_\tau$. Differentiation along the degeneration family uses $N_\tau\to N_\tau+(\dd\Jc/\dd E)N_J$ with
$N_J=r^3(r-2M)^2DE^2$ ($\Delta$ cancelling identically) and
$\dd\Jc/\dd E=-E\,\Jc\,r_d/DE(r_d)=-152.96$ (at $M=1,\,a=9/10,\,E_{\rm eff}=6/5$;
verified against finite differences, block $=$ direct to $10^{-7}$).
\emph{Coordinate-time ($t$) branch:} curve $R_6=r\,Q_2\,DE$ with $Q_2$
\emph{quadratic} in $J$ (it carries $J^2$ and $(J-a)^2$ terms---not linear), so the
discriminant $\mathrm{Res}_r(Q_2,\partial_r Q_2)=0$ has \emph{six} real roots that
must be classified by the sign and location of the associated double root $r_d$
(script \texttt{separatrix\_classification.py}). Only an \emph{external} double
root $r_d>r_+$, at which two real turning points \emph{merge}, is a physical
separatrix (capture$/$scatter boundary): this is the retrograde $\Jc^-=-8.054$
($r_d=+3.514>2M$) of equation~\eqref{eq:Jc-minus}, consistent with the body, plus a
prograde \emph{ergospheric} branch $\Jc=+2.936$ at $r_+<r_d=1.512<2M$. The large
roots $\Jc=+19.089,\,-18.671$ instead sit at $r_d=-6.62,\,-6.59<0$: these are
\emph{algebraic genus-degenerations} $J_{\rm deg}$ (the sextic drops to genus one
and the Weierstrass reduction still closes) but \emph{not} physical separatrices---
no external turning-point merger, no divergent radial period, and they must not be
conflated with the dynamical penetration threshold $J_c(v_0)$. The clock is the
mixed second/third kind $\dd t/\dd r=\rho_t/\sqrt{R_6}$, $\rho_t=P_3+R_\Delta/\Delta$
[equation~\eqref{eq:clock-t}], adding a third-kind horizon letter. On the physical branch
$\Jc^-$ the corrected conformal source (section~\ref{sec:thakurta}, with the
$J\,\partial_J F$ term) gives $b=(b_1,b_2,b_3)=(-0.685,-0.074,-0.0035)$ and the
\emph{tame} separatrix drift
\begin{equation}
\frac{\dd\Jc^-}{\dd E}=-\frac{\partial_E Q_2}{\partial_J Q_2}\bigg|_{(r_d,\Jc^-)}=+2.44
\end{equation}
(the earlier $\mp148$--$150$ magnitudes belonged to the unphysical $J_{\rm deg}$
roots at $r_d<0$). All family-derivative coefficients are closed-form residues, symbolic in
$(M,a,E,r_d,\Jc)$; the triple-pole cancellation below is an algebraic identity at
\emph{any} double root, hence holds on the physical branch as well.

\paragraph{The degeneration-family derivative cancels the leading pole.}
By Lemma~I.6 of Paper~I, at the double root the total family-derivative source \emph{vanishes},
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm tot}(r_d)=-\tfrac12K(r_d)\Big[\partial_\lambda S
+\tfrac{\dd\Jc}{\dd\lambda}\,\partial_JS\Big](r_d)
=\tfrac12K(r_d)\,S'(r_d)\,\frac{\dd r_d}{\dd\lambda}=0,
\label{eq:track-cancel}
\end{equation}
because $S'(r_d)=0$ (the separatrix double-root condition). Hence the triple-pole
residue drops, $b_3^{\rm fam}=0$: the total family derivative softens the
strongest singularity, precisely where the double root travels. For the Thakurta--Kerr
separatrices this is verified symbolically in Mathematica,
$N_{\rm tot}(r_d)\equiv0\pmod{S,\,S'}$.

\paragraph{Fully symbolic coefficients, off-separatrix $q$-series, and independent checks.}
Off the separatrix (generic $J$, genus two) every block coefficient is a
\emph{rational function of $(M,a,E,J)$}: the source coefficients $c_k$,
$Q_{kj}=c_kb_j-c_jb_k$, and the canonical pole coefficients $g_i$ (Taylor
coefficients of $q_6^{-1/2}$ at the odd-model infinity), with
$g_0=1/\sqrt{E^2-1}$ and $g_1=(2E^2-3)/(E^2-1)^{3/2}$ set by the leading behaviour
($E$-only), while $g_2$ and the $c_k$ carry the full $(a,J)$ dependence. The
algebraic remainder is elementary in closed form,
$T_{\rm alg}=P(r)+\sum_i\mathrm{res}_i\ln(r-r_i)$ over the six roots of $S$
(three residues vanish, at $r=0,\,2M,\,2M/(1-E^2)$). The weight-two block---the
genus-two dilogarithm in the descriptive sense fixed above---is a
convergent \emph{nome ($q$-)series}: after Siegel reduction $\tau'=-\tau^{-1}$ (all
nomes ${<}1$), $\Lambda=\int Q\,\dd\mathcal A+\int L\,\dd\mathcal A$ with $Q$
elementary and $L=\log[\theta[\delta](w-e_+)/\theta[\delta](w-e_-)]$ a genus-two
Kronecker--Eisenstein series that converges geometrically (order $N{=}4$ already
matches to $10^{-13}$) and does \emph{not} reduce to classical dilogarithms (there is
no genus-two Jacobi product formula). All these symbolic statements are
\emph{independently reproduced in Mathematica} (native $\wp$, Gröbner reduction,
Laurent extraction): the separatrix residues to $10^{-16}$--$10^{-38}$, the generic-$J$
reductions to an exact polynomial identity, and the family-derivative
cancellation~\eqref{eq:track-cancel}.

\paragraph{Transcendence.}
None of these is elementary. The $U_k$ are third-kind elliptic integrals, $\tilde G$
lives in $\sigma,\zeta,\wp$, and the weight-two content is the elliptic dilogarithm,
conjecturally irreducible to weight one (theta-divisor obstruction $\Theta=W_{g-1}$). The
third-kind letter at the seed Kerr null surface $\Delta=0$ is elementary only as a
$\dd\log$ letter; integrated against the elliptic source it yields the genus-two
dilogarithm, of the same transcendental weight as the on-shell case, with the anchor at
the third-kind dipole \emph{at infinity} of the $t,\tau$ clocks (the spherical
horizon-anchored analogue, at $r=2m$, is in Paper~I). The sole
numerical ingredient anywhere is the optional compression of the weight-two content to
an apparent minimal five-dimensional dilogarithm basis (a numerically stable rank five,
singular values falling to $10^{-79}$---strong evidence, \emph{not} a proof, and
curve-specific), which the block forms above do not require.

\section{Complete first-order extended-Hamiltonian correction}
\label{app:firstorder}
The complete first-order (on-shell \emph{plus} off-shell) adiabatic correction follows
from Theorem~I.5 of the companion Paper~I, where it is established from the extended
Hamiltonian in canonical variables. The new, metric-specific feature of the conformal
Kerr application is that the normalization $P_r=p_r/A$ is time-dependent and
\emph{non-canonical}: passing from the canonical pair $(r,p_r)$ to $P_r$ produces the
dilation term $-(A'/A)P_r$ and the full Euler operator
$\mathcal D=E_{\rm eff}\partial_{E_{\rm eff}}+J_{\rm eff}\partial_{J_{\rm eff}}+P_r
\partial_{P_r}$. This appendix specializes the theorem to the conformal $t$- and
$\tau$-branches and gives the resulting Abelian reduction; the general canonical-variable
proof and the (non-conformal) Vaidya specialization are in Paper~I.

\paragraph{Notation and the slow datum.} $\Ehat$ and $J\equiv J_{\rm TK}=p_\varphi$ are
the \emph{conserved} control charges (the conformal-Killing rail charge and the axial costate); the effective
unit-mass Kerr charges on the frozen slice are $E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$ and
$J_{\rm eff}=J/A$, both of conformal weight $A^{-1}$. The slow datum is $s$ ($s=\eta$ for
Thakurta--Kerr, $s=v$ for Vaidya); we write $A(s)=A_0(1+\varepsilon s+\cdots)$ with
$A_0\equiv1$ and the \emph{dimensionless} rate $\varepsilon=M\,A_{,\eta}/A$ (for Vaidya
$\varepsilon=M\dot m/m$). The operator generating the drift of the frozen family is the
Euler operator
\begin{equation}
    \Theta\equiv E_{\rm eff}\,\partial_{E_{\rm eff}}+J_{\rm eff}\,\partial_{J_{\rm eff}}
    \quad(\text{Thakurta--Kerr}),\qquad
    \Theta\equiv m\,\partial_m\quad(\text{Vaidya}),
    \label{eq:theta-op}
\end{equation}
since $\dd E_{\rm eff}/\dd s=-\varepsilon E_{\rm eff}$, $\dd J_{\rm eff}/\dd s=-\varepsilon
J_{\rm eff}$ at leading order (Vaidya: $\dd m/\dd s=+\varepsilon m$).

\paragraph{Frozen data (per branch).} At fixed $s$ the branch Hamiltonian
$H_0(r,p_r;E_{\rm eff},J_{\rm eff})=0$ fixes: the \emph{ingoing} frozen momentum
$p_{r0}(r)$, the root of $H_0=0$ with $H_{0,p_r}<0$ (the outgoing arc is its mirror
across the turning point); the shape integrand $G_0(r)=\dd\varphi/\dd r=H_{0,J}/H_{0,p_r}$;
and the clock density $\dd\lambda/\dd r=1/H_{0,p_r}$, with $\lambda$ the elapsed clock
oriented \emph{inward from the free-arrival endpoint}.

\paragraph{The conformal rescaling: canonical derivation of the dilation term.} The
Thakurta--Kerr Hamiltonians factorize (equations~\eqref{eq:Heta}--\eqref{eq:Htau-tk}) as
$H_{\rm br}(r,p_r,p_\varphi;A)=A\,\bar H_{\rm br}(r,P_r,J_{\rm eff};E_{\rm eff})$ with the
\emph{normalized} radial momentum $P_r=p_r/A$ (and $J_{\rm eff}=p_\varphi/A$,
$E_{\rm eff}=\Ehat/A$). We derive the flow in the \emph{original} canonical variables
$(r,p_r)$ and only then pass to $P_r$. Adjoining the slow clock $s$ with conjugate
$p_s$, the extended Hamiltonian $\mathcal H=p_s+H_{\rm br}(r,p_r;A(s))=0$ generates the
\emph{canonical} equations $\dd r/\dd s=\partial_{p_r}H_{\rm br}$,
$\dd p_r/\dd s=-\partial_r H_{\rm br}$. The rescaling $P_r=p_r/A$ is \emph{not} itself
canonical---at fixed $A$, $\dd r\wedge\dd p_r=A\,\dd r\wedge\dd P_r$, so it is a
time-dependent (conformally symplectic) \emph{normalization}, not a canonical
transformation. Its $s$-derivative along the canonical flow follows by the chain rule
(with $\alpha\equiv A_{,s}/A=\varepsilon+O(\varepsilon^2)$ and the identity
$\partial_r H_{\rm br}|_{p_r}=A\,\bar H_r|_{P_r}$):
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd r}{\dd s}=\bar H_{P_r},\quad
    \frac{\dd P_r}{\dd s}=\frac1A\frac{\dd p_r}{\dd s}-\frac{A_{,s}}{A}P_r
      =-\bar H_r-\alpha P_r,\quad
    \frac{\dd E_{\rm eff}}{\dd s}=-\alpha E_{\rm eff},\quad
    \frac{\dd J_{\rm eff}}{\dd s}=-\alpha J_{\rm eff},
    \label{eq:normflow}
\end{equation}
an identity verified symbolically (\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/canonical\_dilation\_check.py}).
The $-\alpha P_r$ term is thus a \emph{consequence} of the canonical flow in $(r,p_r)$
together with the normalization---not an added piece, and not the generator of a
canonical transformation; dropping it would misrepresent $\dd(p_r/A)/\dd s$. The
normalized system~\eqref{eq:normflow} is non-canonical (conformally symplectic) but
\emph{equivalent} to the canonical derivation. Consequently the shell-departure rate is
the \emph{full} Euler operator
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd\bar H}{\dd s}=-\alpha\,\mathcal D\bar H,\qquad
    \mathcal D\equiv\Theta+P_r\partial_{P_r}
    =E_{\rm eff}\partial_{E_{\rm eff}}+J_{\rm eff}\partial_{J_{\rm eff}}+P_r\partial_{P_r}.
    \label{eq:Dop}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Extended system and first order.} The canonical extended system adjoins $s$
with conjugate $p_s$ in the \emph{original} variables,
$\mathcal H=p_s+H_{\rm br}(r,p_r;A(s))=0$; passing to the normalized (conformally
symplectic) variables rewrites the shell as $\bar H(r,P_r;E_{\rm eff}(s),J_{\rm eff}(s))=0$
(since $H_{\rm br}=A\bar H$, $A>0$). Free-arrival transversality
sets $\bar H=0$ (hence $p_s=0$) at $\lambda=0$. Along the flow
$\dd p_s/\dd\lambda=\varepsilon\,\mathcal D\bar H$, so the \emph{terminally-anchored}
source is
\begin{equation}
    p_s^{(1)}(\lambda)\equiv S_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\int_0^{\lambda}\mathcal D\bar H\,\dd\lambda'
    =\int_0^{\lambda}\big(\Theta\bar H+P_r\bar H_{P_r}\big)\,\dd\lambda',
    \label{eq:pS-app}
\end{equation}
and, linearizing $\bar H$ at fixed $r$, the momentum-shell shift
\begin{equation}
    \delta P_r=\frac{\lambda\,\Theta\bar H-S_{\mathcal D}}{\bar H_{P_r}},
    \label{eq:dpr-app}
\end{equation}
the axial costate remaining conserved ($\delta J=0$; it is $J_{\rm eff}=J/A$ that
breathes). The shape varies as $\dd\varphi/\dd r=G_0+\varepsilon[\partial_{P_r}G_0\,\delta
P_r-\lambda\,\Theta G_0]+O(\varepsilon^2)$; integrating in $r$ gives
Equation~\eqref{eq:adiab-exact} \emph{with $S\to S_{\mathcal D}$}. The earlier $S=\int\Theta\bar
H\,\dd\lambda$ (omitting $P_r\bar H_{P_r}$) closes only against a surrogate flow lacking
$-\alpha P_r$; against the true canonical flow it leaves an $O(\varepsilon)$ error. The
reduction is non-uniform at the turning point ($\bar H_{P_r}\to0$): it holds on compact
sub-arcs, and all displayed residuals are taken there.

\paragraph{The dilation letter in closed form (so the reader can reconstruct it).}
Since $\dd\lambda=\dd r/\bar H_{P_r}$, the extra source piece is the radial action,
\begin{equation}
    \int_0^{\lambda}P_r\bar H_{P_r}\,\dd\lambda'=\int p_{r0}\,\dd r,
    \label{eq:dilation-action}
\end{equation}
and this is elementary to reduce. On the frozen $t$-branch shell the radial momentum is
\begin{equation}
    p_{r0}=\frac{\sqrt{S}}{\Delta\,[(E^2-1)r+2M]},\qquad
    \Delta=r^2-2Mr+a^2,\qquad S=r\,[(E^2-1)r+2M]\,Q_2(r),
    \label{eq:pr0-tbranch}
\end{equation}
with $E\equiv E_{\rm eff}$, $J\equiv J_{\rm eff}$, $Q_2$ the branch quartic (equation~\eqref{eq:t-K})
and $S$ the on-shell sextic. Polynomial division of $S$ by $\Delta\,[(E^2-1)r+2M]$ gives,
in the standard basis $U_k=\int r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r$,
\begin{equation}
    \int p_{r0}\,\dd r=\sum_{k=0}^{3}a_k\,U_k+(\text{third kind at }\Delta=0),
    \label{eq:action-reduction}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
    a_3=E^2,\quad a_2=2E^2M,\quad a_1=4E^2M^2-(E^2-1)J^2,\quad
    a_0=2M\big[4E^2M^2-J^2+2(1-E^2)Ja\big],
    \label{eq:ak}
\end{equation}
with $[a_k]=\text{length}^{3-k}$. The kernel is likewise algebraic on the shell,
$\partial_{P_r}G_0/\bar H_{P_r}=A(r)/\sqrt S$ with $A$ rational (the inner radical
$\sqrt{\Delta v/\bar P}$ cancels on shell), so the complete off-shell correction is a
length-two iterated integral on the single spectral curve $\sqrt S$, with the symbolic
coefficients~\eqref{eq:ak}. Every step is verified to machine precision against the
\emph{true} canonical flow~\eqref{eq:normflow}
(\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/adiabatic\_offshell\_reduction.py},
\texttt{adiabatic\_first\_order\_exact.py}, \texttt{tests/test\_adiabatic\_noreg.py}).

\paragraph{Special-function class.} The building blocks form a strict transcendence
hierarchy. The second-kind integrals $U_0,\dots,U_3=\int r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r$ are
\emph{ordinary Abelian integrals} (weight one), reducible to the classical Kleinian
$\zeta,\sigma$ basis (Baker; \ref{app:explicit}), the same class as $\varphi_0$. The
letters $W_{jk}=\int(r^jU_k-r^kU_j)/\sqrt S\,\dd r$ are \emph{length-two iterated} Abelian
integrals with algebraic coefficients---one transcendence level above the $U_k$ and
\emph{not} reducible to the weight-one Kleinian class (their irreducibility to weight one
is \emph{conjectural}). The third-kind letter at the seed Kerr null surfaces
$\Delta=0$---whose residue is nonzero, unlike the on-shell $\psi$ where it
vanishes---adds a genus-two dilogarithm (descriptively, a hyperelliptic polylogarithm
\cite{BauneEtal2024,DHokerHiddingSchlotterer2025}), evaluated by the geometrically
convergent theta-nome series in the reproducibility package; its irreducibility and
single-valued completion remain open. Thus the \emph{frozen} reduction
$\partial_\lambda\varphi_0$ closes in the weight-one Abelian/Kleinian class, but already
the \emph{on-shell} first-order response carries a genuine weight-two sector---the
length-two iterated integrals $W_{jk}$; the off-shell term adds further length-two letters
and one third-kind-sourced length-two iterated Abelian integral. Reducibility of the
$W_{jk}$ to weight one and a canonical single-valued completion remain conjectural, so no
function-class statement beyond ``length-two iterated Abelian integral on a genus-two
hyperelliptic curve'' is asserted anywhere in this paper.

\paragraph{What is reconstructible from the text alone.} Everything algebraic is fixed
by the manuscript without the repository: the frozen integrands
$\dd\varphi/\dd r=\mathcal K/\sqrt S$ with their spectral radicals and numerators;
the on-shell reduction (the polynomial identity $2A'S-AS'+2S\sum_k c_kr^k=2N_\lambda$
and its $11\times11$ linear system---no fit); all block coefficients $c_k,a_k,g_i,Q_{kj}$;
the Kleinian $\zeta,\sigma$ closed forms of the $U_k$ (Hermite recursion) and the assembly
of the weight-two $W_{jk}$ from them (not reduced to the weight-one class); the elementary
remainders; and the \emph{form} of the genus-two letter
$L=\log[\theta[\delta](w-e_+)/\theta[\delta](w-e_-)]$ with its odd half-characteristic
$\delta$ and the genus-one nome series $g^{(1)}$ printed in
Section~\ref{sec:closed}. What the text does \emph{not} reproduce by hand is the
\emph{numerical evaluation} of the genus-two $\theta$: constructing the period matrix
$\tau$ from $Y^2=\mathcal S$, its Siegel reduction, and summing the geometrically
convergent nome series (order $N{=}4$ already to $10^{-13}$) are performed by
\texttt{Sage}$+$\texttt{abelfunctions} (\texttt{RiemannTheta}) in the reproducibility
package~\cite{Rosignoli2026Code}. In short: the symbolic reduction and the closed-form
\emph{representation} are reconstructible from the PDF; the genus-two \emph{numbers}
require the archived routine.

\paragraph{Branch closure and validation.} The conformal $t$- and $\tau$-branches close
to $O(\varepsilon^2)$ against the true non-autonomous flow: the least-squares log--log
slope is $\approx1$ for the on-shell term alone versus $2.12$ for the complete first-order
term across both branches and two parameter sets (figure~\ref{fig:phi-true-dynamic}). The
(non-conformal) Vaidya branches---where $m$ is a metric modulus, $\mathcal D=\Theta=
m\partial_m$ with no dilation term---are treated in Paper~I.

\paragraph{Separatrix limit ($J\to\Jc$).} The off-shell separatrix correction is simply
the generic term at $J=\Jc$: the coefficients~\eqref{eq:ak} are polynomials in $J$, hence
finite there, while the spectral curve \emph{degenerates}, $S=(r-r_d)^2Q_4$ (double root
$r_d$, genus two $\to$ genus one). Each hyperelliptic letter collapses,
$U_k=\int r^k/\sqrt S\to V_k+r_d^{\,k}\,\Pi_{r_d}$ with $V_k=\int(\text{poly})/\sqrt{Q_4}$
(elliptic second kind, Weierstrass $\wp,\zeta$) and $\Pi_{r_d}=\int\dd r/[(r-r_d)\sqrt{Q_4}]$
(third kind); the spurious poles at $r_d$ cancel in the physical combination because
$\sum_k a_k(\Jc)r_d^{\,k}+R(\Jc,r_d)/[\Delta\,((E^2-1)r+2M)]|_{r_d}=[S/\text{deng}](r_d)=0$
(as $S(r_d)=0$), leaving the weight-two block as a genuine \emph{elliptic}
dilogarithm~\cite{BeilinsonLevin1994,BrownLevin2011} plus third kind at $\Delta=0$. Thus
the separatrix off-shell form is one degree \emph{lower} than the generic genus-two case;
verified letter by letter to machine precision (\texttt{ThakurtaMetric/tk\_sep\_genus2\_to\_elliptic.py},
\texttt{tk\_sep\_offshell\_elliptic.py}; the Vaidya analogue is in Paper~I).
For Thakurta--Kerr the double root sits inside the ergosphere, on the physical arc, and
the dilation letter injects there a power pole $1/(r-r_d)^2$ (residue
$\propto\Delta S(r_d)$, verified against the true flow) that is \emph{not} removed by
differentiation along the marginal family, $\dd J_c/\dd E$: the pole comes from the kernel $A\sim1/Q_2^2$, not
from the source. Crucially this is not a missing algebraic counterterm. Under the
adiabatic evolution the dilation ray $(\Ehat/A,\,J/A)$ leaves the separatrix locus
$J=J_c(E)$, so the double root does not merely move---it \emph{dissolves}, and the orbit
\emph{crosses} the separatrix. The off-shell non-uniformity at $r_d$ is therefore a
separatrix-crossing phenomenon: the first-order
adiabatic term is non-uniform there and its uniform completion requires inner/outer
matched asymptotics, outside the symbolic-coefficient reduction. The setting is
exactly the classical one---a Hamiltonian $H(p,q,\lambda)$ with $\lambda=\varepsilon
t$ slowly varying, whose phase portrait at fixed $\lambda$ has regions of closed
orbits separated by separatrices, so that slow variation of $\lambda$ carries a
phase point across a separatrix of the frozen problem---and for that setting a
general formula for the resulting change of the adiabatic invariant is known
\cite{Timofeev1978,CaryEscandeTennyson1986,Neishtadt1986}. Two consequences are
worth stating plainly. First, what a uniform treatment would produce is not a
smooth correction but a \emph{jump} in the adiabatic invariant, and that jump is
known to be highly sensitive to initial conditions---quasi-random in the crossing
phase---so no smooth completion of the first-order term should be expected. Second,
the dilation ray crosses the locus \emph{once}; we therefore claim nothing about the
accumulation of jumps over repeated crossings, which is what destroys adiabatic
invariance outright in the systems where that has been studied. For Vaidya the double
root is a branch point off the physical arc, $\mathcal D=\Theta$ carries no dilation,
and no such crossing occurs.
The closest quantitative statement we know of is for a different class of
systems: in two-frequency averaging the method fails only on an exceptional set
of initial data of measure $O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$---those captured into
resonance---with a sharp $O(\sqrt\varepsilon\,|\!\log\varepsilon|)$ error outside
it \cite{Neishtadt2013}. Our system is not of that form, so we quote this as the
scale such failures are known to have, not as a bound on ours.

      Myers requires the MEAN curvature -- in his normalisation the
      trace Ric(v,v) over unit v, his Lemma, p. 402 -- to be >= e^2 > 0
      at every point and in every direction.  Decay of the SECTIONAL
      curvature would not by itself settle this: Myers himself points
      out (Sec. 4) that on S^2 x S^2 the sectional curvature reaches
      zero while the mean curvature stays constant and positive.  The
      hypothesis must therefore be tested on Ric, which is done in the
      three-dimensional block below.  It fails there by SIGN, not by
      decay, so no diameter (hence no arrival-time) bound follows.
      Conjugate points must therefore be located directly, from the
      Jacobi equation, as done in Paper I -- not inferred from a
      curvature-operator or Myers-type hypothesis.
      The curvature-operator hypotheses fail for the SAME computed
      reason.  Boehm-Wilking Thm 1 is about a COMPACT manifold with a
      2-POSITIVE curvature operator (sum of the two smallest
      eigenvalues positive -- weaker than positive); Hamilton 1982 is
      the three-dimensional result, Hamilton 1986 the four-dimensional
      one, so 1986 is not the applicable citation for a 3d optical
      base.  Independently of compactness: 2-positivity forces the
      trace of the curvature operator to be positive, hence R > 0.
      We proved R < 0 everywhere, so the curvature condition itself
      is excluded, not merely its compactness hypothesis.

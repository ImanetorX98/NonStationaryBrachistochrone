ALT TEXT FOR FIGURES — Paper I (JMP submission)
"Controlled-rail brachistochrones in non-stationary spacetimes"
Iman Rosignoli

AIP Publishing requires alt text for every figure. Paste the text below into the
corresponding field of the submission system. Alt text describes what is visible;
it deliberately does not repeat the caption, which carries the interpretation.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 1 — fig_indicatrici
Two side-by-side panels plotting closed curves in the local velocity plane, radial
velocity on the horizontal axis and azimuthal velocity on the vertical. The left
panel shows a nested family of circles centred on the origin, each smaller than the
last. The right panel shows ellipses whose centres are displaced from the origin
toward negative radial velocity and which are elongated along the radial direction.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 2 — fig_flrw_cinematica and fig_flrw_variazionale (two panels, a and b)
Panel (a): two stacked line plots against cosmic time. The upper plot shows four
local-speed curves falling from near unity to zero, each ending at its own vertical
dotted line. The lower plot shows four decaying rail-redshift curves crossing a
horizontal dashed reference line at unity. Panel (b): two travel-time curves plotted
against the amplitude of a sinusoidal deformation of the path; both are smooth and
attain their minimum at zero amplitude.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 3 — fig_flrw_curvatura (two panels, a and b)
Panel (a): three curves in a comoving map, all leaving the same point at the same
height above a marked reference point O. The flat curve is a horizontal straight
line, the closed curve bends downward toward O, and the open curve bends upward away
from it; a dotted segment marks the common closest approach. Panel (b): three
increasing, saturating curves of reachable comoving distance against rail energy,
one per value of the curvature-to-Hubble ratio. A horizontal line marks the
antipodal distance pi, and the region above it is shaded; only the uppermost curve
enters that region.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 4 — fig_kodama_conservazione
Two stacked plots against radius. The upper plot, on a logarithmic vertical axis,
shows the residual of the rail invariant as a nearly flat trace lying below 1e-15
across the whole range. The lower plot shows the free-particle drift of the same
quantity as a smooth curve that is nowhere zero.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 5 — fig_vaidya_traiettorie
Equatorial orbit shapes for the frozen Schwarzschild case and for Vaidya, plotted
together and nearly coincident, with a visible separation growing inward. A second
panel plots the costate p_v against radius: it is zero at the outer endpoint and
departs from zero along the inward part of the path.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 6 — fig_vaidya_plunge_t_tau
Two decreasing curves of plunge radius plotted against the mass-rate parameter, one
for each clock branch, together with a third curve for their difference. The
coordinate-time curve lies below the proper-time curve throughout, and the gap
between them widens toward positive mass rate.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 7 — fig_vaidya_no_inversione_evaporazione
The branch gap plotted against the mass-rate parameter. The curve crosses zero only
inside a shaded band marking the unphysical negative-mass region. A second curve,
for an exponentially decaying mass, approaches zero from above without crossing it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 8 — fig_vaidya_bounce
Radius plotted against advanced time through the turning point, showing a smooth
rounded minimum with the trajectory continuing outward. Overlaid is the
radius-parametrised flow, which terminates abruptly at the same minimum instead of
continuing through it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 9 — fig_vaidya_timing
Two plots against arrival epoch, each carrying three curves for the three mass
rates. The first plots the periapsis radius, the second the same radius divided by
the horizon radius. The ordering of the three curves is reversed between the two
plots.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 10 — fig_vaidya_offshell
A log-log scatter plot of residual against the dimensionless adiabatic rate, with
two fitted straight lines of clearly different slope: the shallower line has slope
close to one, the steeper line slope close to two.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 11 — fig_vaidya_kerr_a0
A numerically integrated orbit and a closed-form reference orbit plotted on the same
axes; the two traces are visually indistinguishable. An inset or companion trace
shows their difference, staying below 1e-12 across the range.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig. 12 — fig_verifica_minimo_brachi
Travel time plotted against the amplitude of a path perturbation, one curve per
clock branch. Each curve is smooth with a single minimum at zero amplitude, and the
two curves are separated from one another.

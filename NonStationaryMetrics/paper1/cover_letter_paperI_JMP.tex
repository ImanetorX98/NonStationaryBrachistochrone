\vspace{0.5em}
I am pleased to submit the manuscript \textbf{``Controlled-rail brachistochrones in
non-stationary spacetimes: conformal symmetry, Kodama energy, and Vaidya dynamics''} for
consideration as a research article in the \emph{Journal of Mathematical Physics}.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{The problem.} In a stationary spacetime the relativistic brachistochrone
reduces to Fermat--Randers optics with a conserved rail energy supplied by a timelike
Killing vector; this is the setting of Perlick's fixed-energy optical metric (1991). When
the geometry itself evolves, no such conservation law exists, the reduced Hamiltonian
ceases to be an interior first integral, and the variational problem becomes genuinely
non-autonomous. The manuscript asks what replaces the Killing charge, and what survives of
the optical-geometry picture.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Mathematical contributions.} The construction is a \emph{controlled-rail}
optimal-control problem in which the invariant $-u\cdot W=\hat E$ is actively maintained by
a selector $W$ following a Killing $\to$ conformal-Killing $\to$ Kodama hierarchy. The
paper's results are:

\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item a \textbf{regularity criterion} reducing the admissible domain to two scalar
inequalities---$W$ timelike and $\hat E>|W|$---from which the compact, strictly convex
velocity indicatrix is \emph{derived} rather than assumed, with the freezing surface
identified as the locus $\hat E=|W|$ where it collapses to a point;
\item an \textbf{existence and normality theorem} (direct method, closed by a Gr\"onwall
estimate on the clock and the Filippov--Cesari theorem on the augmented state) together
with a \textbf{non-autonomous Hamilton--Jacobi verification criterion}, global optimality
being stated as conditional rather than automatic;
\item an \textbf{exact recovery of Perlick's stationary optics}: solving the rail jointly
with the mass shell yields a Randers arrival-time functional whose threading form is
precisely the fixed-energy optical metric $a_{ab}=\hat E^2h_{ab}/[f(\hat E^2-f)]$, with the
standard Fermat--Randers metric $h_{ab}/f$ recovered as $\hat E\to\infty$. The
non-stationary theory is thus a strict extension, not an analogy;
\item a \textbf{reduction theorem} showing that the entire adiabatic hierarchy collapses
onto one degree-ten polynomial identity, whose $11\times11$ determinant is
$32\,\mathrm{Res}_r(S,S')=-32\,s_6\operatorname{disc}_rS$ for an arbitrary sextic; the
reduction is therefore uniquely solvable exactly on the genus-two stratum and degenerates
exactly where the spectral curve does;
\item \textbf{closed forms}: all reduction coefficients as rational functions of
$(\hat E^2,J,m)$ over the reduced discriminant, a complete first-order response with an
$O(\varepsilon^2)$ remainder bound, and a universal boundary form
$S_{\mathcal D}=[\,r\,p_r\,]-\lambda$ for the off-shell source.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Applications.} The formalism is realised in two spherically symmetric
non-stationary geometries: FLRW at arbitrary spatial curvature, where the extremal is the
geodesic of the slice and the closed case supplies an explicit Maxwell set at the
antipodal cut locus, and the ingoing Vaidya dynamical black hole, where the first-order
adiabatic correction closes in a horizon dilogarithm on a genus-two curve degenerating to
genus one.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Fit and verification.} The work sits in the scope of the \emph{Journal of
Mathematical Physics}: optimal control and non-autonomous Hamilton--Jacobi theory, Finsler
and Randers geometry, and exact special-function methods on hyperelliptic curves, applied
to and developed for a problem in general relativity. Every closed form is derived
symbolically and independently re-verified in two computer-algebra systems; the
reproducibility package, including those verification scripts, is openly archived on
Zenodo (DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21782443}{10.5281/zenodo.21782443}).
Statements that remain conjectural---the irreducibility of the weight-two letters to
weight one and the canonical single-valued completion of the genus-two dilogarithm---are
labelled as such at the point of use and no result depends on them.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Companion manuscript.} This is the self-contained first part of a
two-part series and establishes the general formalism with its spherically symmetric
realisations. The rotating, axisymmetric conformal-Kerr (Thakurta--Kerr) application is
developed in a \emph{distinct} companion manuscript, \emph{``Brachistochrones in conformal
Kerr spacetimes: ergosphere trichotomy, separatrices, and adiabatic response''} (Zenodo
DOI: \href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21740000}{10.5281/zenodo.21740000}), currently
under consideration at \emph{Classical and Quantum Gravity}. The two share authorship but
not content, and the present submission can be assessed entirely on its own.

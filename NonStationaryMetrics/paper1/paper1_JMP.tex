\title{Controlled-rail brachistochrones in non-stationary spacetimes:
conformal symmetry, Kodama energy, and Vaidya dynamics}

\author{Iman Rosignoli}
\address{Department of Physics, University of Pavia, Via A.\ Bassi 6, 27100 Pavia, Italy}
\ead{iman.rosignoli01@universitadipavia.it}

\begin{abstract}
What is the fastest constrained worldline in a spacetime that is itself evolving? In a
stationary spacetime the relativistic brachistochrone reduces to Fermat/Randers optics
with a conserved rail energy supplied by a timelike Killing vector; in a dynamical
spacetime no such conservation law exists and the variational problem becomes
non-autonomous. We address this by formulating the brachistochrone as a
\emph{controlled-rail} optimal-control problem, in which the invariant $-u\cdot W=\Ehat$ is
actively maintained along the worldline by a selector $W$ that follows a Killing $\to$
conformal-Killing $\to$ Kodama hierarchy and reduces to the \emph{Kodama} vector when no
timelike Killing field survives. We show that this construction is a legitimate
Pontryagin problem---establishing existence and normality on a regular timelike-selector
domain and a non-autonomous Hamilton--Jacobi \emph{verification} criterion (global
minimisation being conditional, not automatic)---and then derive the extremal equations
and their closed representations in two spherically symmetric non-stationary cases:
spatially flat Friedmann--Lema\^itre--Robertson--Walker (FLRW) and the ingoing Vaidya
dynamical black hole. For Vaidya we obtain the plunge phenomenology and a complete
first-order adiabatic correction, verified against the true non-autonomous flow, that
separates the homogeneous-expansion effects of the FLRW base from the radial
spatial-gradient and mass-flow effects of the dynamical horizon. The rotating,
axisymmetric conformal-Kerr (Thakurta--Kerr)
application is developed in a companion paper.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
The brachistochrone---the path of fastest descent---is a classical variational problem
whose relativistic analogue asks for the fastest constrained ``rail'' worldline from a
launch event to a fixed spatial target, at a \emph{free} value of the arrival clock. In a
\emph{stationary} spacetime the answer is well understood: a timelike Killing vector
supplies a conserved rail energy, and the problem reduces to Fermat's principle and to
optical (Randers--Zermelo--Finsler) geometry
\cite{Perlick1991,PerlickRayOptics,Kovner1990,Randers1941,GibbonsWerner2008,GibbonsRanders2009,BaoRoblesShen}.
Existence, multiplicity and Morse theory for these stationary arrival- and travel-time
brachistochrones, together with their sub-Riemannian and Fermat-principle formulations,
are by now well developed
\cite{GiannoniPiccioneVerderesi1997,GiannoniPiccioneTausk2002,GiannoniPiccione2002,CaponioJavaloyes2026}.
The physically central regimes of gravitation, however---cosmological expansion,
gravitational collapse, accretion---are \emph{not} stationary.

These treatments all exploit stationarity---a prescribed observer-field energy, a
conserved Killing charge, or a time-independent optical metric. When the background
geometry itself evolves that structure disappears: there is no timelike Killing field, no
conserved energy to label the extremals, and the reduced problem is genuinely
non-autonomous, its ordinary Hamiltonian no longer an interior first integral. The
difficulty we address is therefore specific: how to \emph{prescribe and maintain} a rail
charge in an evolving spacetime, what field should supply it, and how time
dependence---as opposed to spatial gradients---reshapes the optimal path.

Our starting point is that the rail energy need not be a symmetry charge: it can be an
invariant that is \emph{actively maintained} along the worldline. We formulate the
brachistochrone as a \emph{controlled-rail} optimal-control problem in which
$-u\cdot W=\Ehat$ is held fixed by a selector $W$ following a Killing $\to$
conformal-Killing $\to$ Kodama hierarchy; in a dynamical spherically symmetric spacetime
$W$ is the \emph{Kodama} vector, whose charge is conserved along the controlled worldline
even where free fall is not. Pontryagin's maximum principle makes this a legitimate
time-optimal control problem, supplying existence, normality and a non-autonomous
Hamilton--Jacobi verification criterion in place of the stationary conservation law.

We develop this general framework in section~\ref{sec:principle} and then apply it to two
spherically symmetric non-stationary spacetimes: the spatially flat FLRW base
(section~\ref{sec:flrw}) and the ingoing Vaidya dynamical black hole
(section~\ref{sec:vaidya}). For Vaidya we obtain the plunge phenomenology and a
first-order adiabatic correction in closed special-function form, verified against the
true non-autonomous flow; the construction cleanly separates homogeneous expansion from
radial spatial-gradient and mass-flow effects. The rotating, axisymmetric conformal-Kerr
(Thakurta--Kerr) case---frame dragging, an ergosphere and genus-two closed forms---is
developed in the companion Paper~II~\cite{PaperTwo}, which cites the foundations proved
here.

\paragraph{Main results and organization.} Six results carry the mathematical content of
the paper and are labelled I.1--I.6 throughout, a numbering that the companion paper cites
verbatim. All six are stated under the standing hypotheses (H1)--(H3) of
section~\ref{sec:indicatrix}, which delimit the \emph{regular domain} by three checkable
scalar inequalities: the selector timelike, the rail energy strictly above the local
freezing value $|W|$, and that margin uniform on a compact domain. The geometric
properties actually used---that the velocity indicatrix is a compact strictly convex
ellipse and that the arrival-time functional is two-sidedly bounded---are not assumed but
\emph{derived} from those inequalities in Lemma~\ref{lem:indicatrix}, which also identifies
the freezing surface as the locus $\Ehat=|W|$ where the indicatrix collapses to a point.
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:rail}} (section~\ref{sec:indicatrix}) reduces the
controlled-rail brachistochrone to a time-optimal control problem with compact control set
$S^1$, whose maximized Hamiltonian is single-valued in closed form and requires no
Filippov relaxation.
\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}} (section~\ref{sec:control-foundations})
establishes existence of a minimizer by the direct method and the \emph{normality} of every
regular extremal, so that the reduction to the rail invariant is never vacuous.
\item \textbf{Proposition~\ref{prop:hjb}} (section~\ref{sec:control-foundations}) supplies
the non-autonomous Hamilton--Jacobi verification criterion that upgrades a saturated
extremal to a global minimizer, together with an explicit statement of what is
\emph{not} certified.
\item \textbf{Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}} (\ref{app:reproduce}) shows that the whole
adiabatic hierarchy collapses onto a single degree-ten polynomial identity, hence a
nonsingular $11\times11$ linear system whose solution is rational in the metric
parameters: every coefficient in the paper is solved for, none is fitted.
\item \textbf{Theorem~\ref{thm:firstorder}} (section~\ref{sec:vaidya-adiabatic}, proved in
\ref{app:firstorder}) gives the complete first-order response of the extremal to the slow
drift---on-shell \emph{and} off-shell---in a metric-independent form whose source collapses
to the universal boundary term $S_{\mathcal D}=[\,r\,p_r\,]-\lambda$.
\item \textbf{Lemma~\ref{lem:tracking}} (\ref{app:tracking}) computes the derivative of the
genus-degeneration family along the drifting locus and shows that it annihilates the
leading (triple) pole of the source.
\end{itemize}
The remaining sections are applications of this apparatus: section~\ref{sec:flrw} treats
the degenerate FLRW base, section~\ref{sec:vaidya} the ingoing Vaidya black hole, where
the closed forms are abelian integrals on a genus-two curve completed by a horizon
dilogarithm. Appendix~\ref{app:validation} records the reproducibility and consistency
data, \ref{app:degeneration} the explicit forms on the genus-degeneration loci, and
\ref{app:firstorder} the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:firstorder}.

\subsection{Rail constraint and the indicatrix}
\label{sec:indicatrix}
The rail is a constrained worldline with the invariant
\begin{equation}
    -\,u\cdot W \;=\; \Ehat ,
    \label{eq:rail}
\end{equation}
actively maintained by an ideal rail force $f_\mu=Du_\mu/\dd\tau$ with
$f\cdot u=0$ (automatic for a rest-mass--preserving force), which creates no rest
mass but is free to exert power against the selector (see below).

        symbol & meaning\\

        $t,\tau,v$ & coordinate, proper, advanced-null clocks\\

\paragraph{The ellipse.} Fix an event and a time function adapted to $W$. The
linear constraint~\eqref{eq:rail} fixes the $W$-component of $u$; the mass shell
$u\cdot u=-1$ is a quadric; their intersection, projected onto the physical
velocity plane $(\dd r,\,r\,\dd\varphi)$ per unit selector-time, is an ellipse
\begin{equation}
    \dot x(\theta)=c+R(\theta),\qquad \theta\in[0,2\pi),
    \label{eq:ellipse}
\end{equation}
whose center $c$ is the Zermelo wind (zero for FLRW, ingoing radial for Vaidya,
azimuthal---frame dragging---for Thakurta--Kerr) and whose axes $R$ shrink with
the local speed as the freezing surface is approached (figure~\ref{fig:indicatrici}).
Every cost clock---arrival/coordinate time $t$, proper time $\tau$, conformal time
$\eta$---shares this \emph{same} indicatrix; the branches differ only in the cost
one-form, i.e.\ in which clock measures the elapsed travel time. Two of these are
\emph{not} independent spatial families: since $\dd t=A(\eta)\,\dd\eta$ with $A>0$ is
a monotone reparametrization, the coordinate-time and conformal-time functionals are
extremized by the \emph{same} spatial path ($\arg\min t_f=\arg\min\eta_f$), so $\eta$
is the canonical conformal-time \emph{representation} of the arrival branch rather
than a third independent brachistochrone. The genuinely distinct branch is the proper
time $\tau$, whose cost carries the extra $\sqrt{1-v^2}$ weight; for Vaidya the native
arrival clock is the advanced null time $v$, again distinct from $\tau$.

\paragraph{Domain of the indicatrix.} The intersection~\eqref{eq:ellipse} is a
compact, strictly convex oval---the hypothesis under which the Pontryagin reduction
below applies---precisely where the selector $W$ is \emph{timelike} and the quadric
intersection is nondegenerate. Three loci require separate treatment. (i) At the
freezing surface the entire indicatrix collapses to a point, $R\to0$, and strict
convexity is lost; this degeneration is distinct from a radial turning point and
from a spectral separatrix, both treated separately below. (ii) At the horizon the
radial metric coefficient degenerates. (iii) For the rotating conformal
Thakurta--Kerr case, inside the \emph{ergosphere} $\partial_\eta$---hence
$W$---becomes spacelike and the mass-shell intersection is no longer automatically a
compact oval in the PMP sense; that regime, and the alternative timelike-congruence
re-anchoring it would require, is developed in the companion Paper~II. The local
theorems of this section are therefore stated in the timelike-$W$, nondegenerate
domain.

\paragraph{The optimal-control problem.} Precisely, let $x=(r,\varphi)$ denote the
spatial position and let $\chi$ be a monotone background time coordinate ($\chi=\eta$
for FLRW and $\chi=v$ for ingoing Vaidya). The full non-autonomous state is therefore
$y=(\chi,x)$, even when only its spatial projection is displayed. An arbitrary monotone
curve parameter is denoted by $\sigma$. The control is the unit spatial direction
$\theta\in S^1$ on the rail indicatrix~\eqref{eq:ellipse}; using $\chi$ as parameter gives
$\dd x/\dd\chi=f(x,\theta,\chi)$. A chosen clock $\mathcal T$ is the \emph{cost},
\[
    J_{\mathcal T}=\int \ell_{\mathcal T}(x,\theta,\chi)\,\dd\chi,
    \qquad \ell_{\mathcal T}=\frac{\dd\mathcal T}{\dd\chi}.
\]
Thus $\ell_{\mathcal T}=1$ when the cost clock itself is the evolution coordinate,
whereas for the Vaidya proper-time branch $\chi=v$ and
$\ell_\tau=\dd\tau/\dd v$. The boundary data are a \emph{fixed initial event}
$(\chi_0,x_0)$ and a \emph{fixed spatial target} $x_f$ (a point, or a target
set/worldline), with the terminal background time $\chi_f$ free and the corresponding
clock cost minimized. The Pontryagin transversality condition is
$H|_{\chi=\chi_f}=0$ [equation~\eqref{eq:transversality}]---a free-final-clock condition,
\emph{not} a two-event condition. We accordingly reserve ``fixed endpoints'' for a
two-point boundary-value problem (BVP), in which both spatial endpoint positions are
prescribed while the branch-dependent arrival clock is determined by the optimal
solution and remains the quantity being compared.

\paragraph{Standing hypotheses.} All statements labelled I.1--I.6 are made on a
\emph{regular domain}, by which we mean the following three conditions. They are the
precise form of the qualitative requirements listed above, and they are referred to
collectively as (H).

\begin{hypothesis}[timelike selector]\label{hyp:selector}
The selector $W$ is timelike on the open domain $\Omega$ under consideration, so that the
rail constraint $-u\cdot W=\Ehat$ cuts the unit-mass shell in a future-timelike locus.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}[above freezing]\label{hyp:convex}
The rail energy exceeds the selector norm pointwise on $\Omega$:
$\Ehat>|W|$, where $|W|=\sqrt{-W\cdot W}$.
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{hypothesis}[uniform margin]\label{hyp:coercive}
$\Omega$ is compact and the margin in Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:convex} is uniform,
$\inf_\Omega(\Ehat-|W|)>0$, the metric and the rail one-form being smooth on $\Omega$.
\end{hypothesis}

Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:selector}--\ref{hyp:coercive} are scalar inequalities, directly
checkable on any candidate domain; the geometric properties actually used below---that the
velocity indicatrix is a compact, strictly convex oval, and that the arrival-time
functional is two-sidedly bounded---are \emph{consequences}, derived in
Lemma~\ref{lem:indicatrix}. Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:convex} is what fails at the freezing
surface, Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:coercive} additionally at the horizon, and
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:selector} inside the ergosphere of the rotating case treated in
Paper~II. The three loci are distinct and are never conflated below.

\begin{auxlemma}[rail indicatrix]\label{lem:indicatrix}
Fix an event at which $W$ is timelike and let $\Ehat>0$. The set
$\mathcal I=\{u:\ u\cdot u=-1,\ u \text{ future-directed},\ -u\cdot W=\Ehat\}$ is
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] empty if $\Ehat<|W|$;
\item[(ii)] the single vector $u=W/|W|$ if $\Ehat=|W|$ \emph{(freezing)};
\item[(iii)] if $\Ehat>|W|$, a round sphere of radius $\sqrt{\Ehat^2/|W|^2-1}$ inside the
spacelike hyperplane $W^\perp$, hence compact, smooth and strictly convex.
\end{enumerate}
In case (iii) the induced set of physical velocities per unit selector time is the image of
that sphere under an invertible affine map, hence a compact, strictly convex ellipse: this
is~\eqref{eq:ellipse}, and its semi-axes vanish exactly as $\Ehat\downarrow|W|$.
Consequently Hypotheses~\ref{hyp:selector}--\ref{hyp:convex} imply the convexity properties
used throughout, and Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:coercive} implies the two-sided
bound~\eqref{eq:coercive} with $\rho,R$ the (uniform) inradius and circumradius of the
resulting ovals.
\end{auxlemma}

\begin{proof}
Set $n=W/|W|$, a unit timelike vector, and split $u=\gamma\,n+\bar w$ with
$\bar w\perp n$ spacelike. The rail constraint reads $-u\cdot W=|W|\,\gamma=\Ehat$, which
fixes $\gamma=\Ehat/|W|$ with no freedom left; the normalization
$u\cdot u=-\gamma^2+|\bar w|^2=-1$ then forces
\[
   |\bar w|^{2}=\gamma^{2}-1=\frac{\Ehat^{2}}{|W|^{2}}-1 .
\]
This is negative, zero or positive according as $\Ehat<|W|$, $\Ehat=|W|$ or $\Ehat>|W|$,
giving (i), (ii) and (iii); in case (iii) the locus is the sphere of radius
$\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}$ in the Euclidean space $W^\perp$, which is compact and strictly convex.
Passing to physical velocities divides the $W^\perp$ components by the fixed component
$\gamma$ and re-expresses them in the coordinate frame; both operations are invertible
linear maps at the given event, and the composition with the translation generated by the
non-orthogonality of $\partial_\chi$ and $n$ is an invertible affine map. The affine image
of a sphere is an ellipsoid, and affine maps preserve compactness and strict convexity, so
the physical velocity locus is a compact strictly convex ellipse whose centre is the
Zermelo wind. Its semi-axes are proportional to $\sqrt{\gamma^2-1}$ and hence vanish as
$\Ehat\downarrow|W|$. Finally the arrival-time Finsler function is the support-type
function of that ellipse, so its inradius and circumradius furnish exactly the constants
$\rho$ and $R$ of~\eqref{eq:coercive}; under Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:coercive} the margin is
uniform on the compact $\Omega$ and so are $\rho>0$ and $R<\infty$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the Vaidya case, explicitly]\label{rem:vaidya-indicatrix}
For ingoing Vaidya with the Kodama selector $W=K=\partial_v$ one has $|W|=\sqrt f$, so
Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:convex} reads $w\equiv\Ehat^2-f>0$. Substituting
$u=u^v\big(\partial_v+V^r\partial_r+(V^\varphi/r)\partial_\varphi\big)$ into
$g(u,u)=-1$ and $-u_v=\Ehat$ and eliminating $u^v$ gives, with no further assumption,
\[
   \big(V^r+(\Ehat^2-f)\big)^2+\Ehat^2\,(V^\varphi)^2=\Ehat^2\,w ,
\]
an ellipse centred at the ingoing radial wind $f-\Ehat^2<0$ with semi-axes $\Ehat\sqrt w$
and $\sqrt w$: this is precisely the parametrization~\eqref{eq:vaidya-ind} of
section~\ref{sec:vaidya}, and the freezing surface is $w=0$. Because the maximum of a
linear form over an ellipse of centre $c$ and semi-axes $(a_1,a_2)$ is
$p\cdot c+\sqrt{(a_1p_1)^2+(a_2p_2)^2}$, the support function of this oval is exactly the
branch Hamiltonian~\eqref{eq:Hv}, which is Theorem~\ref{thm:rail}(ii) made explicit. The
elimination, the match with~\eqref{eq:vaidya-ind} and the identification of the support
function with~\eqref{eq:Hv} were checked symbolically in \textsc{Mathematica}, all three
residuals reducing to zero identically.
\end{remark}

\Ithm{1}
\begin{theorem}[controlled-rail reduction]\label{thm:rail}
Assume (H). Then minimizing the arrival clock subject to the rail
constraint~\eqref{eq:ellipse} is a time-optimal control problem with control
$\theta\in S^1$, and:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] for every nonzero costate $p$ the maximizer of the support function
$p\cdot\dot x(\theta)$ over the indicatrix is unique, so the optimal control
$\theta^\star(x,p)$ is single-valued and smooth and no Filippov relaxation is required;
\item[(ii)] Pontryagin's maximum principle delivers the branch Hamiltonian
$H(x,p)=\max_\theta p\cdot\dot x(\theta)$ in closed form;
\item[(iii)] $p_\varphi=J$ is conserved, and the free-arrival-clock transversality
condition forces the branch Hamiltonian to vanish at the free endpoint,
\begin{equation}
    H\big|_{\chi=\chi_f}=0 \qquad\text{(free arrival time).}
    \label{eq:transversality}
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:convex} the velocity indicatrix~\eqref{eq:ellipse} is a compact,
strictly convex \emph{closed curve} (the boundary of a convex region), and the control is
its direction $\theta\in S^1$, itself a compact one-dimensional set. Maximizing the
support function $p\cdot\dot x(\theta)$ over this curve has a \emph{unique} maximizer by
strict convexity, which is (i): the control set $S^1$ is already compact and the maximizer
single-valued, so \emph{no} Filippov relaxation is needed. We do \emph{not} claim the
velocity locus is a convex \emph{subset} of the plane, nor convexify it (which would admit
averaged velocities violating the unit-mass shell). Excluding $p=0$---legitimate by the
normality statement of Theorem~\ref{thm:existence}, which is proved independently
below---the optimal control $\theta^\star(x,p)$ is single-valued and smooth away from the
freezing and horizon degeneracies, where Hypothesis~\ref{hyp:coercive} fails and which are
treated separately. Pontryagin's maximum principle (PMP) \cite{Pontryagin,Liberzon} then
delivers the branch Hamiltonian $H(x,p)=\max_\theta p\cdot\dot x(\theta)$, evaluated in
closed form in section~\ref{sec:vaidya}, which is (ii). Finally $\varphi$ is cyclic, so
$p_\varphi=J$ is conserved, and the terminal cost being the free arrival clock the
transversality condition of the PMP reads $H|_{\chi=\chi_f}=0$, which
is~\eqref{eq:transversality} and completes (iii).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[vakonomic, not nonholonomic]
This is a genuine time-optimal control problem, for which the Pontryagin
(vakonomic-type) reduction is the correct prescription; we do not invoke the nonholonomic
d'Alembert principle, whose equations differ from the vakonomic ones unless the constraint
distribution is integrable---a coincidence that convexity does not entail.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the branch Hamiltonian is not a first integral]
For a non-autonomous system $H$ is \emph{not} a global first integral: along the
extremal $\dd H/\dd\chi=\partial_\chi H$, proportional to $m'(v)$ (Vaidya) or $A'(\eta)$
(Thakurta--Kerr), so $H\neq0$ in the interior---consistently, $p_v(r_1)=0$ at the
free arrival radius yet $p_v(r)\neq0$ along the path (figure~\ref{fig:vaidya-traj}).
The globally conserved object is the extended Hamiltonian $\mathcal H=p_\chi+H=0$ on
the enlarged phase space, with $p_\chi$ conjugate to the background coordinate $\chi$; only
in the stationary limit is $H$ autonomous and $H\equiv0$ a genuine first integral.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Cost of control.} For an accelerated worldline with rail four-force
$a^\mu=Du^\mu/\dd\tau$, differentiating the invariant gives the \emph{exact}
identity
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd(-u\cdot W)}{\dd\tau}=-\,a\cdot W-u^a u^b\,\nabla_{(a}W_{b)},
    \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
whose two terms play distinct roles. The constraint $a\cdot u=0$ holds
\emph{automatically} for any four-force that preserves the mass shell
$u\cdot u=-1$ (it is the derivative of the shell condition); it is \emph{not} a
special ``magnetic'' property and does not by itself imply that the rail performs no
work relative to a physical observer. It means only that no rest mass is created;
the rail is nonetheless free to exert power \emph{against the selector} $W$ (the term
$a\cdot W$). One should distinguish (i) preservation of rest mass ($a\cdot u=0$),
(ii) power measured by a chosen observer congruence, (iii) the constraint force
itself, and (iv) the additional power needed to compensate non-stationarity.
A free geodesic ($a=0$) would let $-u\cdot W$ drift at $-u^au^b\nabla_{(a}W_{b)}$,
which vanishes for all $u$ iff $W$ satisfies the Killing equation; to hold
$-u\cdot W=\Ehat$ fixed the rail supplies exactly $a\cdot W=-u^au^b\nabla_{(a}W_{b)}$,
so that the left-hand side of~\eqref{eq:cost} is zero. This control power equals the
failure of the Killing equation---the missing symmetry---which is why the
non-stationary rail is legitimate but not free: $\Ehat$ is actively controlled, not
conserved by a symmetry. The optical reductions of $H$
reproduce the Randers/Zermelo structure
\cite{GibbonsRanders2009,CaponioWind}.

\paragraph{Operational reading: thrust.} Maintaining the controlled invariant generally
requires nonzero \emph{proper} acceleration $a^\mu=Du^\mu/\dd\tau$ ($a=0$ only on a
free-fall geodesic), so the rail force is the thrust per unit mass, spacelike
($a\cdot u=0$) with $|a|$ diverging as the freezing/horizon surface is approached. The
present variational problem minimizes the selected elapsed \emph{clock}, not thrust, fuel
or integrated control effort, which define distinct optimal-control problems. In the
non-stationary case the $a\cdot W$ term is the proper acceleration that maintains the rail
against the non-stationary mass-rate drift.

\paragraph{Geometric dictionary.} The indicatrix~\eqref{eq:ellipse} carries two
independent data---its \emph{center} $c=(c_r,c_\varphi)$ (the Zermelo wind) and
the \emph{eccentricity} of its semi-axes (the anisotropy of the local speed,
$a_r\neq a_\varphi$)---and each governs a distinct feature of the
brachistochrones, the same for every branch:
\begin{itemize}\itemsep2pt
\item eccentricity $\neq0$ (a spatial gradient $\partial_r f\neq0$)
$\Rightarrow$ the $t$ and $\tau$ branches split in depth, $n_t/n_\tau=E/f\neq1$;
\item azimuthal offset $c_\varphi\neq0$ (frame dragging)
$\Rightarrow$ rotational plunge inversion becomes possible;
\item radial offset $c_r\neq0$ (a non-stationary wind, $m'\neq0$)
$\Rightarrow$ endpoint-sensitive memory, $p_v\neq0$ along the path;
\item shrinking axes $R\to0$ (local speed $v\to0$) $\Rightarrow$ freezing and
capture.
\end{itemize}
Eccentricity and center offset are \emph{separate} levers: the first switches on
the $t/\tau$ split, the second the inversions. In particular the three branches
\emph{degenerate}---the $t$, $\tau$ and $\eta$ brachistochrones coincide---iff the
indicatrix is, up to a position-independent conformal scale, a rigid centered
circle ($c=0$, $a_r=a_\varphi$, radius depending on the flow parameter alone),
equivalently iff the optical cost ratios $n_t/n_\tau,\,n_t/n_\eta$ are
position-independent. This is exactly spatial homogeneity: FLRW is the unique
such case in our ladder (section~\ref{sec:flrw}), whereas any gradient or wind
breaks it---even the static, non-rotating Schwarzschild ellipse, which is
centered ($c=0$) yet eccentric, already splits $t$ from $\tau$. The indicatrix
shape is thus the organizing thread of the paper: FLRW (circle) $\to$ Vaidya
(radial wind) $\to$ Thakurta--Kerr (angular wind) is a progression of ellipse
deformations, each switching on one further phenomenon.

\subsection{Existence, normality, and global optimality}
\label{sec:control-foundations}
The Pontryagin reduction above supplies only \emph{necessary} conditions. We
record here the three complementary facts that make the constrained problem well
posed for the non-stationary rail: an existence theorem for the minimizer, the
normality of every regular extremal (so the reduction $-u\cdot W=\Ehat$ is never
vacuous), and a Hamilton--Jacobi certificate that upgrades a saturated extremal to
a \emph{global} optimum. All three are elementary under the standing hypothesis
that the indicatrix is a compact, uniformly convex oval [equation~\eqref{eq:ellipse}].

\Ithm{2}
\begin{theorem}[existence and normality; direct method]\label{thm:existence}
Assume (H), and let the spatial arc range over the compact regular domain $\Omega$. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:indicatrix} the arrival-time Finsler function then obeys the uniform
two-sided bound
\begin{equation}
  \rho\,|\nu| \le F(x,\nu) \le R\,|\nu|, \qquad 0<\rho\le R<\infty,
  \label{eq:coercive}
\end{equation}
with $\rho,R$ the inradius and circumradius of the ovals. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(i)] \emph{(existence)} the arrival time
$T[\gamma]=\int F(\gamma,\dot\gamma)\,\dd\sigma$ attains its infimum in the class of
Lipschitz arcs joining the fixed endpoints;
\item[(ii)] \emph{(normality)} every regular minimizer is a \emph{normal} extremal: the
abnormal multiplier $p_0$ cannot vanish, hence normalizes to $-1$, and the costate $p$
never vanishes along the extremal.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) Parametrize spatial arcs on $[0,1]$. The clock along an arc is not a datum but the
solution of an ordinary differential equation: by construction
\begin{equation}
   \frac{\dd\chi}{\dd\sigma}=F\big(x(\sigma),\dot x(\sigma),\chi(\sigma)\big),
   \qquad \chi(0)=\chi_0 ,
   \label{eq:clock-ode}
\end{equation}
and the cost is $T[x]=\chi(1)-\chi_0$. We argue in three steps.

\emph{A priori bounds.} Let $T_0$ be the cost of any admissible competitor. Along a
minimizing sequence, $\rho\,\mathrm{length}(x_n)\le T[x_n]\le T_0$ by the left inequality
of~\eqref{eq:coercive}, so the lengths are bounded by $\Lambda=T_0/\rho$. Reparametrizing
each $x_n$ proportionally to arclength gives $|\dot x_n|\equiv\mathrm{length}(x_n)\le\Lambda$
a.e., a uniform Lipschitz bound. The right inequality gives
$0\le\dd\chi/\dd\sigma\le R\Lambda$, so the clocks $\chi_n$ are uniformly Lipschitz and
uniformly bounded, and remain in a compact interval $[\chi_0,\chi_0+R\Lambda]$ on which,
with $\Omega$ compact, $F$ is smooth.

\emph{Gr\"onwall stability of the clock.} On $\Omega\times[\chi_0,\chi_0+R\Lambda]$ the
function $F$ is $C^1$, hence Lipschitz in its slow argument: there is $L<\infty$ with
$|F(x,\nu,\chi)-F(x,\nu,\chi')|\le L\,|\nu|\,|\chi-\chi'|$, the factor $|\nu|$ appearing
because $F$ is positively homogeneous of degree one in $\nu$. Let $\chi,\tilde\chi$ solve
\eqref{eq:clock-ode} for the same arc but different initial data, or for two arcs with the
same velocity field; subtracting and using the above,
\[
   \big|\chi(\sigma)-\tilde\chi(\sigma)\big|
   \le\big|\chi(0)-\tilde\chi(0)\big|
   +L\Lambda\!\int_0^{\sigma}\!\big|\chi-\tilde\chi\big|\,\dd\sigma' ,
\]
so Gr\"onwall's inequality gives
$\|\chi-\tilde\chi\|_\infty\le e^{L\Lambda}|\chi(0)-\tilde\chi(0)|$. In particular
\eqref{eq:clock-ode} has a unique solution for each arc, the solution map
$x\mapsto\chi_x$ is well defined, no trajectory escapes the compact set in which the
bounds were derived, and $T$ depends on the arc only through that map.

\emph{Existence.} Pass to the augmented state $y=(\chi,x)$ and the extended velocity set
\[
   \mathcal V(y)=\big\{(t,\nu):\ |\nu|\le\Lambda,\ \ F(x,\nu,\chi)\le t\le R\Lambda\big\},
\]
which is nonempty, compact and---because $F$ is convex in $\nu$, so that its epigraph is
convex---\emph{convex}, and depends continuously on $y$ by the smoothness just used. The
relaxed problem of minimizing $\chi(1)$ over trajectories of the differential inclusion
$\dot y\in\mathcal V(y)$ therefore satisfies the hypotheses of the Filippov--Cesari
existence theorem \cite{Cesari1983,Filippov1962}, and admits a minimizer. Since $\chi(1)$
is monotone in $t$, any minimizer saturates $t=F(x,\nu,\chi)$ almost everywhere, hence
solves~\eqref{eq:clock-ode} and is an admissible arc of the original problem with the same
cost. Coercivity forbids the infimum from being attained by escape, and the two problems
have the same infimum, so the minimizer of the relaxed problem is a minimizer of $T$.

(ii) Suppose $p_0=0$. The maximized Hamiltonian would then read $\max_\nu p\cdot\nu=0$
with $p\neq0$; but the support function of the oval obeys
$h(p)=\max_{\nu\in\partial B}p\cdot\nu\ge\rho\,|p|>0$ by the left inequality
of~\eqref{eq:coercive}, a contradiction. Hence $p_0$ normalizes to $-1$ and $p$ never
vanishes.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[relaxation of the epigraph is not convexification of the indicatrix]
\label{rem:no-convexification}
The convexity invoked in the existence proof is that of the \emph{epigraph}
$\{t\ge F(x,\nu,\chi)\}$ in the augmented variable $t$---the classical Cesari property---and
it is used only to close the minimizing sequence. It is \emph{not} a convexification of the
velocity indicatrix, which Theorem~\ref{thm:rail} deliberately avoids: replacing the oval
by its convex hull would admit averaged velocities violating the unit-mass shell, whereas
enlarging $t$ upwards only makes a competitor slower and is therefore discarded by the
minimization itself (the saturation step above). The two statements are consistent: no
relaxation survives in the minimizer.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[what normality buys, and where the hypotheses fail]
Part (ii) is what makes the reduction to the branch Hamiltonian
$H=\max_\theta p\cdot\dot x(\theta)$ together with the rail invariant $-u\cdot W=\Ehat$
legitimate along the \emph{entire} extremal, and it is the control-theoretic content of
``excluding $p=0$'' in the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:rail}: nontriviality is not an
assumption but a consequence of $\rho>0$. The two-sided bound~\eqref{eq:coercive} fails
exactly at the freezing/horizon surfaces, where $\rho\to0$ and the velocity indicatrix of
section~\ref{sec:indicatrix} degenerates; Theorem~\ref{thm:existence} therefore applies on
any compact subdomain bounded away from them.
\end{remark}

\Ithm{3}
\begin{proposition}[Hamilton--Jacobi verification]\label{prop:hjb}
Assume (H). The value function of the non-autonomous problem obeys, in the cost-to-go
convention, the Hamilton--Jacobi--Bellman equation
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\chi V+\min_{\theta\in S^1}\big\{\,\ell_{\mathcal T}(x,\theta,\chi)
  +\nabla_x V\cdot f(x,\theta,\chi)\,\big\}=0
  \label{eq:hjb}
\end{equation}
(equivalently, an autonomous HJB on the augmented state $y=(\chi,x)$; choosing
$\mathcal T=\chi$ gives $\ell_{\mathcal T}=1$ and the usual minimum-time form).
Let now $V$ be a Lipschitz \emph{calibration} on a region where it is single-valued,
that is, a viscosity subsolution of~\eqref{eq:hjb} in the forward (accumulated-cost)
convention:
\begin{equation}
  \partial_\chi V+\nabla_x V\cdot f(x,\theta,\chi)\;\le\;\ell_{\mathcal T}(x,\theta,\chi)
  \qquad\text{for \emph{every} }\theta\in S^1 .
  \label{eq:calibration}
\end{equation}
If a candidate PMP extremal \emph{saturates}~\eqref{eq:calibration}---equality along it,
which in the stationary case is $\nabla V\cdot\dot x=F$ pointwise---then that extremal
realizes the value function and is a \emph{global} minimizer on that region.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The maximum principle is necessary but not sufficient: a PMP extremal is a
\emph{candidate} minimizer, guaranteed only \emph{locally} optimal. The sufficient global
certificate is the calibration~\eqref{eq:calibration}. Let $(x(\cdot),\theta(\cdot))$ be
\emph{any} admissible competitor joining the data. Since~\eqref{eq:calibration} holds for
every $\theta$, it holds in particular for the competitor's own control, so
\[
   \frac{\dd}{\dd\chi}\,V\big(\chi,x(\chi)\big)
   =\partial_\chi V+\nabla_x V\cdot f\big(x,\theta(\chi),\chi\big)
   \;\le\;\ell_{\mathcal T}\big(x,\theta(\chi),\chi\big).
\]
Integrating from $\chi_0$ to the arrival $\chi_f$ gives
$V(\chi_f,x_f)-V(\chi_0,x_0)\le\int\ell_{\mathcal T}\,\dd\chi=J_{\mathcal T}$: the
increment of $V$ between the prescribed data is a lower bound for the cost of every
competitor. Along the candidate the inequality is an equality by hypothesis, so its cost
equals that lower bound and no competitor can beat it. Finally
$V(\chi_f,x_f)-V(\chi_0,x_0)$ therefore equals the value function, and the candidate
realizes it. In the stationary case~\eqref{eq:calibration} reduces to
$F^\ast(x,\nabla V)\le1$ with $F^\ast$ the dual (optical) norm, the familiar statement
that $V$ is a subsolution of the eikonal equation, and saturation is
$\nabla V\cdot\dot x=F$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[scope of the certificate]
We do \emph{not} construct such a global $V$ for generic non-stationary Kerr/Vaidya
trajectories; in the stationary limit the closed-form optical (Randers) metric of
section~\ref{sec:vaidya} supplies one on any region carrying a single-valued $V$, and
elsewhere our statements are those of PMP extremals with local checks. Where $V$ turns
multivalued the certificate localizes, as follows.
\end{remark}

\paragraph{Conjugate points, Maxwell set, cut locus.} Two mechanisms end
optimality. A \emph{conjugate point} (Jacobi) ends \emph{local} minimality when
the second variation degenerates; up to the first conjugate point the extremal is
a strict local minimum. A \emph{Maxwell point}---two distinct extremals of equal
arrival time---ends \emph{global} minimality earlier and is exactly a shock
(gradient discontinuity) of $V$, i.e.\ a point of the cut locus. Strict convexity
of the indicatrix removes Filippov relaxation but does \emph{not} exclude Maxwell
points: distinct windings past the rotation axis, or the two $\pm J$ arcs reaching
a common target, can tie. A closed-form extremal admitting a single-valued saturated
verification function is globally certified only up to its first Maxwell or conjugate
obstruction---the first such singularity of $V$. For the generic non-stationary
trajectories treated here we establish PMP extremality and local numerical minimality
checks, \emph{not} a complete global Hamilton--Jacobi certificate; beyond the first
obstruction the global optimum is selected by comparing arrival times across the competing
extremals.

\paragraph{What is \emph{not} claimed inside the ergosphere.} Where the Killing
selector $\xi$ becomes spacelike---inside the ergosphere---the invariant
$-u\cdot\xi=\Ehat$ no longer bounds a timelike-future cost and the indicatrix oval
degenerates: a closed-form $\varphi(r)$ continued through $\re$ is a genuine
\emph{analytic continuation} of the
extremal, not a certified physical minimum. The existence and Hamilton--Jacobi
statements above are asserted only outside the ergosphere, on the region where $F$
is a bona fide convex indicatrix; inside, a timelike observer congruence or a different
timelike selector would be required to formulate a compact future-timelike indicatrix,
which we do not develop. Freezing (where
$\rho\to0$), turning (where a semi-axis vanishes), and the separatrix (a double
root of the radical) are distinct degeneracies and are not conflated.

\subsection{Selecting $W$: the Killing / conformal-Killing / Kodama hierarchy}
\label{sec:whierarchy}
The selector $W$ is fixed by a hierarchy of decreasing symmetry, each level
recovering the previous one in the appropriate limit.

\emph{(a) Killing.} If a timelike Killing vector $\xi$ exists, $W=\xi$ and the
right-hand side of equation~\eqref{eq:cost} vanishes identically: $-u\cdot\xi$ is
Noether-conserved and the rail is free. This is Perlick's stationary case
\cite{Perlick1991}.

\paragraph{Recovery of Perlick's stationary optics.} In the Killing limit
$W=\xi=\partial_t$ the controlled-rail arrival-time Finsler function is exactly a
Randers metric, so the construction reduces to Perlick's fixed-energy optics rather
than merely resembling it. Solving the rail $-u\cdot\xi=\Ehat$ jointly with
$g(u,u)=-1$ for the coordinate-time rate $t'=\dd t/\dd\sigma$ gives a quadratic whose
future-directed root is
\begin{equation}
F_{\rm rail}=\beta_a \nu^a+\sqrt{a_{ab}\,\nu^a \nu^b},\quad
\beta_a=\frac{g_{ta}}{f},\quad
a_{ab}=\frac{\Ehat^2\bigl(g_{ta}g_{tb}+f\,g_{ab}\bigr)}{f^2(\Ehat^2-f)},
\label{eq:perlick-randers}
\end{equation}
with $f=-g_{tt}$ and $\nu^a=\dd x^a/\dd\sigma$. In the threading form
$g=-f(\dd t-\omega_a\dd x^a)^2+h_{ab}\dd x^a\dd x^b$ one has
$g_{ta}g_{tb}+f\,g_{ab}=f\,h_{ab}$, so $\beta_a=\omega_a$ and
$a_{ab}=\Ehat^2 h_{ab}/[f(\Ehat^2-f)]$---precisely Perlick's fixed-energy stationary
optical metric \cite{Perlick1991}. The null/high-energy limit $\Ehat\to\infty$ gives
$a_{ab}\to h_{ab}/f$, the standard Fermat--Randers optical metric of Kerr
\cite{GibbonsWerner2008}. This is the functional equality $F_{\rm rail}=F_{\rm Perlick}$
(stronger than coincidence of the Hamiltonian flows on $H=0$), verified against the
directly solved $F_{\rm rail}$ on equatorial Kerr to machine precision.

\emph{(b) Conformal Killing.} If the spacetime is conformally stationary,
$g=\Omega^2\hat g$ with $\hat g$ stationary, the timelike conformal Killing
vector $\partial_\eta$ selects the conformal-time branch; $-u\cdot W$ is exactly
conserved for null rays and controlled up to the conformal factor for massive
rails. FLRW and constant-$A$ Thakurta--Kerr live at this level.

\emph{(c) Kodama.} In a spherically symmetric \emph{dynamical} spacetime with no
Killing or conformal Killing vector, the Kodama vector
$K^a=\varepsilon^{ab}\nabla_b r$ \cite{Kodama1980,Hayward1996} is the canonical
replacement: it is divergence-free (equation~\eqref{eq:kodama}), reduces to $\xi$ in
the static limit, and its flux $G^{ab}K_b$ is conserved with charge the
Misner--Sharp mass. Vaidya is the paradigm (section~\ref{sec:kodama-energy}).

\emph{(d) Convention.} Absent even spherical symmetry the Kodama construction
fails and $W$ becomes a declared convention, with no distinguished normalization
of the cost one-form. Levels (a)--(c) are what make the non-stationary rail
canonical rather than arbitrary.

\section{FLRW: the degenerate base case}
\label{sec:flrw}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
For spatially flat FLRW, $g=a(\eta)^2\eta_{\rm Mink}$, the rail uses the
conformal Killing vector $\partial_\eta$; the local speed is
\begin{equation}
    v(\eta)=\sqrt{1-a(\eta)^2/\Ehat^{2}},
    \label{eq:flrwspeed}
\end{equation}
vanishing at the freezing barrier $a=\Ehat$.
\paragraph{Branch degeneracy.} Because $a=a(\eta)$ depends on conformal time
alone (spatial homogeneity), the optical index built from the conformal Killing
vector,
\begin{equation}
    n(\eta,r)=\frac1v=\frac{\Ehat}{\sqrt{\Ehat^2-a(\eta)^2}},
    \label{eq:flrwindex}
\end{equation}
is \emph{position-independent} at fixed $\eta$. The argument is then elementary and does
not even need Fermat's principle: the rail fixes the comoving speed to $v(\eta)$, so the
Euclidean arc length accumulated up to conformal time $\eta_f$ is
$\int_{\eta_0}^{\eta_f}v\,\dd\eta$, a strictly increasing function of $\eta_f$ alone.
Reaching the target therefore costs the least conformal time exactly when the comoving
path is the shortest, i.e.\ a straight line. The same curve minimizes $T_t$ and $T_\tau$
because on the rail \emph{every} clock is a function of $\eta$ alone: $\dd t=a\,\dd\eta$,
while the rail-fixed speed makes the Lorentz weight collapse,
\begin{equation}
   \frac{\dd\tau}{\dd\eta}=a\sqrt{1-v^2}=\frac{a^2}{\Ehat},
   \label{eq:flrw-dtau}
\end{equation}
so $t_f$ and $\tau_f$ are strictly increasing functions of $\eta_f$ and share its
minimizer. Hence
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
    \arg\min T_t&=\arg\min T_\tau=\arg\min T_\eta\\
    &=\text{comoving straight line}.
\end{split}
    \label{eq:flrwdegen}
\end{equation}
The $t/\tau$ splitting that drives every later result is a gravitational-%
\emph{gradient} effect ($\partial_r f\neq0$); it is absent whenever the metric
functions depend on time only. FLRW is thus the degenerate base of the ladder:
it exhibits freezing at $a=\Ehat$ but, under perturbation, no branch structure
(figure~\ref{fig:flrw-kin}a,\,b).

\subsection{Arbitrary spatial curvature}
\label{sec:flrw-curvature}
Nothing above used spatial flatness. Write the slices with arbitrary curvature
$k=+1,0,-1$ in the conformally static form
\begin{equation}
   g=a(\eta)^2\Big[-\dd\eta^2+\dd\chi^2+S_k(\chi)^2\dd\Omega^2\Big],
   \qquad S_k(\chi)=\sin\chi,\ \chi,\ \sinh\chi ,
   \label{eq:flrw-k}
\end{equation}
and keep the same conformal selector $W=\partial_\eta$. Then $W\cdot W=g_{\eta\eta}=-a^2$,
so
\begin{equation}
   |W|=a(\eta)\qquad\text{for every }k :
   \label{eq:Wnorm-k}
\end{equation}
the conformal factor multiplies the whole metric and $W$ points along the conformal time,
so the spatial curvature never reaches the norm of the selector. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:indicatrix} the indicatrix is therefore the same centred sphere of speed
$v(\eta)=\sqrt{1-a^2/\Ehat^2}$ as in the flat case, the freezing surface is again
$a=\Ehat$, and the clock relation~\eqref{eq:flrw-dtau} is unchanged. Every clock remains a
strictly increasing function of $\eta$ alone, so the branch degeneracy~\eqref{eq:flrwdegen}
persists and the minimiser is the shortest comoving path. Hence for all three curvatures
\begin{equation}
   \text{brachistochrone}=\text{spatial geodesic of the slice},
   \qquad
   \int_{\eta_0}^{\eta_f}v(\eta)\,\dd\eta=d_\gamma(x_0,x_f),
   \label{eq:flrw-geodesic}
\end{equation}
with $d_\gamma$ the geodesic distance of the constant-curvature $3$-metric: curvature
enters \emph{only} through that distance. The comoving shapes differ---great-circle arc,
straight line, hyperbolic geodesic---as shown in
figure~\ref{fig:flrw-curvature}(a).

The closed case is not merely cosmetic: its slices are compact, and it supplies the
simplest concrete instance of the obstruction that Proposition~\ref{prop:hjb} can only
describe in the abstract. Two points of $S^3$ at geodesic distance $\pi$---antipodes---are
joined by a whole $S^2$ of minimising geodesics, a textbook Maxwell set and cut locus.
Whether the rail can reach it is quantitative: with $\Delta_{\max}$ the comoving distance
accumulated up to freezing, the antipode is attainable iff $\Delta_{\max}\ge\pi$ in units
of the comoving curvature radius $R_{\rm c}$. For de Sitter,
$\Delta_{\max}=\big[\sqrt{\Ehat^2-1}+\arcsin(1/\Ehat)-\pi/2\big]/(\Ehat H)$, so the
threshold is a condition on $HR_{\rm c}$ alone
(figure~\ref{fig:flrw-curvature}(b)): a closed universe whose curvature radius is large
compared with the Hubble radius keeps the minimiser unique, while a small enough one lets
the extremal reach its own cut locus, where global optimality fails even though every
competing arc is a perfectly good Pontryagin extremal. The open case $k=-1$ adds no such
feature: the slices are non-compact, geodesics between two points are unique, and the
structure is that of the flat case throughout.

\section{Vaidya: dynamical black hole and the Kodama rail}
\label{sec:vaidya}
% ----------------------------------------------------------------------
The ingoing Vaidya metric $\dd s^2=-f\,\dd v^2+2\,\dd v\,\dd r+r^2\dd\Omega^2$,
$f=1-2m(v)/r$ \cite{Vaidya1951,LindquistSchwartzMisner1965}, has no timelike
Killing vector; its trapping structure is that of a dynamical horizon
\cite{AshtekarKrishnan2003,Booth2005,FaraoniBook}. The Kodama vector
\cite{Kodama1980,Hayward1996,AbreuVisser2010}
\begin{equation}
    K^a=\varepsilon^{ab}\nabla_b r = \partial_v ,\qquad
    \nabla_a K^a=0,
    \label{eq:kodama}
\end{equation}
is divergence-free without any Killing symmetry, and its charge is the
Misner--Sharp mass $m(v)$ \cite{MisnerSharp1964,HaywardMukohyama1999}.

\subsection{Kodama energy is the rail invariant}
\label{sec:kodama-energy}
With the canonical selector $K=\partial_v$, the rail constraint~\eqref{eq:rail} is
exactly $-u\cdot K=-u_v=\Ehat$, hence
\begin{equation}
    -\,u\cdot K \;=\; \Ehat \quad\text{identically along the brachistochrone.}
    \label{eq:kodama-conserved}
\end{equation}
This is \emph{controlled} conservation, not Noether: for a geodesic
$-u\cdot K$ would drift at rate $-m'(u^v)^2/r\propto m'$, exactly the
right-hand side of equation~\eqref{eq:cost} for $W=K$. Only the unnormalized
$K=\partial_v$ has zero control cost in the static limit; the normalized
$\hat K=K/\sqrt f$ does not (discriminating test), which is why the Kodama
vector, not a unit selector, is the canonical choice.

\paragraph{Indicatrix and Hamiltonians.} With $w\equiv\Ehat^2-f$ the constraint
$-u_v=\Ehat$ turns the admissible velocities at fixed $(v,r)$ into an ellipse,
\begin{equation}
    r'=(f-\Ehat^2)+\Ehat\sqrt w\,\cos\theta,\qquad
    \varphi'=\frac{\sqrt w}{r}\sin\theta,
    \label{eq:vaidya-ind}
\end{equation}
a Zermelo problem with ingoing radial wind $f-\Ehat^2<0$. Maximizing
$p\cdot\dot x$ over $\theta$ at fixed $p_\varphi=J$ gives the closed-form branch
Hamiltonians
\begin{align}
    H_v&=p_r(f-\Ehat^2)-1+\sqrt w\,\sqrt{\Ehat^2 p_r^2+J^2/r^2},
    \label{eq:Hv}\\
    H_\tau&=p_r(f-\Ehat^2)-\Ehat
        +\sqrt w\,\sqrt{(\Ehat p_r+1)^2+J^2/r^2}.
    \label{eq:Htau-vaidya}
\end{align}
The $\tau$-branch is the $v$-branch under $p_r\to p_r+1/\Ehat$ with unit cost
replaced by $\Ehat$. Non-stationarity is the explicit dependence
$\dd H/\dd v=\partial_v H\propto m'(v)$; the \emph{endpoint-sensitive} (arising from the
two-point boundary-value problem with a free future arrival---a costate memory, not a
causal effect) momentum $p_v=-H$
satisfies $p_v(r_1)=0$ at the free arrival radius yet $p_v(r)\neq0$ along the
path, so the turning point \emph{anticipates future accretion}
(figure~\ref{fig:vaidya-traj}).

\paragraph{Branch costate vs mechanical angular momentum.} The conserved $J=p_\varphi$
is the Noether \emph{costate} of the reduced control Hamiltonian---the canonical
momentum conjugate to $\varphi$ in the arrival-time problem---\emph{not} the spacetime
angular momentum $L_{\rm mech}=g(u,\partial_\varphi)$. Although $\partial_\varphi$ is a
Killing vector, Noether's theorem for the \emph{control} symmetry returns the control
costate, not the mechanical momentum: along a forced (rail-constrained, accelerated)
worldline $L_{\rm mech}$ is \emph{not} conserved, since
$\dd L_{\rm mech}/\dd\tau=a\cdot\partial_\varphi\neq0$. The two are therefore distinct
objects. On the native Vaidya $v$-branch shell the Legendre map gives the exact
relation
\begin{equation}
    L_{\rm mech}=r^2 u^\varphi=\frac{\Ehat^2-f(r)}{\Ehat}\,J,
    \label{eq:costate-mech}
\end{equation}
which varies with $r$ while $J$ stays constant (they agree only at the isolated radius
$f=\Ehat^2-\Ehat$); verified from the indicatrix~\eqref{eq:vaidya-ind}. The Legendre
map is branch-specific, but on all Vaidya branches the conserved axial control costate is
$J$ and the slow deformation is generated solely by $m\partial_m$; we reserve ``angular
momentum'' for the mechanical $g(u,\partial_\varphi)$ and call $J$ the branch costate,
$L_{\rm mech}$ being a derived, non-conserved quantity.

\paragraph{On-shell drift versus off-shell shell departure.} For the Vaidya family the frozen orbit shape
$\mathrm{d}\varphi/\mathrm{d}r=F(r;\Ehat,J)$ depends on the mass modulus $m$, and its
on-shell drift is generated by $m\partial_m$. Conservation of the Kodama rail charge
$\Ehat$ does \emph{not} constrain the non-autonomous trajectory to the instantaneous
$H=0$ shell---the costate equation is independent information---so the \emph{complete}
first-order response adds the $p_v$-driven off-shell contribution
[equation~\eqref{eq:adiab-exact}, section~\ref{sec:vaidya-adiabatic},
Figure~\ref{fig:vaidya-offshell}].


\subsection{Phenomenology: penetration, timing, bounce}
\label{sec:vaidya-pheno}
Three features distinguish the dynamical hole from its frozen-mass
Schwarzschild limit.

\emph{Penetration.} The $\tau$-brachistochrone reaches the apparent horizon
$r=2m(v)$ only if $|J|$ lies below a threshold $\Jpen(v_0)$; accretion ($m'>0$)
opens a \emph{finite} window $\Jpen>0$, whereas the negative-rate continuation
($m'<0$ in the \emph{ingoing} metric---a formal continuation; physical evaporation is
the \emph{outgoing} metric, section~\ref{sec:vaidya-plunge}) closes it to zero
measure---an accretion/(negative-rate) asymmetry with no static analogue. The
finite capture interval $J\in(0,\Jpen]$ is the analogue of the Kerr $t$-branch
dichotomy, generated here by the \emph{dynamics}: grazing orbits linger near
periapsis while the growing horizon closes the gap.

\emph{Bounce.} The $r$-parametrization degenerates at periapsis
($\dd v/\dd r\to\infty$); reparametrizing by $v$ makes the Euler--Lagrange
problem regular at the turning point $r'=0$, so the trajectory is integrated
straight \emph{through} the bounce---the same extremal, via the exact change of
variable $(f v'-1)\dd r=(f-r')\dd v$. The $v$-flow reaches the periapsis exactly
where the $r$-flow diverges ($r_{\min}=3.0105$ at $v=7.29$ for $\mu\equiv m'=0.01$),
and the endpoint-sensitive memory $p_v(r)\propto m'$ measures the departure from the
frozen-mass Schwarzschild curve (figure~\ref{fig:vaidya-bounce}).

\emph{Timing and the orbit--horizon race.} Because $m$ grows along the path,
$r_{\min}$ depends on \emph{when} the particle arrives, the epoch $v_1$---a
genuinely non-stationary effect absent in Kerr. The absolute and
relative penetration have \emph{opposite} winners
(figure~\ref{fig:vaidya-timing}): under the negative-rate branch ($m'<0$) the absolute
periapsis descends but the horizon $2m(v)$ retreats faster, so the relative penetration
$r_{\min}/2m(v_{\rm peri})$ \emph{worsens} for later arrivals; under accretion the
periapsis rises yet the horizon grows faster, so late arrivals graze the relative
horizon. The orbit--horizon chase has reversed outcomes measured in $r$ versus in
horizon units.

\subsection{Plunge-radius law and the absence of inversion in the frozen-clock comparison}
\label{sec:vaidya-plunge}
The turning-point law has the static Schwarzschild form evaluated at the mass
at periapsis, $f_*=1-2m(v_{\rm peri})/r_{\min}$:
\begin{align}
    \tau:&\quad (\Ehat^2-f_*)J^2 = f_* r_{\min}^2 ,\\
    t:&\quad (\Ehat^2-f_*)J^2 = \Ehat^2 r_{\min}^2/f_* ,
    \label{eq:vaidya-turn}
\end{align}
plus a genuine $O(m')$ correction from the endpoint-sensitive costate memory $p_v$. The
static ratio $J^2_t/J^2_\tau=\Ehat^2/f^2>1$ gives $r_{\min}^t<r_{\min}^\tau$
(``$t$ deeper''), a \emph{branch-intrinsic} ordering set by $\Ehat/f>1$ alone---it
does not depend on the sign of $m'$ nor on the ingoing/outgoing character of the
flux. \emph{Physical} evaporation is the \emph{outgoing} Vaidya metric
$\dd s^2=-f\dd u^2-2\dd u\,\dd r$ (retarded time $u$), not the ingoing metric with
$m'<0$. The depth ordering $r_{\min}^t<r_{\min}^\tau$ is \emph{branch-intrinsic}: it
follows from $\Ehat/f>1$ alone and is therefore independent of the sign of $m'$ and of
the ingoing/outgoing character of the flux, so to this (static, frozen-clock) order it
is unchanged under evaporation. The apparent linear-model ``inversion'' from flipping
$m'<0$ in the \emph{ingoing} model is an unphysical extrapolation to $m\le0$. We do
\emph{not} here solve the full outgoing optimal-control problem with its own
Hamiltonians, costates and transversality: the advanced/retarded frozen-clock asymmetry
[equation~\eqref{eq:vaidya-asymmetry}] is a clock decomposition ($v\!\leftrightarrow\!u$,
the tortoise $r_*$ changing sign) of the frozen first-order correction, not a complete
outgoing boundary-value solution. Accordingly we state the absence of an evaporative
inversion as a property of the branch-intrinsic static ordering together with this
frozen clock decomposition, and leave the full outgoing BVP---and a genuine outgoing
trajectory to accompany figure~\ref{fig:vaidya-noinv}---to future work.

\subsection{Closed-form adiabatic correction and the horizon dilogarithm}
\label{sec:vaidya-adiabatic}
When $m(v)$ varies slowly we apply the general frozen-coefficient reduction of
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} and the first-order response of Theorem~\ref{thm:firstorder} to the Vaidya mass modulus, with
$\dot m=\dd m/\dd v$ the small rate and $\varepsilon=M\dot m/m$ its dimensionless
bookkeeping parameter. In the spectral formulae from this point onward we abbreviate
$E\equiv\Ehat$ and define the single polynomial factor
$D_E\equiv(E^2-1)r+2m$. At a fixed reference slice the two normalizations satisfy
$\delta\varphi/\varepsilon=(m/M)\,\delta\varphi/\dot m$. The frozen family
is the \emph{Schwarzschild} brachistochrone---the $a\to0$ limit of the
Thakurta--Kerr forms---on the sextic
$S=r(r-2m)D_E\,[r^2(r-2m)-J^2 D_E]$. Concretely it is the \emph{proper-time} branch
$H_\tau$ of~\eqref{eq:Htau-vaidya}, on the orientation of increasing $r$ that renders
$\sqrt S$ positive: solving $H_\tau=0$ for $p_r$ and forming
$\varphi'=H_{\tau,J}/H_{\tau,p_r}$, $\dd v/\dd r=1/H_{\tau,p_r}$ and $\dd\tau/\dd r$
returns exactly the three spectral expressions used below,
$\varphi'=J D_E/\sqrt S$, equation~\eqref{eq:vaidya-clock} and
$\dd\tau/\dd r=r^2(r-2m)/\sqrt S$ (checked to $10^{-25}$ at several radii). Notably this
sextic keeps six distinct roots: the constrained brachistochrone is genus \emph{two} even in
Schwarzschild (only the geodesics are elliptic; the genus drop occurs at the algebraic
degeneration value $|J|=J_{\rm deg}$). The mass derivative reduces exactly, the horizon factor cancelling,
\begin{equation}
    \partial_m F=\frac{N_m}{S^{3/2}},\quad N_m=S\,\partial_m K-\tfrac12 K\,\partial_m S,
    \qquad
    \partial_m\varphi_0=\frac{\mathcal{A}^m}{\sqrt S}+\sum_{k=0}^4 c_k^m U_k,
    \label{eq:dm-reduction}
\end{equation}
with $U_k=\int r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r$ and \emph{algebraic} constants $c_k^m$ (verified
to $10^{-15}$). The clock is now the \emph{advanced time}: from $v=t+r_*$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\dd v}{\dd r}=\frac{E}{f}\frac{\dd\tau}{\dd r}+\frac1f
    =\frac{E\,r^3}{\sqrt S}+\frac{r}{r-2m}
    \;\Rightarrow\;
    v=E\,U_3+r+2m\ln(r-2m),
    \label{eq:vaidya-clock}
\end{equation}
the tortoise term injecting a \emph{horizon logarithm}. This is the advanced-time
(native) branch; the \emph{proper-time} branch instead uses the second-kind clock
$\dd\tau/\dd r=r^2(r-2m)/\sqrt S$, i.e.\ $T_\tau=U_3-2m\,U_2$, $b=(0,0,-2m,1,0)$ (no
horizon log), and closes by the very same
universal on-shell assembly established here (specialized to the Vaidya clock)---$\delta\varphi_\tau/\dot m=\tfrac12\sum_{k<j}Q_{kj}
W_{kj}-T_\tau(\mathcal{A}^m/\sqrt S+\tfrac12\sum c_k^m U_k)+\sum_j b_j\mathcal{I}_j$ with
$Q_{kj}=c_k^m b_j-c_j^m b_k$, all coefficients algebraic (verified to
$10^{-14}$). The two canonical clocks of the ingoing problem are therefore the
\emph{advanced null time} $v$ (adapted to the Kodama selector $K=\partial_v$, the
native evolution parameter) and the \emph{proper time} $\tau$; the coordinate time
$t$ that enters the static depth comparison~\eqref{eq:vaidya-turn} is the
\emph{frozen} Schwarzschild-like time, a comparison device valid at frozen order and
\emph{not} a canonical non-autonomous clock---once $m=m(v)$ the tortoise
$r_*=r+2m\ln(r-2m)$ acquires $\dd m$ terms and no Killing time survives. The retarded
time $u=t-r_*$ of the outgoing metric is a \emph{separate} outgoing-Vaidya problem
(its own Hamiltonians, costates, and transversality), not a third clock of the ingoing
family. The advanced-time first-order correction
$\delta\varphi=\dot m\!\int\!\partial_m F\,v\,\dd r$ then integrates by parts into a
fully explicit closed form (verified to $9\times10^{-14}$, coefficients given),
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\delta\varphi}{\dot m}
    =A_m\,v-E\!\int\! A_m\,\frac{r^3}{\sqrt S}\,\dd r
    -\int\! A_m\,\frac{r}{r-2m}\,\dd r,\qquad A_m\equiv\partial_m\varphi_0,
    \label{eq:vaidya-deltaphi}
\end{equation}
This is the \emph{on-shell} component of the Vaidya first-order correction (the
mass-drift of the frozen family).

\Ithm{5}
\begin{theorem}[complete first-order response]\label{thm:firstorder}
Assume (H) and let the background depend on a single slow parameter with dimensionless
rate $\varepsilon$. Linearizing the extended Hamiltonian $\mathcal H=p_\chi+H=0$ about the
frozen extremal, in the original canonical variables $(r,p_r)$ with $\lambda$ the elapsed
clock measured from the free-arrival endpoint, the momentum-shell shift is
$\delta p_r=(\lambda\,\Theta H-S_{\mathcal D})/H_{p_r}$ and the shape correction is
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    &\frac{\delta\varphi}{\varepsilon}=\int\!\Big[\frac{\partial_{p_r}G}{H_{p_r}}\,
    \big(\lambda\,\Theta H-S_{\mathcal D}\big)-\lambda\,\Theta G\Big]\dd r,\qquad
    G=\frac{H_J}{H_{p_r}}=\varphi',\\[2pt]
    &S_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)=\!\int_0^{\lambda}\!\mathcal D H\,\dd\lambda',
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:adiab-exact}
\end{equation}
which closes the correction to $O(\varepsilon^2)$, uniformly on any compact sub-arc on
which $|H_{p_r}|$ is bounded away from zero. Here $\Theta$ is the Euler operator
generating the frozen-family drift and $\mathcal D$ is the drift generator, equal to
$\Theta$ unless the slow parameter rescales the canonical momenta. For single-parameter
Vaidya, $\mathcal D=\Theta=m\partial_m$ and the terminally-anchored source collapses to
the universal boundary form
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathcal D}=[\,r\,p_r\,]-\lambda .
    \label{eq:universal-boundary}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The derivation is metric-independent and is carried out, then closed explicitly for the
two Vaidya branches, in~\ref{app:firstorder}; the boundary form~\eqref{eq:universal-boundary}
follows there from the Randers--Euler identity together with the degree-zero
self-similarity of the frozen Hamiltonian, and the $O(\varepsilon^2)$ remainder from a
Gr\"onwall estimate on the sub-arc.
\end{proof}

As in Thakurta--Kerr, the interior costate does not
vanish---$p_v=-H_v=O(\dot m)$ with $p_v(r_1)=0$ only at the free arrival---so the
\emph{complete} first-order term adds the off-shell piece given by the Vaidya analogue
of equation~\eqref{eq:adiab-exact}, with the single-parameter operator $\Theta=m\partial_m$
and $S_{\mathcal D}=\int_0^\lambda\Theta H_v\,\dd\lambda'$ terminally anchored; the extra term is
$-\!\int(\partial_{p_r}G/H_{p_r})\,S_{\mathcal D}\,\dd r$, a length-two iterated integral of the
same horizon-dilogarithm class. Applying the identical canonical perturbation theory
to $H_v$ closes the Vaidya first order to $O(\dot m^2)$ (residual slope $\approx2$;
\texttt{VaidyaMetric/vaidya\_first\_order\_offshell.py}). We display the on-shell
component in closed form below;
its two integrals expand into named building blocks with algebraic coefficients.
Writing $A_m=\mathcal{A}^m/\sqrt S-\alpha_0+\sum_k c_k^m U_k$ with the constant
$\alpha_0=\mathcal{A}^m(r_0)/\sqrt{S(r_0)}$, integration by parts in each factor gives
the \emph{fully assembled} closed form (verified to $2\times10^{-13}$)
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
    \frac{\delta\varphi}{\dot m}
    ={}&A_m\,v
    -E\Big[\mathcal{I}_{\rm poly}-\alpha_0 U_3
        +\tfrac12\sum_{k=0}^4 c_k^m\big(U_kU_3-W_{3k}\big)\Big]\\
    &{}-\Big[\mathcal{L}_{2m}-\alpha_0\,\big(v-E\,U_3\big)\Big|_{r_0}^{\,r}
        +\sum_{k=0}^4 c_k^m\big(r\,U_k-U_{k+1}+2m\,\mathcal{D}_k\big)\Big],
\end{aligned}
    \label{eq:vaidya-full}
\end{equation}
All blocks below are normalized to \emph{vanish at the lower limit} $r_0$, i.e.\ every
integral is $\int_{r_0}^{r}$; this is what makes the boundary term of the integration by
parts reduce to $A_m(r)\,v(r)$, since $A_m(r_0)=0$ by the choice of $\alpha_0$. The one
elementary primitive that is not of this form is the tortoise term, which is why it is
displayed with an explicit bracket $\big|_{r_0}^{\,r}$: evaluating it at the upper limit
alone would spoil the identity by the constant
$\alpha_0\,[r_0+2m\ln(r_0-2m)]$. With that convention the assembled
right-hand side reproduces the quadrature of~\eqref{eq:vaidya-deltaphi} to $10^{-39}$ at
$m=1$, $E=7/5$, $J=5/2$, $r_0=5$, $r=9$ (forty-digit arithmetic).

The named building blocks are: the abelian integrals $U_k=\int r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r$;
the elementary rational-log primitive
$\mathcal{I}_{\rm poly}=\int\mathcal{A}^m r^3/S\,\dd r$; the genus-two
polylogarithms $W_{3k}=\int(U_3\dd U_k-U_k\dd U_3)$ \emph{at infinity};
the \emph{second}-kind horizon integral
$\mathcal{L}_{2m}=\int\mathcal{A}^m r/[(r-2m)\sqrt S]\,\dd r$---since
$\mathcal{A}^m(2m)\ne0$ its integrand behaves as $(r-2m)^{-3/2}$, a double pole with
\emph{no} residue at the horizon Weierstrass point, closed by the Kleinian
$\zeta$/quasi-periods~\cite{BuchstaberEnolskii1997,BakerAbelian1897}, not by Fay; the
genuinely \emph{third}-kind object is the horizon letter $\dd r/(r-2m)$ (the tortoise
log, a simple pole of residue two at that Weierstrass point), which closes as a Fay
$\theta$-ratio~\cite{Fay1973} centred at $r=2m$; and
the \emph{horizon dilogarithms} $\mathcal{D}_k=\int U_k/(r-2m)\,\dd r$. Since $r=2m$
is a branch point of $S$, in the local uniformiser $t=\sqrt{r-2m}$ one has
$\dd r/(r-2m)=2\,\dd t/t$: a \emph{simple} pole of residue two, i.e.\ a genuine
third-kind letter $\dd\log$ anchored \emph{at the horizon}. On the compact
genus-two curve this letter carries the divisor $2\,(r{=}2m)-\infty_+-\infty_-$ (residues
summing to zero) and closes as a Fay prime-form $\theta$-ratio; one integration by parts
then closes $\mathcal{D}_k$ onto a \emph{single} weight-two letter (verified to
$10^{-12}$). Displayed horizon logarithms carry a fixed reference length,
$\ln(r-2m)\equiv\ln[(r-2m)/L]$, which cancels in every assembled result.
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{D}_k=U_k\,\ln(r-2m)-\mathcal{G}_k,\qquad
    \mathcal{G}_k=\int\ln(r-2m)\,\frac{r^k}{\sqrt S}\,\dd r,
    \label{eq:horizon-dilog}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{G}_k$ is the horizon-anchored genus-two dilogarithm---the exact
counterpart of the Kerr weight-two piece $W_{jk}$, in the same
hyperelliptic-dilogarithm class (conjecturally irreducible to weight one, the
theta-divisor obstruction $\Theta=W_{g-1}$ preventing the genus-one factorization),
but with its $\dd\log$ letter sourced by the
advanced-time divergence at the horizon $r=2m$ rather than by a third-kind dipole at
infinity. At the genus-degeneration $|J|=J_{\rm deg}$ the sextic acquires a double root at $r=r_d$
and the genus drops to one: the $U_k$ reduce to elliptic integrals and $\mathcal{G}_k$
collapses to the tabulated elliptic (Bloch--Wigner/Zagier) dilogarithm
\cite{Bloch2000,Zagier1990} on $E:\,w^2=Q_4(r)$. For spherical Vaidya this locus is
\emph{algebraic} ($r_d<0$, a double root off the physical arc, not an external
separatrix, and not to be conflated with the dynamical penetration threshold
$\Jpen(v_0)$); the explicit Weierstrass $\sigma,\zeta$ reduction, with representative
numerical values, is given in \ref{app:degeneration}. Every coefficient is algebraic
(rational in $m,E,J$); the transcendence lives entirely in $W_{jk},\mathcal{G}_k$, so the
correction is reusable by direct evaluation once $S$ and the $c_k^m$ are fixed.

\paragraph{Explicit degeneration-family correction.} On the algebraic genus-degeneration
locus $J=J_{\rm deg}$ (a double root at $r_d<0$, not a physical separatrix) the correction
reduces to a finite basis of standard letters. The antiderivative of the source is a sum
of Weierstrass letters,
\begin{equation}
    G(z)=\int\partial_m F=C_0\,z+\sum_a\Big[b_1^a\ln\sigma(z-a)-b_2^a\zeta(z-a)
        -\tfrac12 b_3^a\wp(z-a)\Big],
    \label{eq:vaidya-sep-G}
\end{equation}
with every residue $b_n^a$ closed in algebraic form over the poles
$a\in\{\pm z_d,0,i\omega_{\rm im}\}$, while the weight-two blocks $W_{kj}$ are length-two
iterated (Brown--Levin elliptic-polylogarithm~\cite{BrownLevin2011}) integrals evaluated by
a convergent $q$-series. \ref{app:degeneration} gives the algebraic reduction,
Mittag--Leffler block structure and residue formulae. Construction of the period data and
numerical Kronecker--Eisenstein/$q$-series evaluation use the archived routine. Every
coefficient is an algebraic function of $(m,E)$, so
$\delta\varphi_\tau|_{\rm deg}$ is reconstructed on any curve by evaluating these formulae
(checked to $10^{-9}$ against the direct integral across $E\in[1.3,1.6]$). The minimal
dilogarithm-basis dimension and the irreducibility of the weight-two letters remain
\emph{conjectural}; a secondary numerical rank experiment is in the reproducibility
package.

\paragraph{Advanced/retarded frozen-clock decomposition.} At first order $\delta\varphi$ is
linear in $\dot m$, so na\"ively $\dot m\to-\dot m$ merely flips its sign. The
formal advanced/retarded frozen-clock asymmetry is subtler: accretion ($\dot m>0$) is
described by the \emph{ingoing} Vaidya model and its \emph{advanced} clock $v=t+r_*$,
whereas the \emph{retarded} clock $u=t-r_*$ ($\dd s^2=-f\dd u^2-2\dd u\,\dd r$) belongs to
the outgoing geometry. The frozen reduction $\partial_m F$ is identical
(it depends only on $S$); only the clock changes, and $v\leftrightarrow u$ flips the
sign of the tortoise $r_*$. Splitting the clock into its bulk and tortoise parts,
$v=E\,U_3+r_*$ with $r_*=(r-r_0)+2m\ln\!\frac{r-2m}{r_0-2m}$,
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\delta\varphi}{\dot m}\Big|_{\rm adv}=\mathcal{A}_\infty+\mathcal{B}_{\rm hor},
    \quad
    \frac{\delta\varphi}{\dot m}\Big|_{\rm ret}=\mathcal{A}_\infty-\mathcal{B}_{\rm hor},
    \quad
    \mathcal{A}_\infty=\!\int\!\partial_m F\,E\,U_3\,\dd r,\;\;
    \mathcal{B}_{\rm hor}=\!\int\!\partial_m F\,r_*\,\dd r,
    \label{eq:vaidya-asymmetry}
\end{equation}
so that the retarded frozen-clock branch is
$\delta\varphi_{\rm ret}=\dot m(\mathcal{A}_\infty-\mathcal{B}_{\rm hor})$; at equal
rate magnitude the \emph{formal advanced/retarded frozen-clock difference} is
$\delta\varphi_{\rm adv}-\delta\varphi_{\rm ret}=2|\dot m|\,\mathcal{A}_\infty$
(carried by the infinity bulk), while the \emph{deviation from exact antisymmetry} is
$\delta\varphi_{\rm adv}+\delta\varphi_{\rm ret}=2|\dot m|\,\mathcal{B}_{\rm hor}$,
carried \emph{entirely by the tortoise/horizon}: the horizon term $\mathcal{B}_{\rm hor}$
is exactly what breaks the na\"ive $\dot m\to-\dot m$ antisymmetry. This
advanced/retarded split is a \emph{frozen-clock} comparison at first order, isolating the
sign change of the tortoise contribution; it is \emph{not} a solution of the full
outgoing-Vaidya optimal-control boundary-value problem, whose Hamiltonians, costates and
transversality remain to be derived. Within
$\mathcal{B}_{\rm hor}$ the transcendental, direction-sensitive content is precisely
the horizon term $2m\!\int\!\partial_m F\ln(r-2m)\,\dd r$, which by parts is the
horizon dilogarithm $\mathcal{D}_k$ of equation~\eqref{eq:horizon-dilog}. Thus the
advanced/retarded frozen-clock asymmetry isolates the contribution of the
horizon-anchored weight-two sector. For
$m=1,E=1.4,J=2.5$: $\mathcal{A}_\infty=8.7276$, $\mathcal{B}_{\rm hor}=6.1127$ (of which
$1.5301$ is the horizon-log dilogarithm sector), giving
$\delta\varphi/\dot m|_{\rm adv}=14.840$ against
$\delta\varphi/\dot m|_{\rm ret}=2.615$ (both verified to $10^{-15}$); the frozen-clock
difference is then $2\mathcal{A}_\infty=17.455$ and the deviation from antisymmetry
$2\mathcal{B}_{\rm hor}=12.225$ (each $\times|\dot m|$).

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:concl}
\paragraph{Proved.} The non-stationary brachistochrone is a well-posed controlled-rail
problem on the regular domain where the selector is timelike and the velocity indicatrix
is nondegenerate: the invariant $-u\cdot W=\Ehat$ is legitimate via Pontryagin's principle, with
$W$ fixed by the Killing / conformal-Killing / Kodama hierarchy and the Kodama energy conserved along
the Vaidya rail even when free fall does not. Existence, normality, and the
non-autonomous Hamilton--Jacobi verification criterion hold on the timelike-selector
domain. FLRW is the degenerate base---freezing without branch splitting---while in
Vaidya the mass breaks the $t/\tau$ symmetry through its radial gradient.

\paragraph{Conditional and numerical.} The ingoing plunge phenomenology (penetration,
timing, bounce) and the absence of an inversion in the frozen advanced/retarded clock
comparison hold; the negative-rate continuation ($m'<0$ in the ingoing metric) is a formal
continuation, not physical evaporation (which belongs to the outgoing metric). The
first-order adiabatic correction closes in a horizon dilogarithm and is verified against
the true non-autonomous flow to $O(\varepsilon^2)$; its off-shell source has the
universal boundary form $S_{\mathcal D}=[\,rp_r\,]-\lambda$.

\paragraph{Outlook.} The full outgoing-Vaidya boundary-value problem---with its own
Hamiltonians, costates and transversality---remains open, as does a genuine third
(Kodama-observer) clock. The rotating Thakurta--Kerr case, its ergosphere trichotomy,
closed-form separatrices and conformal plunge inversion, is developed in the companion
paper. More broadly, the controlled-rail brachistochrone turns the comparison of
arrival-time and proper-time optimal worldlines into a quantitative probe of how
homogeneous expansion, radial spatial gradients and time-dependent mass flow shape causal
transport in dynamical black-hole spacetimes.

\subsection{Reproducing the reductions}
\label{app:reproduce}
\Ithm{4}
\begin{lemma}[polynomial reduction]\label{lem:reduction}
Let the slow derivative of the shape integrand have the form
$\partial_\lambda F=N_\lambda/S^{3/2}$ with $S$ the sextic radicand and $N_\lambda$
polynomial. Then the depth-one reduction
$\partial_\lambda\varphi_0=\mathcal{A}/\sqrt S+\sum_{k=0}^4 c_k U_k$
[equation~\eqref{eq:dm-reduction}] holds if and only if
\begin{equation}
    2\,\mathcal{A}'(r)\,S(r)-\mathcal{A}(r)\,S'(r)
    +2\,S(r)\sum_{k=0}^4 c_k\,r^k \;=\; 2\,N_\lambda(r),
    \qquad \mathcal{A}=\sum_{i=0}^5 a_i r^i,
    \label{eq:reduction-identity}
\end{equation}
a polynomial identity of degree ten. Matching the eleven coefficients of
$r^0,\dots,r^{10}$ gives an $11\times11$ linear system $M$ in the eleven unknowns
$\{a_0,\dots,a_5,c_0,\dots,c_4\}$ whose determinant is, for the Vaidya sextic,
\begin{equation}
    \det M=-32\,(\Ehat^2-1)\,\operatorname{disc}_r S .
    \label{eq:det-disc}
\end{equation}
The system is therefore nonsingular precisely when $S$ is squarefree and $\Ehat\neq1$,
i.e.\ away from the genus-degeneration locus $|J|=\Jc$ and away from the marginal energy;
there its solution is unique and returns the $c_k$ as \emph{rational} functions of the
metric parameters.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Differentiate the ansatz in $r$ and clear the radical using
$\partial_\lambda F=N_\lambda/S^{3/2}$: the term $\mathcal{A}/\sqrt S$ contributes
$(2\mathcal{A}'S-\mathcal{A}S')/(2S^{3/2})$ and each $U_k=\int r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r$
contributes $c_k r^k/\sqrt S$, so multiplying through by $2S^{3/2}$
gives~\eqref{eq:reduction-identity}. Both sides are polynomials of degree ten in $r$
(recall $\deg\mathcal{A}=5$, $\deg S=6$), so the identity is equivalent to the vanishing
of eleven coefficients, a linear system in $\{a_i,c_k\}$.

For nonsingularity consider the homogeneous problem
$2\mathcal{A}'S-\mathcal{A}S'+2S\sum_k c_kr^k=0$. It gives $S\mid \mathcal{A}S'$. If $S$
is squarefree then $\gcd(S,S')=1$, hence $S\mid\mathcal{A}$; but
$\deg\mathcal{A}=5<6=\deg S$, forcing $\mathcal{A}=0$ and then $\sum_kc_kr^k=0$, i.e.\
$c_k=0$. The kernel is trivial and the matrix invertible; Cramer's rule exhibits each
$c_k$ as a ratio of determinants in the coefficients of $S$ and $N_\lambda$, hence as a
rational function of the metric parameters.

Conversely, if $S$ is not squarefree---on the degeneration locus, where
$S=(r-r_d)^2Q_4$ with $\deg Q_4=4$---take $\mathcal{A}=(r-r_d)Q_4$, of degree five and
hence admissible. Using $S=(r-r_d)\mathcal{A}$ one computes
$2\mathcal{A}'S-\mathcal{A}S'=(r-r_d)\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}'-\mathcal{A}^2
=S\,(\mathcal{A}'-Q_4)$, and $\deg(\mathcal{A}'-Q_4)=4$, so
$\big(\mathcal{A},\,\sum_kc_kr^k\big)=\big((r-r_d)Q_4,\,-\tfrac12(\mathcal{A}'-Q_4)\big)$
is a nonzero admissible kernel vector: the system is singular, with a kernel of dimension
one. The determinant formula~\eqref{eq:det-disc} is Lemma~\ref{lem:det-res} below.
\end{proof}

The determinant of the reduction system is not a Vaidya-specific quantity: it is the
resultant of the radicand with its derivative, for any sextic whatsoever.

\begin{auxlemma}[the reduction determinant is a resultant]\label{lem:det-res}
Let $S=\sum_{i=0}^{6}s_ir^i$ be arbitrary and let $M$ be the $11\times11$ matrix, in the
monomial bases, of the linear map
\[
   T:\ \mathbb{P}_5\times\mathbb{P}_4\longrightarrow\mathbb{P}_{10},
   \qquad
   T(\mathcal{A},C)=2\mathcal{A}'S-\mathcal{A}S'+2SC ,
\]
$\mathbb{P}_d$ denoting the polynomials of degree at most $d$. Then
\begin{equation}
   \det M=32\,\mathrm{Res}_r(S,S')=-32\,s_6\,\operatorname{disc}_rS .
   \label{eq:det-res}
\end{equation}
In particular $T$ is invertible if and only if $\deg S=6$ and $S$ is squarefree. For the
Vaidya sextic $s_6=\Ehat^2-1$, which is~\eqref{eq:det-disc}.
\end{auxlemma}

\begin{proof}
Each column of $M$ collects the coefficients of $2(r^i)'S-r^iS'$ or of $2Sr^k$, so every
entry of $M$ is linear in $(s_0,\dots,s_6)$ and $\det M$ is homogeneous of degree eleven in
them. So is $\mathrm{Res}_r(S,S')$: the resultant of a form of degree six with one of
degree five is of degree five in the coefficients of the first and six in those of the
second, and the coefficients of $S'$ are themselves linear in the $s_i$. For degree six the
classical relation reads $\mathrm{Res}_r(S,S')=(-1)^{6\cdot5/2}s_6\operatorname{disc}_rS
=-s_6\operatorname{disc}_rS$.

Next, $\det M$ vanishes on the zero locus of $s_6\operatorname{disc}_rS$. If $s_6=0$ then
$\deg\big(T(\mathcal{A},C)\big)\le9$ for all arguments, so the image lies in the proper
subspace $\mathbb{P}_9$ and $T$ is not surjective. If $s_6\neq0$ and $S$ has a repeated
root $r_d$, write $S=(r-r_d)\mathcal{A}$ with $\mathcal{A}=S/(r-r_d)$ of degree five; since
$r_d$ is at least a double root, $(r-r_d)\mid\mathcal{A}$, say $\mathcal{A}=(r-r_d)Q$ with
$\deg Q=4$. Then
\[
   2\mathcal{A}'S-\mathcal{A}S'
   =\mathcal{A}\big[(r-r_d)\mathcal{A}'-\mathcal{A}\big]
   =(r-r_d)^3\,Q\,Q' ,
\]
so that $T\big(\mathcal{A},\,-\tfrac12(r-r_d)Q'\big)=0$ with $\deg(-\tfrac12(r-r_d)Q')=4$:
a nonzero element of $\ker T$, whence $\det M=0$.

Finally, $\operatorname{disc}_rS$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$ as a polynomial in the
$s_i$, and it is coprime to $s_6$; both therefore divide $\det M$, hence so does their
product, of degree $1+10=11$. Since $\det M$ is also of degree eleven and is not
identically zero, $\det M=c\,s_6\operatorname{disc}_rS$ for a rational constant $c$, which
a single evaluation fixes: at $S=r^6-1$ one has $\operatorname{disc}_rS=46656$, $s_6=1$
and $\det M=-1492992=-32\cdot46656$, so $c=-32$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[the reduction and the genus are governed by the same discriminant]
Identity~\eqref{eq:det-res} makes Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} sharp: the depth-one
reduction is uniquely solvable exactly on the genus-two stratum
$\operatorname{disc}_rS\neq0$ and fails exactly where the spectral curve degenerates,
with the rank dropping by exactly one. This is the structural reason why the degeneration
loci need the separate treatment of \ref{app:degeneration}. Independently of the proof
above, the identity was confirmed symbolically in the seven generic coefficients
$(s_0,\dots,s_6)$, and both it and the kernel vector were re-checked on the Vaidya family
in two computer-algebra systems: at $m=1$, $\Ehat=7/5$, $J=5/2$ the determinant is nonzero
with $\gcd(S,S')=1$, while at $|J|=\Jc$ it vanishes with $\operatorname{rank}M=10$ and
$S=(r-r_d)^2Q_4$ exactly.
\end{remark}

The practical consequence is that every coefficient in the adiabatic corrections is fixed
by a \emph{single} polynomial identity that the reader can solve directly; nothing is
fitted. For Vaidya $S$ is the
Schwarzschild-seed sextic and the numerator is
$N_m=S\,\partial_m K-\tfrac12K\,\partial_m S$; the advanced-time clock is entirely
on-curve, its second-kind vector $b$ feeding the $W_{kj}$ of $S$ and its horizon part the
third-kind $\ln\sigma$-quotients.

For the Vaidya mass reduction the linear solve can in fact be carried out once and for
all. Writing $\mathfrak E\equiv\Ehat^{2}$ and introducing the \emph{reduced discriminant}
\begin{equation}
   \Delta\;\equiv\;(\mathfrak E-1)^3J^4+(\mathfrak E^2-20\,\mathfrak E-8)J^2m^2-16m^4,
   \qquad
   \operatorname{disc}_rS=2^{18}\,\mathfrak E^{6}\,J^{10}\,m^{16}\,\Delta,
   \label{eq:reduced-disc}
\end{equation}
the solution of~\eqref{eq:reduction-identity} is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
   c_0^m&=\frac{4Jm^2\big[3(\mathfrak E-1)J^2+4m^2\big]}{\Delta},&\qquad
   c_4^m&=\frac{2(\mathfrak E-1)(\mathfrak E+8)J}{\Delta},\\[2pt]
   c_1^m&=\frac{4Jm\big[3(\mathfrak E-1)(\mathfrak E-2)J^2+5\mathfrak Em^2\big]}{\Delta},\\[2pt]
   c_2^m&=\frac{J\big[(\mathfrak E-11)(\mathfrak E-1)^2J^2
     +4(\mathfrak E^2-\mathfrak E-15)m^2\big]}{\Delta},\\[2pt]
   c_3^m&=-\frac{J\big[(\mathfrak E-1)^3J^2
     +3(2\mathfrak E^2+9\mathfrak E-20)m^2\big]}{m\,\Delta}.
\end{aligned}
   \label{eq:ck-closed}
\end{equation}
The accompanying algebraic part is likewise explicit,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
   \mathcal{A}^m&=-\frac{J\,r\,D_{\Ehat}}{m\,\Delta}\;\Pi_{\mathcal A},\\
   \Pi_{\mathcal A}&=(\mathfrak E-1)^3J^4
     +mr\big(\mathfrak E(r-2m)^2+4r(2r-5m)\big)\\
   &\quad+J^2\big((\mathfrak E^2-8\mathfrak E-20)m^2
     +2(\mathfrak E-1)(\mathfrak E-4)mr-(\mathfrak E-1)^2r^2\big),
\end{aligned}
   \label{eq:Am-closed}
\end{equation}
so that~\eqref{eq:dm-reduction} is completely printed: no linear solve is required to
reproduce the Vaidya mass reduction. Substituting~\eqref{eq:ck-closed}
and~\eqref{eq:Am-closed} into~\eqref{eq:reduction-identity} makes it vanish identically in
$(r,m,\Ehat,J)$, which is the statement that they solve it.

Three structural features are visible. Each $c_k^m$ is \emph{odd} in $J$ and depends on the
energy only through $\mathfrak E=\Ehat^2$, as it must, since $N_m$ is odd in $J$ while $S$
is even. The common denominator is the reduced discriminant, so
by~\eqref{eq:det-disc} the reduction degenerates exactly where the curve does. The third
feature is a scaling law, which is not a dimensional observation but a consequence of the
uniqueness established in Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}.

\begin{auxlemma}[scaling of the reduction]\label{lem:ck-scaling}
Let $\sigma_\alpha$ denote the scaling $(r,m,J)\mapsto(\alpha r,\alpha m,\alpha J)$,
$\alpha>0$, let $S$ be homogeneous of degree six and $N_\lambda$ homogeneous of degree
$\nu$, and assume the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}. Then the unique solution
of~\eqref{eq:reduction-identity} is itself homogeneous, of degrees
\begin{equation}
   \deg\mathcal{A}=\nu-5,\qquad \deg c_k=\nu-6-k .
   \label{eq:ck-scaling}
\end{equation}
For the Vaidya mass reduction $K=JD_{\Ehat}$ is homogeneous of degree \emph{two}, hence
$N_m=S\,\partial_mK-\tfrac12K\,\partial_mS$ is homogeneous of degree $\nu=7$, giving
$\deg\mathcal{A}^m=2$ and $\deg c^m_k=1-k$, i.e.\
$c^m_k(\alpha m,\Ehat,\alpha J)=\alpha^{1-k}c^m_k(m,\Ehat,J)$ and equivalently
$c^m_k=m^{1-k}\,C_k(\Ehat^2,J/m)$.
\end{auxlemma}

\begin{proof}
Fix $\alpha>0$ and define
$\tilde{\mathcal{A}}:=\alpha^{5-\nu}\,\mathcal{A}\!\circ\!\sigma_\alpha$ and
$\tilde c_k:=\alpha^{6+k-\nu}\,c_k\!\circ\!\sigma_\alpha$. These are admissible: $\sigma_\alpha$
maps polynomials of degree at most five in $r$ to polynomials of degree at most five, and
the $\tilde c_k$ remain independent of $r$. Evaluating~\eqref{eq:reduction-identity} at
$\sigma_\alpha(r,m,J)$ and using $S\circ\sigma_\alpha=\alpha^6S$,
$(\partial_rS)\circ\sigma_\alpha=\alpha^5\partial_rS$ and
$N_\lambda\circ\sigma_\alpha=\alpha^\nu N_\lambda$ gives
\[
   2\,\alpha^{6}\,(\mathcal{A}'\!\circ\!\sigma_\alpha)\,S
   -\alpha^{5}\,(\mathcal{A}\circ\sigma_\alpha)\,S'
   +2S\sum_k\alpha^{6+k}\,(c_k\circ\sigma_\alpha)\,r^k
   \;=\;2\,\alpha^{\nu}N_\lambda ,
\]
and dividing by $\alpha^{\nu}$ turns the left-hand side into
$2\tilde{\mathcal{A}}'S-\tilde{\mathcal{A}}S'+2S\sum_k\tilde c_kr^k$, because
$\tilde{\mathcal{A}}'=\alpha^{6-\nu}\,\mathcal{A}'\!\circ\!\sigma_\alpha$. Hence
$(\tilde{\mathcal{A}},\tilde c)$ solves the same identity with the same data. By
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} that solution is unique, so
$\tilde{\mathcal{A}}=\mathcal{A}$ and $\tilde c_k=c_k$, which is~\eqref{eq:ck-scaling}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}
The load-bearing step is uniqueness, not the exponent count: a linear problem whose data
are equivariant under a group action has an equivariant solution only when that solution
is unique. Accordingly~\eqref{eq:ck-scaling} holds exactly on the stratum where
Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction} applies and says nothing on the degeneration locus, where the
solution set is a line and the $c_k$ have poles. The same homogeneity of $S$, used through
Euler's theorem instead, is what fixes $\Jc=m\,j(\Ehat)$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:tracking}.
\end{remark}

\section{Explicit adiabatic forms on the genus-degeneration loci}
\label{app:degeneration}
This appendix collects in one place the fully explicit first-order adiabatic
brachistochrone on the loci where the branch sextic acquires a double root and drops to
genus one. For \emph{spherical} Vaidya the relevant value $J=\Jc$ is the \emph{algebraic}
genus-degeneration $J_{\rm deg}$---the double root sits at $r_d<0$
(a negative-radius double root), so it is \emph{not} a physical, accessible separatrix,
though the genus-two$\,\to\,$genus-one reduction is valid there all the same; we give both
the proper-time ($\tau$) and advanced-time ($v$) branches with every coefficient in closed
form. All objects live on
the elliptic curve $E:\,w^2=Q_4(r)$ into which the branch sextic collapses. The adiabatic expansion is
$\varphi(r)=\varphi_0(r)+\dot\lambda\,\delta\varphi(r)+O(\dot\lambda^2)$, $\lambda=m$.

\paragraph{Curve and special functions.}
$\Jc=5\sqrt{3011/3072+581\sqrt{249}/9216}$ (at $E=7/5,\,m=1$; in general $\Jc=m\,j(E)$),

\subsection{Adiabatic derivative of the degeneration family}
\label{app:tracking}
As the slow parameter drifts, the genus-degeneration value itself moves,
$J=\Jc(\lambda)$. This is \emph{not} the evolution of a single torque-free extremal:
the axial costate $J$ is conserved along every extremal
(section~\ref{sec:principle}), so one trajectory cannot follow the moving locus by
changing $J$ unless an external azimuthal torque---a modified control law with
$\dd J/\dd\lambda\neq0$---is supplied. The object below is instead the total
$\lambda$-derivative of the \emph{one-parameter family of marginal (degeneration)
solutions} along $J=\Jc(\lambda)$: the adiabatic derivative of the family, which
packages how the closed form deforms as the locus drifts.

\Ithm{6}
\begin{lemma}[degeneration-family derivative]\label{lem:tracking}
Let $r_d(\lambda)$ be the double root of $S$ along the degeneration locus
$J=\Jc(\lambda)$, and set
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm tot}=N+\frac{\dd\Jc}{\dd\lambda}\,N_J,\qquad
N_J=S\,\partial_JK-\tfrac12K\,\partial_JS.
\label{eq:Ntot-track}
\end{equation}
Then the block form and residue formulae of \ref{app:degeneration} hold verbatim under
$N\to N_{\rm tot}$, with $\varphi_0$, the clocks and the dilogarithm structure unchanged;
and the tracked source vanishes at the double root,
\begin{equation}
N_{\rm tot}(r_d)=-\tfrac12K(r_d)\Big[\partial_\lambda S
+\tfrac{\dd\Jc}{\dd\lambda}\,\partial_JS\Big](r_d)
=\tfrac12K(r_d)\,S'(r_d)\,\frac{\dd r_d}{\dd\lambda}=0,
\label{eq:track-cancel}
\end{equation}
so that the triple-pole residue drops, $b_3^{\rm track}=0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Evaluating~\eqref{eq:Ntot-track} at $r=r_d$, the terms proportional to $S$ drop because
$S(r_d)=0$, leaving the first equality in~\eqref{eq:track-cancel}. For the second, note
that the Vaidya sextic is \emph{jointly homogeneous of degree six} in $(r,m,J)$,
\[
   S(\alpha r,\alpha m,\alpha J)=\alpha^6\,S(r,m,J),
   \qquad\text{hence}\qquad
   r\,\partial_rS+m\,\partial_mS+J\,\partial_JS=6\,S
\]
by Euler's theorem. Homogeneity also fixes the scaling of the degeneration locus,
$\Jc=m\,j(\Ehat)$ and $r_d=m\,\rho(\Ehat)$, so that $\dd\Jc/\dd m=\Jc/m$ and
$\dd r_d/\dd m=r_d/m$. Evaluating Euler's identity at the double root, where both $S$ and
$\partial_rS=S'$ vanish, leaves $m\,\partial_mS+J\,\partial_JS=0$, that is
\[
   \Big[\partial_mS+\frac{\Jc}{m}\,\partial_JS\Big](r_d)
   =\Big[\partial_\lambda S+\frac{\dd\Jc}{\dd\lambda}\,\partial_JS\Big](r_d)=0 ,
\]
which is the bracket in~\eqref{eq:track-cancel}; equivalently it equals
$-S'(r_d)\,\dd r_d/\dd\lambda$, obtained by differentiating the double-root condition
$S(r_d(\lambda),\Jc(\lambda);\lambda)=0$ totally in $\lambda$, and this vanishes because
$S'(r_d)=0$. Hence $N_{\rm tot}(r_d)=0$, and since the triple-pole residue is proportional
to $N_{\rm tot}(r_d)$ it vanishes as well.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[independent verification]
Euler's identity for $S$ was confirmed to reduce to zero identically in
\textsc{Mathematica} and \textsc{SymPy}. At the concrete locus $m=1$, $\Ehat=7/5$---where
$\Jc=5\sqrt{3011/3072+581\sqrt{249}/9216}=7.0266237403890\ldots$, reproducing the value
quoted in \ref{app:degeneration}, and $r_d=-3.3637111847170\ldots$---one finds
$N(r_d)=1.16239\times10^{4}$ and $N_J(r_d)=-1.65427\times10^{3}$ separately nonzero, while
$N_{\rm tot}(r_d)=0$ exactly in both systems.
\end{remark}

Following the moving genus-degeneration locus therefore softens the strongest
singularity, precisely where the double root travels. The cancellation is verified
symbolically in Mathematica, $N_{\rm tot}(r_d)\equiv0\pmod{S,\,S'}$. The two Vaidya
branches (proper-time $\tau$ and advanced-time $v$) differ only in $\dd\Jc/\dd\lambda$
and $N_J$.

\paragraph{Vaidya ($\lambda=m$).}
The linear scaling $\Jc=m\,j(E)$ gives $\dd\Jc/\dd m=\Jc/m$, with
$N_J=S\,D_E+\Jc^2\,r(r-2m)D_E^3$ (verified to $10^{-8}$).

\paragraph{Fully symbolic coefficients, generic-genus $q$-series, and independent checks.}
Away from the degeneration locus (generic $J$, genus two) every block coefficient is a
\emph{rational function of $(m,E,J)$}: the source coefficients $c_k$,
$Q_{kj}=c_kb_j-c_jb_k$, and the canonical pole coefficients $g_i$ (Taylor
coefficients of $q_6^{-1/2}$ at the odd-model infinity), with
$g_0=1/\sqrt{E^2-1}$ and $g_1=(2E^2-3)/(E^2-1)^{3/2}$ set by the leading behaviour
($E$-only), while $g_2$ and the $c_k$ carry the full $J$ dependence. The
algebraic remainder is elementary in closed form,
$T_{\rm alg}=P(r)+\sum_i\mathrm{res}_i\ln(r-r_i)$ over the six roots of $S$
(three residues vanish, at $r=0,\,2m,\,2m/(1-E^2)$). The genus-two dilogarithm is a
convergent \emph{nome ($q$-)series}: after Siegel reduction $\tau'=-\tau^{-1}$ (all
nomes ${<}1$), $\Lambda=\int Q\,\dd\mathcal A+\int L\,\dd\mathcal A$ with $Q$
elementary and $L=\log[\theta[\delta](w-e_+)/\theta[\delta](w-e_-)]$ a genus-two
Kronecker--Eisenstein series that converges geometrically (order $N{=}4$ already
matches to $10^{-13}$) and does \emph{not} reduce to classical dilogarithms (there is
no genus-two Jacobi product formula). All these symbolic statements are
\emph{independently reproduced in Mathematica} (native $\wp$, Gröbner reduction,
Laurent extraction): the degeneration residues to $10^{-16}$--$10^{-38}$, the generic-$J$
reductions to an exact polynomial identity, and the tracking
theorem~\eqref{eq:track-cancel}.

\paragraph{Transcendence.}
None of these is elementary. The $U_k$ are third-kind elliptic integrals, $\tilde G$
lives in $\sigma,\zeta,\wp$, and the weight-two content is the elliptic dilogarithm,
conjecturally irreducible to weight one (theta-divisor obstruction $\Theta=W_{g-1}$). The
advanced-time horizon logarithm is elementary only as a $\dd\log$ letter; integrated
against the elliptic $\partial_m F$ it yields the horizon elliptic dilogarithm, of the
same transcendental weight as the proper-time case---the horizon merely relocates the
dilogarithm's anchor from infinity to the two-torsion point $r=2m$. The sole
numerical ingredient anywhere is the optional compression of the weight-two content to
an apparent minimal five-dimensional dilogarithm basis (a numerically stable rank five,
singular values falling to $10^{-79}$---strong evidence, \emph{not} a proof, and
curve-specific), which the block forms above do not require.

\section{Complete first-order extended-Hamiltonian correction}
\label{app:firstorder}

This appendix proves Theorem~\ref{thm:firstorder}, stated in section~\ref{sec:vaidya-adiabatic}: it derives
equation~\eqref{eq:adiab-exact}---the complete first-order (on-shell \emph{plus} off-shell)
adiabatic correction to the orbit shape---from the extended Hamiltonian in a
metric-independent way, and closes it explicitly for the Vaidya branches so that a reader
can reconstruct it without the accompanying code. Here $\Theta$ is the Euler operator that
generates the drift of the frozen family through the slow parameter, and the drift
generator $\mathcal D$ equals $\Theta$ \emph{unless} the slow parameter rescales the
canonical momenta, in which case it acquires a momentum-normalization term. For
single-parameter Vaidya the mass is a metric modulus (no rescaling), so
$\mathcal D=\Theta=m\partial_m$ and the source collapses to the universal boundary form
$S_{\mathcal D}=[\,rp_r\,]-\lambda$ derived below. If a slow parameter also rescales a
canonical momentum, the drift generator requires the corresponding dilation term; that
different deformation class is not used in the Vaidya calculation below.

\paragraph{Notation and the slow datum.} $\Ehat$ and $J=p_\varphi$ are the
\emph{conserved} control charges (Kodama energy and axial costate). For Vaidya the
background coordinate is the advanced time $v$. Before expanding, choose a fixed
reference mass $M$ and introduce the dimensionless slow coordinate $s=v/M$; radii and
masses in this appendix are likewise measured in units of $M$. We then write
$m(s)=m_0(1+\varepsilon s+\cdots)$ with the unambiguous dimensionless rate
\[
    \varepsilon\equiv\frac{1}{m}\frac{\dd m}{\dd s}
    =\frac{M\dot m}{m},\qquad \dot m\equiv\frac{\dd m}{\dd v}.
\]
The operator generating the drift of the frozen family is the
Euler operator
\begin{equation}
    \Theta\equiv m\,\partial_m\quad(\text{Vaidya}),
    \label{eq:theta-op}
\end{equation}
since $\dd m/\dd s=+\varepsilon m$ at leading order.

\paragraph{Frozen data.} At fixed $s$ the frozen Hamiltonian $H_0(r,p_r;\lambda)=0$
fixes: the \emph{ingoing} frozen momentum $p_{r0}(r)$, the root of $H_0=0$ with
$H_{0,p_r}<0$ (the outgoing arc is its mirror across the turning point); the shape
integrand $G_0(r)=\dd\varphi/\dd r=H_{0,J}/H_{0,p_r}$; and the clock density
$\dd\lambda/\dd r=1/H_{0,p_r}$, with $\lambda$ oriented \emph{inward from the
free-arrival endpoint}.

\paragraph{Drift generator.} Adjoining the slow clock $s$ with conjugate $p_s$, the
extended Hamiltonian $\mathcal H=p_s+H(r,p_r;\lambda(s))=0$ generates the \emph{canonical}
flow $\dd r/\dd s=H_{p_r}$, $\dd p_r/\dd s=-H_r$, and the shell-departure rate is
$\dd H/\dd s=-\varepsilon\,\mathcal D H$ with $\mathcal D$ the Euler operator generating
the frozen-family drift. When the slow parameter enters only as a metric modulus
(Vaidya) it does not rescale the momenta, so $\mathcal D=\Theta=m\partial_m$. When
instead a slow parameter rescales a normalized momentum $P_r$, the rescaling is
time-dependent (conformally symplectic), \emph{not} canonical: the chain rule adds a
dilation term and promotes $\Theta$ to a full Euler operator containing
$P_r\partial_{P_r}$. Here no such rescaling occurs: the canonical pair is $(r,p_r)$ and
$\mathcal D=\Theta$.

\paragraph{Extended system and first order.} Adjoining $s$ with conjugate $p_s$,
$\mathcal H=p_s+H(r,p_r;\lambda(s))=0$; free-arrival transversality sets $H=0$ (hence
$p_s=0$) at $\lambda=0$. Along the flow $\dd p_s/\dd\lambda=\varepsilon\,\mathcal D H$, so
the \emph{terminally-anchored} source is
\begin{equation}
    p_s^{(1)}(\lambda)\equiv S_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)
    =\int_0^{\lambda}\mathcal D H\,\dd\lambda',
    \label{eq:pS-app}
\end{equation}
and, linearizing $H$ at fixed $r$, the momentum-shell shift
\begin{equation}
    \delta p_r=\frac{\lambda\,\Theta H-S_{\mathcal D}}{H_{p_r}},
    \label{eq:dpr-app}
\end{equation}
the axial costate remaining conserved ($\delta J=0$). The shape varies as
$\dd\varphi/\dd r=G_0+\varepsilon[\partial_{p_r}G_0\,\delta p_r-\lambda\,\Theta G_0]
+O(\varepsilon^2)$; integrating in $r$ gives equation~\eqref{eq:adiab-exact}. The reduction is
non-uniform at the turning point ($H_{p_r}\to0$): it holds on compact sub-arcs, and all
displayed residuals are taken there.

\paragraph{The universal boundary form of the Vaidya source.} The source does not
require a per-metric reduction to be written in closed form. Any arrival-time
Hamiltonian is Randers, $H=\beta(r)\,J+\sqrt{A_{rr}(r)\,p_r^2+A_{\varphi\varphi}(r)\,J^2}-1$,
homogeneous of degree one in $(p_r,J)$ apart from the $-1$, so Euler's theorem gives
\begin{equation}
    \big(J\,\partial_J+p_r\,\partial_{p_r}\big)H=H+1 .
    \label{eq:finsler-euler}
\end{equation}
Second, $H_v$ of~\eqref{eq:Hv} is invariant under the simultaneous rescaling
$(r,m,J)\mapsto(\alpha r,\alpha m,\alpha J)$ at fixed $p_r$---the Schwarzschild
self-similarity of the frozen family---so it is homogeneous of degree zero in $(r,m,J)$ and
Euler's theorem in those variables gives
\begin{equation}
    m\,\partial_m H=-\big(r\,\partial_r H+J\,\partial_J H\big)
    \qquad\text{identically, not merely on the shell.}
    \label{eq:selfsimilar}
\end{equation}
Combining the two turns the source integrand into a total derivative. Along the frozen
flow $\dd r/\dd\lambda=H_{p_r}$ and $\dd p_r/\dd\lambda=-H_r$, so
\[
   \frac{\dd (r\,p_r)}{\dd\lambda}=p_r H_{p_r}-r H_r
   \overset{\eqref{eq:finsler-euler}}{=}\big(H+1-J H_J\big)-r H_r
   \overset{\eqref{eq:selfsimilar}}{=}1+\Theta H+H ,
\]
and the shell condition $H=0$ removes the last term, leaving
$\Theta H=\dd(r\,p_r)/\dd\lambda-1$. Integrating from the terminal anchor gives the
\emph{universal boundary form}
\begin{equation}
    S_{\mathcal D}(\lambda)=[\,rp_r\,]_0^{\lambda}-\lambda,
    \label{eq:SD-vaidya}
\end{equation}
metric-independent up to the endpoint bracket. Identities~\eqref{eq:finsler-euler}
and~\eqref{eq:selfsimilar}, the expression~\eqref{eq:ThetaHv} for $\Theta H_v$, and the
combination $p_rH_{p_r}-rH_r-(1+\Theta H)=H$ were each verified symbolically in
\textsc{Mathematica}, all four residuals reducing to zero identically in
$(r,m,\Ehat,J,p_r)$; the numerical cross-check is
\texttt{VaidyaMetric/universal\_SD\_source\_vaidya.py}. The explicit Vaidya on-shell and
off-shell closed forms, with the horizon dilogarithm, are assembled in
section~\ref{sec:vaidya-adiabatic}.

\paragraph{The remainder is $O(\varepsilon^2)$.} The expansion above is an asymptotic
statement about the Hamiltonian flow, and on the sub-arcs where the reduction is used it
carries a genuine bound. Fix a compact sub-arc $[\lambda_-,\lambda_+]$ on which the frozen
solution stays in a compact subset $\mathcal K$ of the regular domain and on which
$|H_{p_r}|\ge\kappa>0$ (i.e.\ bounded away from the turning point). On a neighbourhood of
$\mathcal K$ the extended Hamiltonian $\mathcal H=p_s+H(r,p_r;m(s))$ is $C^2$ with bounded
derivatives, so writing $z=(r,p_r)$ and $z_\varepsilon$ for the exact flow, the exact and
first-order flows obey
\[
   \big|z_\varepsilon(\lambda)-z_0(\lambda)-\varepsilon\,z_1(\lambda)\big|
   \le C\,\varepsilon^{2}\,e^{L(\lambda-\lambda_-)} ,
   \qquad \lambda\in[\lambda_-,\lambda_+],
\]
by the standard Gr\"onwall estimate applied to the difference between the exact flow and
its first variation, with $L$ a bound for $\|\partial_z^2\mathcal H\|$ on $\mathcal K$ and
$C$ depending on the same $C^2$ bounds. Dividing by $H_{p_r}$, legitimate since
$|H_{p_r}|\ge\kappa$, transfers the estimate to $\dd\varphi/\dd r$ and, after integration
over the sub-arc, to $\delta\varphi$; this is the precise sense in
which~\eqref{eq:adiab-exact} closes the correction to $O(\varepsilon^2)$. The estimate
degrades as $\kappa\to0$, which is why the reduction is non-uniform at the turning point
and why all displayed residuals are taken on compact sub-arcs bounded away from it. The
measured log--log slope $\approx2.0$ of figure~\ref{fig:vaidya-offshell} is the numerical
confirmation of this exponent.

\paragraph{Special-function class.} The building blocks form a strict transcendence
hierarchy. The second-kind integrals $U_0,\dots,U_3=\int r^k/\sqrt S\,\dd r$ are
\emph{ordinary Abelian integrals} (weight one), reducible to the classical Kleinian
$\zeta,\sigma$ basis (Baker~\cite{BakerAbelian1897}; section~\ref{sec:vaidya-adiabatic}),
the same class as $\varphi_0$. The letters
$W_{jk}=\int(r^jU_k-r^kU_j)/\sqrt S\,\dd r$ are \emph{length-two iterated} Abelian
integrals with algebraic coefficients---one transcendence level above the $U_k$ and
\emph{not} reducible to the weight-one Kleinian class (their irreducibility to weight one
is \emph{conjectural}). The third-kind letter at the horizon $r=2m$---the advanced-time
$\dd\log$ letter, whose residue is nonzero, unlike the on-shell $\psi$ where it
vanishes---adds a genus-two dilogarithm (descriptively, a hyperelliptic polylogarithm
\cite{BauneEtal2024,DHokerHiddingSchlotterer2025}), evaluated by the geometrically
convergent theta-nome series in the reproducibility package; its irreducibility and
single-valued completion remain open. Thus the \emph{frozen} reduction
$\partial_\lambda\varphi_0$ closes in the weight-one Abelian/Kleinian class, but already
the \emph{on-shell} first-order response carries a genuine weight-two sector---the
length-two iterated integrals $W_{jk}$; the off-shell term adds further length-two letters
and one genus-two (horizon) dilogarithm. Reducibility of the $W_{jk}$ to weight one and a
canonical single-valued completion remain conjectural.

and its $11\times11$ linear system---no fit); all block coefficients $c_k,g_i,Q_{kj}$;
the Kleinian $\zeta,\sigma$ closed forms of the $U_k$ (Hermite recursion) and the assembly
of the weight-two $W_{jk}$ from them (not reduced to the weight-one class); the elementary

\paragraph{Branch forms.} For the \emph{Vaidya} $v$-branch the slow datum $m$ is a metric
modulus, \emph{not} a conformal rescaling of momenta: there is no $p_r/A$ transformation
and hence no dilation term, so $\mathcal D=\Theta=m\partial_m$ is complete and
\begin{equation}
    \Theta H_v=m\,\partial_m H_v
    =m\!\left[-\frac{2p_r}{r}+\frac{\sqrt{\Ehat^2p_r^2+J^2/r^2}}{r\sqrt w}\right],
    \qquad w=\Ehat^2-f;
    \label{eq:ThetaHv}
\end{equation}
since $m$ \emph{increases} along the ingoing flow while the elapsed clock $\lambda$ is
oriented inward from the free-arrival endpoint, $\lambda$ runs \emph{opposite} to the mass
drift on this branch. This fixed orientation ($\mathrm{sgn}(\dd\lambda/\dd m)=-1$) is what
inverts both source contributions in equation~\eqref{eq:adiab-exact}, i.e.\
$\lambda\Theta H_0-S_{\mathcal D}\to S_{\mathcal D}-\lambda\Theta H_0$ and
$-\lambda\Theta G_0\to+\lambda\Theta G_0$---a declared sign convention, not a change of
result. The proper-time branch uses $H_\tau$ of equation~\eqref{eq:Htau-vaidya}. Both Vaidya branches (advanced-time $v$ and proper-time
$\tau$) close to $O(\varepsilon^2)$ against the true flow: least-squares log--log slope
$\approx1$ for the on-shell term alone versus $\approx2.0$ for the complete first-order
term (figure~\ref{fig:vaidya-offshell}).

\paragraph{Algebraic genus-degeneration limit ($J\to\Jc$).} The degeneration form is
\emph{not} obtained by substituting $J=\Jc$ into the generic expressions. The source
\emph{numerator} $N_\lambda$ is polynomial in $J$ and hence perfectly finite at $\Jc$, but
the reduction \emph{coefficients} $c_k$ are not: by~\eqref{eq:ck-closed} they carry the
reduced discriminant $\Delta$ in the denominator, and $\Delta$ vanishes precisely at
$|J|=\Jc$, so each $c_k$ has a simple pole there---the analytic counterpart of the rank
drop of Lemma~\ref{lem:reduction}. The degeneration forms below are therefore constructed
\emph{directly} on the degenerate curve, $S=(r-r_d)^2Q_4$ (double root $r_d$, genus two
$\to$ genus one), and not as a limit of the genus-two formulas. Each hyperelliptic letter collapses,
$U_k=\int r^k/\sqrt S\to V_k+r_d^{\,k}\,\Pi_{r_d}$ with $V_k=\int(\text{poly})/\sqrt{Q_4}$
(elliptic second kind, Weierstrass $\wp,\zeta$) and $\Pi_{r_d}=\int\dd r/[(r-r_d)\sqrt{Q_4}]$
(third kind); the spurious poles at $r_d$ cancel in the physical combination (as
$S(r_d)=0$), leaving the weight-two block as a genuine \emph{elliptic}
dilogarithm~\cite{BeilinsonLevin1994,BrownLevin2011} plus the horizon third kind. Thus the
degeneration off-shell form is one degree \emph{lower} than the generic genus-two case;
verified letter by letter to machine precision
(\texttt{VaidyaMetric/vaidya\_sep\_offshell\_elliptic.py}). For Vaidya the double root is a
branch point \emph{off} the physical arc and $\mathcal D=\Theta$ carries no dilation, so
the degeneration limit is regular and elliptic (non-uniform only at $r_d$) and no
orbit crosses it under the mass drift. The rotating case is qualitatively
different---there the conformal dilation ray drives the orbit \emph{across} the moving
separatrix, a Neishtadt separatrix-crossing problem
\cite{Neishtadt1987,Neishtadt2013} treated in Appendix~C of Paper~II.

                                     § 1.   Introduction

     For the black hole formed by the dynamical collapse of a star, if neither
matter nor grayitational wave is presented outside the horizon in a sufficiently
late stage, the area of the horizon does not change in time. 11 On the other hand,
when there remains matter and/or gravitational wave around the horizon, the
interaction between them changes its area gradually and can affect its structure
largely!1 The knowledge of this interaction between the horizon and the energy
flow around it is also important in the study of the backreaction problem in the
so-called black hole evaporation.S 1 ~ 61 If the Einstein equation holds and the energy-
momentum tensor satisfies the weak energy condition, as is well-known, the area
of the horizon does not decrease. 21 ' 81 In contrast, if quantum fields are present, in
 general, the energy-moment um tensor satisfies this condition no longer and the
horizon may reduce its area. 31 ' 31
     As these examples show, the know ledge of the interaction between the horizon
and the energy flow around it is interesting and important, but it has been obtained
so far only for the limited case of small perturbation from the stationary vacuum.')
This can be thought to he chiefly because energy cannot be covariantly localizable
in general relativity and conseqt1ently the definitions of the local energy so far
yielded are coordinate-depe ndent and its local conservation law cannot be applicable
to such cases that the horizon exists. 71 The only exceptional case is the one in
which a time-like Killing vector exists, but in this case we cannot deal with the
 dynamical problems. 8 l' 121
      In this paper we show that, for the spherically symmetric system, in contrast
 to the general case, there exist a preferable covariantly localizable energy flux and

the energy-moment um tensor as in the case in which a Killing vector exists. In

    With these considerations we proceed to the problem of backreaction. As a
model of the back reaction, Hawking's intuitive one, 51 'black hole evaporation', is
widely accepted and applied to many cosmological problems. w He argued that
the paradox 4l,sl of the infinite energy outflow to infinity, which is obtained if the
background geometry is fixed and the horizon is stable, is resolved if we take
account of the negative energy inflow across the horizon which reduces the area
of the horizon and vanishes it away. 51 ' 61 ' 151 This naive image about the effect of the
negative energy on the horizon behavior, which has been so far based on the
 result lirnited to the case of linear perturbation fron1 the stationary vacuntn, ) can

 be justified by our argument in § 4 based on the exact local conseryation law.
 There is, however, an important difficulty in this model. As has been mentioned
 in § 2, there inevitably occurs a naked singularity in this case, 101 since a causally
 trapped region appears and disappears again in a finite time. In fact, in the spheri-

cally symmetric system, by direct calculation, it can be shown that the scalars
constructed from the Riemann curvature tensor diverges at the vanishing point of
the horizon. Therefore, even apart from the causal ambiguity due to the nakedness
of the singularity, this singularity seems to make the geometrical optics approxima-
tion break down, on which Hawking's argument is essentially based. This assertion
is supported by the estimation by Ford and Parker 16 ) that the total energy flux to
infinity diverges if the stellar collapse forms a Reissner-Norcls tri:im type naked
singularity. Furthermore if singularity remains after the disappearance of the hori-
zon, as has been shown in § 4, this singularity should be of negative mass type

and the total energy radiated to 3+ can exceed the initial mass of the system.
This seems to suggest that Hawking's model may not be a stable solution to the
backreaction problem. Of course, near the singularity, the quantum effect of the
gravitational field itself will be important and the mean field (gpv) will lose mean-
ing. But this effect will introduce an uncertainty of a Planck mass order at most
into the above argument, otherwise the whole arguments on the quantum pheno-
mena around a collapsing stellar object by many authors, including Hawking, will
break down.
     Though these considerations do not exclude the possibility of the black hole
evaporation, we think that they are sufficient to convince ourselves that Hawking's
naive image and estimation on the evaporation process have no reliable ground.
Therefore, in turn, we briefly speculate on the other possibility that the black
hole does not evaporate away. Of course, if the spacetime has a quasi-stationary
structure in its late stage, the geometrical optics approximation is valid and the
existence of a stable horizon will produce a divergent energy flux to 3+, contradict-
ing to the conservation law in § 4. But if there exists the quantum fluctuation
of the geometry around the horizon, such as shown schematically in Fig. 2, due
to the continual absorption and emission of quantum energy by the horizon, we can
expect that the evaporation of the black hole may be suppressed. The reason is
that, clue to the scattering of the out-going wave mode during its propagation
through this oscillatory region, the transmission rate of each mode may be sup-
pressed for sufficiently large retarded time, leading to the suppression of the quan-
tum radiation to 3+. The detail of the study in this approach will be published
elsewhere.
     Finally we remark that, though the whole argument in this paper is restricted
to the spherically symmetric case, the considerations in this section on the backreac-
tion problem will have general validity since the symmetry assumption will play

Abstract As an example of a dynamical cosmological black hole, a spacetime
that describes an expanding black hole in the asymptotic background of the
Einstein-de Sitter universe is constructed. The black hole is primordial in the sense
that it forms ab initio with the big bang singularity and its expanding event hori-
zon is represented by a conformal Killing horizon. The metric representing the
black hole spacetime is obtained by applying a time dependent conformal trans-
formation on the Schwarzschild metric, such that the result is an exact solution
with a matter content described by a two-fluid source. Physical quantities such as
the surface gravity and other effects like perihelion precession, light bending and
circular orbits are studied in this spacetime and compared to their counterparts
in the gravitational field of the isolated Schwarzschild black hole. No changes
in the structure of null geodesics are recorded, but significant differences are ob-
tained for timelike geodesics, particularly an increase in the perihelion precession
and the non-existence of circular timelike orbits. The solution is expressed in the
Newman-Penrose formalism.


Keywords Cosmological black holes · Conformal Killing horizons · Conformal
transformations · Einstein-de Sitter universe

1 Introduction

For more than thirty years, black hole physics has been one of the most active
areas of research in both classical and quantum gravity. However almost all the
research has been limited to isolated black holes, represented in general by the
Kerr-Newman solution [1], of which the Schwarzschild, Reissner-Nordström and
Kerr solutions are special cases. All these exact solutions share two basic char-
acteristics. The first one is asymptotic flatness which means that the spacetime is
Minkowskian at large distances from the black hole. The second one is time inde-
pendence which implies the existence of a global timelike Killing vector field, ξ a ,
that generates the Killing horizon which is used to describe the event horizon in
these black hole spacetimes. These black hole solutions, which are considered as
good approximations to real astrophysical black holes, have been very useful for
studying the physical effects like the classical tests and quantum effects like black
hole evaporation.
     However in a more realistic situation, the black hole is part of a cosmological
model where it may be surrounded by a local mass distribution. In this case the
spacetime becomes cosmological and non-flat at large distances from the black
hole, and it may also cease to be time independent. One expects significant dif-
ferences in the structure and properties of these so called cosmological black
holes, from the well known isolated black holes, where even the passage from
the Schwarzschild black hole to the Kerr black hole brings profound changes.
     The introduction of a non-zero cosmological constant  in General Relativ-
ity, made it possible to study black holes in asymptotically de Sitter spacetimes
( > 0), and in asymptotically anti-de Sitter spacetimes ( < 0). The well known
Kerr-Newman family of solutions [1] can be extended [2] to  < 0, i.e. the Kerr-
Newman-anti-de Sitter family, and also to  > 0, i.e. the Kerr-Newman-de Sitter
family. The particular case of the Schwarzschild-de Sitter solution has been abun-
dantly studied [3]. However since both the anti-de Sitter and de-Sitter vacuum
universes are not appropriate for describing the observed universe, these family of
solutions cannot be considered as examples of cosmological black holes.
     Einstein and Straus [4] were the first to construct the simplest model of a cos-
mological black hole, which came to be known as the Einstein-Straus model, also
known in the literature as the “Swiss-Cheese Model”. They obtained a solution
representing a black hole in an expanding Friedmann universe by cutting out a
sphere of arbitrary radius from a homogeneous Friedmann model and matching
the rest with the Schwarzschild vacuum spacetime, which includes a black hole of
mass equal to the mass that was cut out. Recently Nayak et al. [5], obtained another
model representing a black hole in the cosmological background of the Einstein
static universe. It uses the family of solutions discovered earlier by Vaidya [6],
particularly the special case representing the superposition of the Schwarzschild
solution with the metric of the Einstein universe. This spherically symmetric so-
lution which is called the Vaidya-Einstein-Schwarzschild (VES) spacetime, is a
perfect fluid solution with an equation of state ρ + 3 p = 0, where ρ is the matter
density and p is the pressure. Nayak et al. used this solution to construct a compos-
ite static spacetime, in which the Schwarzschild vacuum spacetime representing
the interior of the black hole, is matched smoothly to the VES spacetime across
Cosmological black holes                                                          1351


the black hole surface. This is in turn matched to the spacetime of the Einstein
universe across a surface distribution of matter.
    This composite static spacetime is similar to the Einstein-Straus model in the
sense that the “cut-and-paste” method is used to construct both models. For this
reason the cosmological black holes merely exist, they do not form, and no relation
exists between the black hole singularity and the big bang or big crunch singular-
ities. Moreover in these two models although the condition of asymptotic flatness
is relaxed, time independence is preserved and so in both cases the event horizon
is defined as a Killing horizon. Being part of an expanding universe, a black hole
would cease to be time independent and so a more realistic picture requires that
time independence is given up as well. In this case the event horizon cannot be
defined as a Killing horizon, because the spacetime being time dependent does
not admit a global timelike Killing field which is required to generate a Killing
horizon. Hence an alternative definition of a dynamical cosmological black hole
has to be found.
    This was done [7] for spacetimes admitting a conformal Killing field, ξ a , that
generates a conformal Killing horizon [8]. If a spacetime (M, gab ) admits a con-
formal Killing vector field ξ a , satisfying

                Lξ gab ≡ ∇a ξb + ∇b ξa = 2φgab ,         4φ = ∇c ξ c ,             (1)

where Lξ denotes the Lie derivative in the direction ξ a and ∇ denotes covariant
derivative, one can define a conformal stationary limit surface (CSLS) [8] as the
hypersurface 0 on which ξ a is null, i.e. N ≡ ξ a ξa = 0. If the conformal station-
ary limit surface 0 , is itself a null surface then it is called a conformal Killing
horizon (CKH). It was shown [7], that a necessary and sufficient condition for the
nullity of the CSLS is that the rotation, ωa = 12 εabcd ∇b ξc ξd , of the conformal
Killing congruence vanishes on the hypersurface 0 , provided that dN = 0 on
0 , or in other words the surface 0 is non-degenerate. In this case besides being
irrotational, it is easy to show [7,8] that the conformal Killing trajectories are null
geodesics on the conformal Killing horizon 0 , and hence coincide with its null
generators. Being a null geodesic hypersurface the conformal Killing horizon acts
as a one-way membrane, i.e. the future null cone lies entirely on one side of the
null surface such that future directed timelike directions cross the null surface in
the same sense. In this way 0 satisfies the local conditions for it to constitute the
event horizon of a dynamical black hole spacetime. Spacetimes admitting con-
formal Killing horizons can be obtained by applying a conformal transformation,
g̃ab = 2 gab , to a stationary, asymptotically flat black hole spacetime (M, gab )
which admits a Killing horizon, 0 , generated by the Killing field ξ a . Under a
conformal transformation the Killing field ξ a is mapped to a conformal Killing
field, provided that ξ a ∇a  = 0. Also since conformal transformations preserve
causal structure as well as null geodesics, the hypersurface 0 in the manifold
(M, g̃ab ), is still a null surface whose null geodesic generators now coincide with
the conformal Killing trajectories of ξ a , i.e. it is a null geodesic hypersurface and
therefore a conformal Killing horizon. In this way the conformal stationary limit
surface and conformal Killing horizon in (M, g̃ab ), are the images of the corre-
sponding stationary limit surface and Killing horizon in the spacetime (M, gab ).
    We must point out that besides the above definition there are other definitions
of dynamical black holes in the literature. The first was proposed by Hayward [9]
1352                                                               J. Sultana, C. C. Dyer


who gave a general definition of a black hole which does not require any global
assumptions and relies only on the quasi-local geometry. His definition uses the
notion of trapping horizons and he gives the general definition of a black hole as a
future outer trapping horizon (FOTH). The so called dynamical horizons [10] were
later introduced. These are closely related to Hayward’s trapping horizons and in
cases of physical interest dynamical horizons are basically FOTHs. Recently [11]
the laws of black hole mechanics were generalised to include FOTHs. An interest-
ing aspect of Hayward’s definition of a dynamical black hole is that the FOTH is
in general spacelike and is only null for stationary spacetimes. This contrasts with
our definition of a dynamical black hole, where the conformal Killing horizon is
always a null hypersurface, as in the usual definition of a black hole for stationary
asymptotically flat spacetimes.
    In the next section an example of a dynamical cosmological black hole where
both conditions of time independence and asymptotic flatness are relaxed, is ob-
tained. This is done by conformally deforming the Schwarzschild vacuum solu-
tion such that the resulting metric is an exact solution of Einstein’s field equa-
tions, with a matter content described by a two-fluid source: one of which is a
null fluid and the other is pure dust. The conformal transformation applied to the
Schwarzschild solution is chosen in such a way that the resulting metric is asymp-
totically Einstein-de Sitter with the source reducing to a comoving pure dust at
null infinity. In this way the solution could be interpreted as a hole in the asymp-
totic background of the Einstein-de Sitter Universe. The expanding event hori-
zon is now described by a conformal Killing horizon which is the image of the
Killing horizon of the Schwarzschild black hole under the conformal transforma-
tion. Although conformal techniques in General Relativity have been used before
to generate conformally Ricci-flat perfect fluid solutions [12] and also conformally
Ricci-flat viscous fluid solutions [13], this is the first application of these methods
for the generation of time dependent black hole spacetimes. Some physical char-
acteristics of the event horizon, such as the surface area, temperature, expansion
and surface gravity are computed.
    In Sect. 3, physical effects in the spacetime surrounding the event horizon are
studied. These include perihelion precession, light bending, circular orbits and
a study of timelike and null geodesics in general. They are later compared with
their counterparts in the gravitational field of the isolated Schwarzschild solution.
In Sect. 4, the spacetime is expressed in the Newman-Penrose formalism.

5 Conclusion

The motivation for this work originated from the need for obtaining and studying
non-isolated and time dependent black hole spacetimes. These spacetimes which
generalise the previous models of Einstein and Straus [4] and Nayak et al. [5] rep-
resent a more realistic picture of a dynamical cosmological black hole, because
being part of an expanding universe a black hole would cease to be time indepen-
dent. Moreover these dynamical black holes are of fundamental importance to the
areas of black hole mechanics and black hole thermodynamics, because it allows
the thermodynamical laws to be treated in full generality.
    In our case this was obtained by studying a conformally deformed
Schwarzschild solution of Petrov type D, in which the conformal factor t 4 is
chosen in such a way that the solution is asymptotically Einstein-de Sitter. In
this way one can give a cosmological significance to the conformally deformed
Schwarzschild solution. We have to point out that besides our choice of the
conformal factor t 4 , there may be other ways of conformally deforming the
Schwarzschild metric in such a way that it asymptotically approaches the Einstein-
de Sitter universe as written in (6). Our choice of the conformal factor may be the
easiest one.
    A study of the radial null geodesics in this spacetime suggests that the solution
represents a black hole and is in fact the first example of a dynamical cosmologi-
cal black hole. The expanding event horizon is represented by a conformal Killing
horizon, generated by the conformal Killing field ∂/∂t, and its expansion is uni-
form everywhere. The source is described by a non-comoving two-fluid: one of
which is dust and the other is a null fluid. The source reduces asymptotically to
the comoving pure dust of the Einstein-de Sitter universe. A calculation of the
Kretschmann scalar reveals that the black hole is primordial, i.e. it forms with
the big-bang singularity of the Einstein-de Sitter universe, and not as a result of
gravitational collapse of a matter distribution. The energy conditions are satisfied
everywhere for t < 4m. For times t > 4m the dust particles close to the horizon
become superluminal as they fall into the black hole. This results in a violation of
the energy conditions in a region surrounding the event horizon which increases
in extent for later times.
    Unlike the two previous models studied by Einstein and Straus [4] and Nayak
et al. [5], which rely on the matching of different spacetimes across a surface
boundary or a surface layer, our model doesn’t use any matching, and the back-
ground Einstein-de Sitter spacetime is reached asymptotically as r → ∞. More-
over in the previous models there is no relation between the black hole singularity
and the big bang singularity.
    One application of this model is to address the question of the effect of the
cosmic expansion on the local dynamics, which was raised by many authors
[18–20] following the Einstein-Straus model. The Einstein-Straus model predicts
that the expansion or contraction of the background universe has no influence on
the local physical processes, like for example the geodesic orbits, inside the vac-
uole. The model of Nayak et al. uses the static Einstein universe as a cosmological
background, and hence it cannot be used to study the effect of the cosmic expan-
sion on the local physics. In our case this question was addressed by studying
the timelike and null geodesics in the cosmological black hole spacetime. It was

found that the null geodesics and the associated physical properties like for exam-
ple the bending of light by the gravitational field of the hole, are the same as in
the Schwarzschild case. However as Eq. (55) reveals, the situation in the timelike
case is different. In fact as was shown in (58), the term in (55) arising from the
expansion of the black hole dominates over the Schwarzschild term 3mu 2 . Al-
though we haven’t been able to solve the differential equation governing timelike
geodesics, we were able to study some physical effects like for example circular
orbits. It was found that no stable circular orbits are allowed. Moreover by apply-
ing a time-dependent perturbation to the Schwarzschild solution we have shown
that the expansion of the event horizon increases the perihelion shift. This increase
in the perihelion shift is very small for typical planetary orbits. However we found
that it is proportional to the rate of expansion of the event horizon and hence indi-
cates that the effect would have been much more pronounced had we considered
a cosmological black hole model like the one studied in this article, where the
expansion of the event horizon is proportional to 1/t.
    These two observations lead us to conclude that contrary to the predictions of
the Einstein-Straus model, the cosmic expansion can effect the local dynamics.

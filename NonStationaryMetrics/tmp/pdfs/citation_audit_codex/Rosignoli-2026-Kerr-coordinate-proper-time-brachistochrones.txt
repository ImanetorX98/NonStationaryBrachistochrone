   Brachistochrones in coordinate time and in proper time in
                      the Kerr geometry:
     analytical derivation, the t–τ plunge inversion, off-equatorial Hamilton–Jacobi
       structure, approximate quasi-invariants, and the Schwarzschild limit a → 0

                                                       I. Rosignoli∗
                                                       July 2, 2026

                                                           Abstract
    Building on Perlick’s formulation of the brachistochrone problem in stationary spacetimes [1] and on the optical
    Randers–Finsler framework for stationary metrics [2, 3], we give a unified treatment of the two brachistochrones of a
    test particle in the Kerr field: the one minimizing the coordinate time t and the one minimizing the proper time τ . On
    the equatorial plane Tt is the length of a Randers metric, due to a linear gravitomagnetic term, whereas Tτ is purely
    Riemannian. We derive the first integrals, exact orbit equations, travel times, and Hamiltonian formulations for both
    branches. From the equatorial Hamiltonians we obtain a new analytical result: for common initial phase-space data
    the two branches carry different on-shell energies, H0τ /H0t → E 2 /f02 , and this, combined with the gravitomagnetic
    enhancement of the centrifugal barrier, produces an inversion of the radial plunge across a critical angular momentum
    Jinv (a). The resulting closed-form criterion is validated numerically to four significant digits. Off the equator the
    brachistochrone Hamilton–Jacobi equation is non-separable: the conformal factor n2τ = F/DE obstructs Carter
    separation at finite energy, while separability is recovered in the Fermat limit. We therefore construct approximate
    quasi-invariants by perturbative Poisson-bracket closure: for τ a single O(a2 ) term reduces the drift, while for t the
    bare Carter form is already optimal. We also verify the a → 0 Schwarzschild limit, develop spin and weak-field
    expansions, and extend the construction toward the ergoregion using ZAMO variables. These results complement
    the companion interior treatments [17, 18].

10.2    Fermat limit and recovery of Carter
As E → ∞ the conformal factor degenerates, DE →
E 2 Σ, so ΣF/DE → F/E 2 with F = (r2 − 2M r) +
a2 cos2 θ additively separable. Equation (42) then splits
and Qstd becomes exact: the brachistochrone reduces to
the null (Fermat) geodesic problem of the Kerr optical
geometry [2, 4], whose fourth invariant is the Kerr Carter
constant. The finite-E obstruction is thus controlled by
w = E 2 − e2Φ and disappears in the high-energy (null)
regime — the canonical consistency check.

11.3     On-shell reduction and exact non-
         integrability
Substituting the radial shell p2r → 2h0 r2 /DE0 − p2θ /D0 −
J 2 /(D0 sin2 θ) collapses the NLO τ correction exactly:
g2 = f2 + F2 = 0, so the on-shell NLO invariant is
again Qstd (first nonzero on-shell term at O(a4 ), g4 =
N2 sin2 θ/[D02 DE0 ]). For t the same reduction doubles
the harmful correction, g2t = 2f2t . The fully nonpertur-
bative ansatz K = Qstd + φ(r, θ)p2θ forces φ = −1 from
the pθ p2r bracket, leaving K = J 2 cot2 θ − a2 E 2 cos2 θ;
this kills the pθ p2r and p3θ brackets exactly but leaves
an irreducible p1θ residue, and numerically drifts 8–800×
worse. Hence:
Observation 4 (non-integrability). No exact second
constant of the form Qstd + φ p2θ exists at a ̸= 0: the
system {K, Hτ } = 0 is overdetermined. The Randers–
Zermelo reduction injects Σ3 /F 2 terms absent from the
Kerr geodesic flow, obstructing Hamilton–Jacobi separa-
tion.

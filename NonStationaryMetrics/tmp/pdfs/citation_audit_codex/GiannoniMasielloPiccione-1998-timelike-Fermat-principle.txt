A timelike extension of Fermat’s principle
in general relativity and applications

Dedicated to the memory of Ennio De Giorgi


Abstract. We define a variational problem based on the arrival time functional
for timelike curves on a Lorentzian manifold M parameterized by a fixed con-
stant multiple of their proper time. Under a causality assumption for the manifold
M, we prove that the stationary points of our problem are geodesics, obtaining
an extension of the Fermat’s Principle for light rays proven in [14] (see also [2]).
Moreover, we study the compactness properties of the arrival time functional by
global variational techniques. Under intrinsic assumptions on the metric of M
we get results of existence and multiplicity for geodesics with a given energy
between an event and an observer of M.


1 Introduction

The Fermat’s Principle in Classical Optics states that the trajectory of a light
ray, starting from a source A directed towards a point B , is such that the travel
time is minimal, or better, stationary. Nowadays, it has been developed quite an
extensive literature about the Principle, and several versions of the same have
been proven in the context of General Relativity.
    Roughly speaking, if M is the model for a given relativistic space–time (a
Lorentzian manifold), p is an event and γ(s) is an observer in M, the trajectory
of a photon traveling from p to γ under the effect of the gravitational field is
a lightlike geodesic for the metric tensor of M. This kind of geodesics are
characterized by the property of minimizing (or extremizing) the proper time of
the observer at the arrival point. Here, the stationarity is intended in the space
of all regular lightlike curves in M joining p and γ. Without claiming any
completeness, we will refer to the articles [2, 5, 14, 17] for a more detailed

discussion of the issue, both from a physical and from a mathematical point of
view.
    The main result of this paper asserts that the Principle holds also in the case
of the trajectory of a free falling massive object, which is given by a timelike
geodesic in M. The interest of the authors in investigating the situation also in
the case of timelike geodesics arises naturally for several reasons. In first place,
the problem of determining the trajectory of a free falling massive particle, as
in the massless case of a photon, under the action of the gravitational field is
perfectly meaningful in physics, and in general it is desirable to have alterna-
tive variational principles for the study of (relativistic) dynamical systems. From
this point of view, it is probably not too far from reality to think of a gener-
alized Fermat’s Principle as the analog of a Maupertuis Principle in Classical
Mechanics.
    A second indication of the fact that the study of the arrival time functional
might have a certain physical interest is given in Perlick’s paper [15] on a gen-
eralized brachistochrone problem in a stationary relativistic space–time. In this
paper, the author studies the critical points of the arrival time for curves satisfy-
ing a certain property of conservation of energy with respect to a fixed timelike,
Killing observer field. Using the stationarity assumption for the space–time M,
the problem is reduced to a purely spatial (Riemannian) setup, and it is proven
a Maupertuis–Jacobi principle for curves of stationary arrival time. Even though
the aim of that article and the conservation law assumed in the space of curves
in consideration are quite different from ours, in both cases the main point under
investigation is the geodesical property for minimizers of the arrival time.
    To be precise, an earlier attempt to extend the Fermat’s principle to the
timelike case has already appeared in the physics literature (see [8]). It should be
remarked that Kovner’s approach to the problem, although very well motivated
from a physicist’s point of view, seems to be somewhat unsatisfactory from a
mathematical point of view. Moreover, in his work Kovner does not deal with
the compactness properties of the arrival time functional, needed for existence
results.
    There are also some mathematical aspects of the problem that it is worth
describing. The most important one (in the authors’ opinion) was noticed during
an earlier work regarding the study of light rays on stably causal Lorentzian
manifold (see [6]). In that case, the space of all timelike curves z , parametrized
by g(z )[ż , ż ] = −ε2 (g is the Lorentzian metric tensor on M) was used as a
smooth approximation for the non smooth space of all lightlike curves, yielding
an approximation scheme that was proven to be useful from the point of view
of calculus of variations. The functional used in [6] is slightly different from the
arrival time functional, but it should be considered essentially equivalent as far
as its critical points are concerned. Further details may be found in [2].
    To give a more direct approach, we will deal only with the case in which
the Lorentzian manifold has a (globally defined) time function, even though a
general treatment of the problem is possible. Indeed, the main definitions and
Timelike extension of the Fermat’s principle                                       265


theorems stated in this paper may be rephrased to make sense in the general case,
using the proper time of the observer γ (see Remark 5.3).
    From a technical point of view, it is interesting to notice that, unlike the light-
like case, under a suitable compactness property for the Lorentzian manifold, in
the timelike case the arrival time functional does indeed satisfy good compactness
condition, like the Palais–Smale condition (see Sect. 5), on the Banach manifold
of all timelike curves of class H 1,1 joining an event and an observer of M and
parametrized by a fixed multiple of their proper time. The class of manifolds for
which the Palais–Smale condition of the arrival time functional holds is reason-
ably large, including all the globally hyperbolic manifolds (see Example 5.4).
    The condition of Palais and Smale has important consequences as far as
the existence and, possibly, the multiplicity of timelike geodesics between an
observer and an event. Further developments of the theory could then provide
useful tools for the study of an infinite dimensional Morse theory for timelike
geodesics, which at this stage seems a challenging task (see [4]).

    The paper is organized as follows: Sect. 2 contains a few basic facts on
Lorentzian geometry needed for the statement and the proof of our results. In
Sect. 3 we will define the functional framework of our work and we will intro-
duce our variational problems. In Sect. 4 we will show that the critical points
for the arrival time functional are geodesics, and in Sect. 5 we will prove the
Palais–Smale condition for the arrival time. Finally, in Sect. 6, using standard
techniques of Nonlinear Analysis, we will derive an existence result for timelike
geodesics joining an event and an observer in a Lorentzian manifold. This kind
of techniques, together with the use of the Ljusternik–Schnirelman theory (see
[10, 16]), allows to get also some multiplicity results. For the sake of complete-
ness, in Appendix A we give a sketch of the proof of a regularity result used in
Section 3.


2 Preliminaries

We recall a few basic facts from Lorentzian Geometry; a complete account of the
subject can be found in classical texts, such as [3, 7, 12]. A Lorentzian manifold
is a smooth, finite dimensional manifold M, equipped with a metric tensor g
of index 1. This means that, for every z ∈ M, g(z )[·, ·] is a nondegenerate,
symmetric bilinear form on the tangent space Tz M such that the maximal di-
mension of a subspace on which the form is negative definite is 1. The bilinear
form g(z )[·, ·] will also be denoted by h·, ·i in the rest of the article.
     A vector v ∈ Tz M is said timelike (resp. lightlike, spacelike) if hv, vi is
negative (resp. null, positive); v is called causal if it is not spacelike. A regular
curve z : (a, b) ⊆ IR 7−→ M is called timelike (resp. lightlike, spacelike, causal )
if ż (s) is timelike (resp. lightlike, spacelike, causal) for all s ∈ (a, b). This
classification is called the causal character of a tangent vector, or of a curve.
If z is not smooth, but only absolutely continuous, we say that z is timelike,
266                                                                        F. Giannoni et al.


lightlike or spacelike respectively if hż , ż i is negative, null or positive almost
everywhere.
    A C 2 –curve z :]a, b[7−→ M is a geodesic in M if z satisfies the second
order differential equation:
                                    ∇s ż = 0,
where ∇ denotes the covariant derivative operator along z induced by the Levi–
Civita connection of the metric tensor g. If z is a geodesic, then the quantity:

                                    hż (s), ż (s)i = Ez                                (1)

is constant, since dsd hż (s), ż (s)i = 2h∇s ż (s), ż (s)i = 0. Timelike geodesics (Ez <
0) represent in general relativity the trajectories of massive objects under the
action of the gravitational field.
    A Lorentzian manifold is said to be time-oriented if there exists a continuous
vector field Y on M such that Y (z ) is timelike for all z ∈ M. A timelike vector
field defines a past a a future of a point z in M: a causal vector v ∈ Tz M
is said to be future pointing (resp. past pointing) if hv, Y (z )i is negative (resp.
positive).
    A Lorentzian manifold is said to be stably causal if it is causal, i.e. it does
not contain closed causal curves, and if this property is preserved after uniformly
small perturbations of the metric g. Equivalently (see [7], Proposition 6.4.9), M
is stably causal if there exists a time function T on M, which is a smooth
function T : M 7−→ IR, satisfying:

                                 h∇T (q), ∇T (q)i < 0,

for every q ∈ M, where ∇T is the Lorentzian gradient of T , defined by:

                                    dT (·) = h∇T , ·i.

We can use the map T to define a time-orientation for non necessarily smooth
curves. We say that a curve z :]a, b[7−→ M is future-pointing if the map
s 7−→ T (z (s)) is strictly increasing.
    A causal Lorentzian manifold is said to be globally hyperbolic (see [7]) if
for every pair of points p, q ∈ M there exists a compact subset K (p, q) of M
that contains the support all the causal curves from p to q.
    We recall also that, if f : M 7−→ IR is function of class C 2 , the Lorentzian

On the Finiteness of Light Rays Between a Source
and an Observer on Conformally Stationary
Space-Times

Fabio Giannoni,1 Antonio Masiello,2 and Paolo Piccione3
               Received May 4, 2000

               We use a convexity condition to prove the finiteness of the number of light rays
               joining a pointlike source with a pointlike observer in a stationary relativistic
               spacetime. The result is extended to the case of conformally stationary metrics under
               suitable boundedness assumptions on the conformal factor. We discuss our hypotheses
               in some explicit examples of standard static and stationary Lorentzian manifolds.

               KEY WORDS: Gravitational lensing



1. INTRODUCTION
The gravitational lensing phenomenon in Astrophysics is the effect by which an
observer detects more than one image of the same source, due to the deflection
of the light rays caused by the gravitational field. For a comprehensive exposi-
tion of theoretical and observational material on gravitational lenses we refer for
instance to [18]. It is well known as a theoretical possibility that the number of
observed images of the source can be either finite, or countable or even a whole
continuum of images, like in the case of the so called ‘Einstein rings’. Actually,
all the observed phenomena of Gravitational Lensing show only a finite number
of images or a continuum of images. This second case corresponds to the case
where the observer is in the caustic of the source (conjugate case). From this
point of view, one can ask whether there exists some topological or metrical

      This kind of analysis complements the mathematical description of the mul-
tiple quasar phenomenon by Ljusternik–Schnirelmann Theory done in [4], and by
Morse Theory done in [5] and [7]. In Reference [6] the authors have studied a sim-
ilar problem under the assumption that the Lorentzian metric is standard static,
by a reduction to a purely Riemannian problem. Here we consider the case of the
larger class of conformally stationary metrics (which are not necessarily in stan-
dard form) motivated by the fact that many physically interesting examples are of
this kind. We will assume that the source and the observer are pointlike and (M , g)
is a (conformally) stationary Lorentzian manifold, i.e. there is a smooth (confor-
mal) Killing timelike vector field Y on M . Moreover, we will assume that the time-
like curve g representing the source is an integral curve of Y, i.e. the light source
is at rest with respect to the distinguished observer field. The light rays emanat-
ing from the source towards the observer are represented in this model by past-
pointing lightlike geodesics between p and g; exchanging past and future, finite-
ness of observed images correspond to the finiteness of future-pointing lightlike
geodesics between the event p and the timelike curve g. This problem is somewhat
analogous to the corresponding problem in Riemannian geometry of the finiteness
of geodesics between two fixed points; there are classical results in this direction
involving assumptions on the curvature of the manifold [2, 9]. If a Riemannian
manifold has nonpositive sectional curvature, then between any two points there
exists one and only one geodesic; in [6] the authors prove that if the Riemannian
manifold admits a strictly convex function, the number of geodesics joining two
nonconjugate points is finite. In this paper we introduce the concept of light-con-
vex function, we give examples of static and stationary metrics that admit this kind
of function, and we prove that the existence of a light convex function implies the
finiteness of the future pointing light rays between p and g (Theorem 2.2).

       Definition 2.1. A function F : M @b R is said to be (strictly) light-convex if
for all (non constant) lightlike geodesic z : I ⊆ R @b M the composition F 8 z : I
@b R is (strictly) convex.
       It is easy to see that, if F is a light-convex function on M such that F 8 z
 is non constant for all non constant lightlike geodesic z in M , then, given any
 increasing and strictly convex function w : R @b R, the composition w 8 F is
 strictly light-convex in M . If F : M @b R is a map of class C 2 , then F is light
convex if and only if the Hessian H F is positive semidefinite on the light cone,
i.e., if H F (x)[v, v] ≥ 0 for all x ∈ M and all lightlike vector v ∈ T x M . If H F (x)
is strictly positive definite on the light cone, then F is strictly light convex. We
remark, however, that a light convex function need not be of class C 2 . In our
main results, no assumptions are made concerning the regularity of the convex
functions involved.

3. THE CONFORMALLY STATIONARY CASE
      In this Section we will extend the results of Section 2 to the more general
case of conformally stationary Lorentzian manifolds.
      Recall that a smooth map f : M @b M on a Lorentzian manifold (M , g)
is said to be conformal if its differential preserves the metric tensor g up to a
conformal factor. A conformal Killing vector field is a smooth vector field on M
whose flow x @b w(x, t) is a conformal map for all t. A conformally stationary
Lorentzian manifold is a Lorentzian manifold that admits a timelike conformal
Killing vector field. Alternatively, (M , gc ) is conformally stationary if and only
if there exists a stationary Lorentzian metric g on M and a smooth map f : M

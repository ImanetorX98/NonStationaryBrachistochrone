# Editorial notes — Abstract

Manuscript reviewed: `paper1-11.pdf` (38 pages, 3 August 2026 build).

## Current function

The abstract correctly identifies the problem, introduces the controlled-rail constraint, names the selector hierarchy, states the optimal-control framework, and identifies FLRW and Vaidya as the two applications. It also distinguishes Paper I from the companion rotating application. At roughly two hundred words, it is within IOP's usual 300-word ceiling.

## What should be preserved

- The opening question is immediate and physically intelligible.
- The stationary/non-stationary contrast gives the reader a reason for the paper.
- FLRW and Vaidya are named explicitly, so the abstract is discoverable.
- The true non-autonomous-flow validation is mentioned; this is one of the strongest credibility signals in the paper.
- The companion-paper boundary is disclosed rather than hidden.

## Editorial problems

1. The abstract carries too many concepts before the reader knows what a “rail” physically is: controlled rail, selector, Killing/CKV/Kodama hierarchy, Pontryagin, normality, Hamilton–Jacobi, closed special functions and radial mass flow all appear in one paragraph.
2. “We prove that this construction is a legitimate Pontryagin problem—with existence, normality, and a … verification criterion” can be read as a proof of global optimality. The body is more careful: the HJB statement is a sufficient criterion, and a global value function is not constructed for the generic non-stationary trajectories.
3. “Solve two … cases in closed form” is too broad. FLRW is closed directly; in Vaidya the manuscript derives frozen, adiabatic and special-function representations, while parts of the phenomenology are numerical/conditional. “Derive the extremal equations and a closed first-order representation” is safer.
4. The abstract does not state the most accessible physical result sharply enough. The reader should leave knowing what changes in Vaidya: the mass flow breaks the arrival/proper-time degeneracy and makes the trajectory depend on the arrival epoch.
5. “Conformal symmetry” in the title and selector hierarchy occupies substantial abstract space even though the detailed rotating conformal case is deferred to Paper II.

## Recommended direction

Use four moves: problem; controlled protocol; general mathematical result with its scope; FLRW/Vaidya findings and validation. Keep “global” out unless referring explicitly to the conditional HJB certificate. Replace “solve … in closed form” by a claim calibrated to the representations actually printed.

Suggested abstract text is included in the final report.

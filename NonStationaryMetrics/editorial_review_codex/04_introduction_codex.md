# Editorial notes — Introduction

## Strengths

The introduction is short, has a clean stationary-to-dynamical transition, and states a concrete protocol: fixed launch event, fixed spatial target, free arrival clock. The final paragraph gives a useful section map and makes the Paper-I/Paper-II split explicit.

## Major editorial issue: novelty positioning

The sentence claiming that existence, the replacement invariant and the effect of time dependence “have not been addressed” is too sweeping. Direct relativistic-brachistochrone literature is missing, including work by Giannoni, Piccione, Verderesi and Tausk on sub-Riemannian formulations, existence, travel-time Morse theory and arrival-time brachistochrones. General-relativistic Fermat principles also exist without stationarity, although they concern different admissible curves and often null/timelike geodesics rather than an actively maintained rail charge.

The defensible novelty is narrower and stronger:

- a controlled, fixed-selector invariant in a genuinely time-dependent timelike problem;
- the Killing → conformal-Killing → Kodama selection rule;
- a Pontryagin formulation with explicit free-arrival transversality;
- explicit FLRW/Vaidya application and first-order non-autonomous response.

State this contrast rather than claiming the whole non-stationary area is untouched.

## Accessibility

The introduction should explain in one plain sentence what the ideal rail does physically: it applies whatever proper acceleration is required to keep the chosen observer-measured energy fixed, while the objective minimises a selected clock rather than thrust or fuel. At present the operational meaning arrives later, after the reader has already accepted the central construction.

Add a compact “what is fixed / what is optimised / what is not claimed” paragraph. This will prevent repeated clarifications later:

- fixed: launch event, spatial target, rail charge and selector;
- free: arrival clock and control direction;
- optimised: selected elapsed clock;
- not optimised: work, thrust, comfort or geodesic motion;
- global optimality: conditional on an HJB certificate or comparison across extremals.

## Narrative recommendation

End the introduction with three contributions rather than a section-by-section inventory:

1. general controlled-rail/PMP construction;
2. FLRW and Vaidya physical consequences;
3. closed first-order representation plus true-flow residual test.

Then give the paper map in one sentence. This would align the opening with CQG's broad gravitational readership.

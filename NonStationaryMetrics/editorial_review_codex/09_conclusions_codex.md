# Editorial notes — Conclusions

## Strengths

The division into “Proved”, “Conditional and numerical”, and “Outlook” is excellent. It is unusually helpful in a technically ambitious paper and should be preserved. The conclusion also keeps the outgoing-Vaidya boundary-value problem open rather than presenting the formal negative-rate continuation as physical evaporation.

## Problems

The section is fragmented across pages 23–25 by figures 8–10. A conclusion should give the reader an uninterrupted hierarchy of results. As typeset, “Proved” appears under Figure 8, “Conditional and numerical” under Figure 9, and “Outlook” under Figure 10.

“Well-posed” and “minimizer” need the same qualifications used in section 2: compact timelike-selector domain, fixed protocol, and globality only with an HJB certificate or explicit comparison. The conclusion presently risks sounding stronger than the careful middle of the paper.

The last paragraph spends too much space on Paper II. End Paper I on its own Vaidya result and only then give one sentence about the rotating extension.

## Recommended structure

1. One paragraph: controlled protocol and exact general statements, with domain.
2. One paragraph: FLRW and Vaidya physical findings.
3. One paragraph: complete first-order correction, universal boundary source and `O(epsilon^2)` validation.
4. One paragraph: limitations—global HJB certificate, outgoing Vaidya, turning-point nonuniformity.
5. One sentence: companion rotating application.

Avoid repeating the full special-function taxonomy. The conclusion should say what the closed representation enables physically and computationally.

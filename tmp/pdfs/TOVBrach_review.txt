                          Coordinate-Time and Proper-Time Brachistochrones
                           in Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff Stellar Interiors
                                                        Iman Rosignoli1, ∗
                                   1
                                       Department of Physics, University of Pavia, Pavia, Italy

                 We formulate the relativistic brachistochrone problem inside static, spherically symmetric stellar
              interiors generated by the Tolman–Oppenheimer–Volkoff equations for a prescribed equation of state.
              The stellar geometry is determined by the pressure, energy-density, mass and lapse profiles obtained
              from the TOV system. For a massive test particle released from rest at the stellar surface, we derive
              two distinct variational problems: the minimization of the coordinate travel time t measured by an
              observer at infinity, and the minimization of the proper time τ measured along the particle worldline.
              Both problems reduce to Fermat-type variational principles on the spatial geometry of the static
              slice, with two different effective refractive indices. The cyclic angular coordinate yields generalized
              Snell laws and integral formulae for the opening angle, the turning radius and the travel times. A
              universal weak-field expansion in the compactness parameter µ = rs /R shows that the difference
              between the coordinate-time and proper-time turning radii, q (t) −q (τ ) , is determined at O(µ) for any
              equation of state by two one-dimensional quadratures and one derivative over the Newtonian density
              profile, without solving the full TOV system. In the constant-density limit this reduces to the closed
              form µ q0 (1 − q02 )2 /4. These results provide a framework for comparing relativistic brachistochrone
              curves in constant-density interiors, polytropic stars and realistic neutron-star equations of state.
